Soesokkak (쇠소깍) is where Hyodon Stream opens toward the shore. [Visit Jeju](https://www.visitjeju.net/kr/themtour/view?contentsid=CNTS_300000000012862) identifies both a walking path beside the water and a traditional **tewoo** raft experience. Decide how much time you want at the water before you make your other Seogwipo plans.

| Plan | What to settle before you go |
| --- | --- |
| A flexible shore walk | Save the estuary address, then check path signs and the conditions on arrival. Turn back where access is restricted. |
| A specific boat experience | Confirm which craft is being offered, the meeting point, fares, passenger rules and whether the trip is operating. |
| Outing with children or a tight return | Compare the last part of the journey, including any queue or check-in time. Keep enough time for the ride back. |

## Keep the walking option open

Save **제주특별자치도 서귀포시 쇠소깍로 104**. Follow local signs for the walking path and stay clear of restricted banks and water. If you extend the outing to the nearby Hahyo black-sand beach, check local signs and weather there as well. The estuary pin on this page helps locate the area. A ticketed boat can have a separate meeting point.

## Check the boat and the way home

Use the operator information shown for your specific activity to confirm boarding and availability. Weather, water conditions or staffing can affect departures. Save your return stop or prearranged pickup before you go, especially if a boat is only one stop in your day. If the boat is unavailable and the path is open, a shore visit can still work. If local closures affect the whole outing, try a separate [Seogwipo market stop](/blog/seogwipo-olle-market-takeaway-plan) or check [Cheonjiyeon’s opening and last admission](https://eticket.seogwipo.go.kr/contents?bmcode=cheonjiyeon).
