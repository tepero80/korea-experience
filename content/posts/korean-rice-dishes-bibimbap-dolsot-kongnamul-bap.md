Look at what the menu says about the **bowl, toppings and sauce**. A photo can help you identify the dish, but it cannot tell you what is in the sauce or whether egg and meat are optional.

## Read the Korean dish name

| Menu name | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| **비빔밥** (bibimbap) | Mixed rice with toppings. One [Jeonju example](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=231469) includes seasoned vegetables, beef and other additions. |
| **돌솥비빔밥** (dolsot bibimbap) | A [hot stone-pot version](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=178036). Check the toppings and handle the hot vessel with care. |
| **콩나물밥** (kongnamulbap) | [Bean-sprout rice](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=179077), often with a seasoning sauce. This is a rice dish. If you want soup with rice, look for **콩나물국밥**. |

## Decide how much sauce to add

If sauce is separate, taste a small amount before adding more. Ask **소스를 따로 주세요** (Please serve the sauce separately) if this matters to you. Ask what is in the broth or sauce instead of assuming a vegetable topping tells the whole story.

If you want the stone bowl, use the stand or tray and avoid touching the vessel even after you begin eating. If you want an easier bowl to share or eat quickly, ask which version comes in a regular bowl.
