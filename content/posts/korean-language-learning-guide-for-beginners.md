## Before a trip, practice with your actual route

Start with the first station, your accommodation and one place you want to visit. Save their exact Korean names from a map or booking confirmation. Add the station exit number and the accommodation’s Korean address. Keep one screenshot offline so you can show the address if a map search or mobile data fails. When names look similar, compare the entire name and the exit number before following a sign.

Hangul groups letters into syllable blocks. For example, 한 groups ㅎ, ㅏ and ㄴ. Practice recognizing the first block in a station name, then match the remaining blocks. Use the audio in the [National Institute of Korean Language entry for 출구](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=72138) to pair one useful written word with its sound. The entry for [환승역](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=89135) shows the term for a station where you can change lines.

| What you see | Where it helps |
| --- | --- |
| 출구 | Exit. Match the posted number to the number you saved. |
| 환승 | Transfer. Check the line number and destination as you follow signs. |
| 입구 | Entrance. Use it to spot where to go inside. |
| 화장실 | Restroom. Look for the word or the accompanying pictogram. |

## Rehearse one question and an easy backup

**화장실이 어디예요?** means “Where is the restroom?” For your real route, try **3번 출구가 어디예요?**, meaning “Where is Exit 3?” Have the Korean place or number visible on your phone. If the answer is too fast, **천천히 말씀해 주세요** means “Please speak slowly.” You can also point at the destination and ask **여기 맞아요?**, meaning “Is this the right place?”

Before you leave, look at a route screenshot and see whether you can pick out your station and exit. Then practice asking a partner one question and listening for one cue, such as a number, a direction or a repeat of the name. Keep your destination and address on the same screen as a backup you can show someone while keeping personal booking details out of view.

## If you want to study, choose a course for your level

The government’s [Study in Korea language directory](https://www.studyinkorea.go.kr/en/plan/examAndKoreanStudy.do) lists King Sejong Institute Foundation options under Korean language study, including Online King Sejong Institute (온라인 세종학당). Follow the directory to the current course list. If you are still learning to read syllable blocks, look for the introductory Korean course that teaches Hangul. If you can already sound out simple signs, compare the beginner course’s sample unit or placement guidance before registering.

Open the course listing and check the teaching language, whether the lessons are independent or live, the registration window and the time zone for any live session. Set a goal for the first week, such as reading the names on your route without romanization and introducing yourself in two or three sentences. When you are ready to practice with someone, the [language exchange guide](/blog/language-exchange-meetups-and-apps) can help you choose a format and prepare a short first conversation.
