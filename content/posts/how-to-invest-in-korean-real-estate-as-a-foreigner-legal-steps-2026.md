## Start with the exact unit and parcel

Write down the Korean road address, unit number and parcel number. Ask for a current registration certificate, called 등기사항증명서. [Article 19 of the Real Estate Registration Act](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1026156537) sets out how to request access to recorded details. Compare the registered owner with the proposed seller. If someone is signing for the owner, ask a Korean property lawyer to verify that person's authority. Have the lawyer explain registered rights and ask the seller separately about occupancy, lease terms and any tenant deposit the buyer would assume.

| Check | Bring these details |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Exact unit and parcel, current registration certificate, seller and any representative. |
| Zone | Property location and type, your nationality and buyer status, and the planned contract date. |
| Use | Who would live there, any current tenant, and whether you plan to rent the unit after purchase. |
| Funds | Your status under foreign-exchange rules, where each part of the purchase money comes from, and the deposit and balance dates. |

## Ask the district about permission and use

Give the office responsible for the property the exact details from the table. Ask whether its current designations cover this parcel and contract, whether you need a permit before signing, and whether the permit limits how soon you must move in or whether you may let the property. [Article 9](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1034962075) sets out prior permission for certain protected land categories. Separately, the [ministry's 2026 update](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148970419&pWise=main&pWiseMain=R4) announced an extension of specific foreign-buyer permit zones through August 25, 2027. Confirm which rule and dates apply with the property office before committing to a contract.

## Ask the bank how each part of the price can be paid

Speak to a bank that handles foreign exchange. Explain where the money is held now and whether any part would come from a Korean loan or a tenant deposit you would take over. The [Bank of Korea's nonresident purchase examples](https://www.bok.or.kr/portal/bbs/B0000267/list.do?menuNo=200796&searchCnd=1) describe cases that go to a foreign-exchange bank when the money comes from abroad, and cases that go to the Bank of Korea when funding includes locally raised funds. Ask the bank which filing or exception applies and what proof to keep. Start this check before sending money so you can align the filing with the proposed payment dates.

## Confirm what gets filed and by whom

Covered sales under [Article 3](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1028346425) have a 30-day reporting deadline from contract formation. The 60-day rule in [Article 8](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1033340101) applies to other qualifying foreign acquisition contracts. Ask the district what date starts the clock for your agreement, who submits the report and how you get a copy. Confirm title transfer and local tax filing separately. Before paying, have your adviser review the deposit terms, including what happens if a permit or funding is unavailable.

If the sale involves a tenant's large deposit, read the [guide to how Korean jeonse deposits work](/blog/korean-jeonse-housing-system-explained-for-foreigners-2026) and ask your adviser which obligations would transfer with this property.
