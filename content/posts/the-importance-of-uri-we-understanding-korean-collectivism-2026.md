## Listen for the noun or action

우리 (uri) can refer to a group that includes the speaker. In a question like **우리 같이 갈까요?**, a natural translation is “Shall we go together?” Said to you while making plans, it can include you. In **우리 먼저 갈게요**, the speaker may be telling you their own group will head out first. Use the situation to see who is going with whom.

Before a noun, 우리 can also point to a relationship or group that belongs with the speaker. The [National Institute of Korean Language’s answer on 우리](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=311736) discusses usage that excludes the listener. The question also gives family examples. In English, the wording depends on whose family or group is being discussed.

| Phrase | Possible English |
| --- | --- |
| **우리 가족** | My family or our family, depending on the speaker and listener. |
| **우리 아내** | My wife, when the speaker means their own spouse. |
| **우리 회사** | My company or our company, referring to the company where the speaker belongs or works. |

## Recognize 저희 in a polite introduction

The [Basic Korean Dictionary entry for 저희](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=71231) gives examples of speakers referring to their own group or someone close to them in a deferential way. You may hear **저희 회사** from a company representative or **저희 남편** from someone speaking about their husband. In both cases, consider the relationship the speaker is describing.

## Confirm who is included before you act

If you are arranging a meal or trip, ask what matters to the plan. **누가 같이 가요?** means “Who is going together?” For more examples of requests and introductions, see [polite Korean phrases for everyday travel](/blog/korean-honorifics-in-everyday-situations-practical-phrases-and-when-to-use-them).
