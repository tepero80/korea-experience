## Identify the experience the temple actually offers

Official Templestay describes a temple food day trip at Hwaeomsa with a temple visit and a food activity. Use it as a concrete example of a hosted session. Confirm the current date, number of visitors needed and what is included before organizing transport. A temple may also offer a separate cooking lesson with different arrangements.

| Ask the host | Confirm in writing |
| --- | --- |
| Date | Arrival time, end time and whether registration is complete. |
| Group | Minimum size, maximum size, and whether a solo visitor can join an existing session. |
| Language | The language your group will hear and what support is available. |
| Food | Whether the program includes cooking, a tasting or a full meal, and whom to ask about ingredients. |
| Arrival | Exact meeting point, late arrival policy, seating and a place for bags. |

## Build the day around the return trip

Save the temple’s confirmed meeting point and your last usable transport option. Leave room for walking between the stop and the temple. If the schedule is still tentative, keep lodging and onward rail flexible.

If the temple cannot confirm your group, consider a temple-food restaurant with a normal dining reservation instead. The Templestay Information Center lists Balwoo Gongyang in Seoul; check its current booking terms separately. For a visit that also includes temple routines or overnight accommodation, start with the [Templestay decision guide](/blog/korean-temple-stay-experience-complete-guide).
