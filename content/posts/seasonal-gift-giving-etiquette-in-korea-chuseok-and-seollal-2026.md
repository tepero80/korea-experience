If you are invited to a home for Seollal or Chuseok, ask the host what would be useful and whether a small gift is welcome. A gift is a gesture of thanks, and preferences vary between households; there is no need to guess an elaborate ritual.

## Choose something the household can use

| Before buying | Check with the host |
| --- | --- |
| Fruit or a packaged snack | Dietary preferences and when they plan to serve it. |
| Something from home | Whether it travels well and can enter Korea under current rules. |
| A gift at an already busy meal | Whether bringing a gift or food would actually help. |

[VISITKOREA’s Seollal guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=220321) lists fruit, traditional sweets and other packaged food among examples of holiday gifts. Consider a modest box of fruit, tea or sweets if suitable for the recipient. Ask about allergies, dietary restrictions and storage; avoid assuming everyone drinks alcohol or wants a large gift. A small item from your home region is another personal option.

Confirm the invitation time, address and any contribution to the meal. On arrival, follow your host’s lead and thank them for inviting you. [VISITKOREA’s Chuseok guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=219&vcontsId=1593350) notes that holiday opening hours vary, so buy ahead and check your transport. For practical closures and backup plans, use the [Korean holiday calendar](/blog/korean-holiday-calendar-navigating-closed-days).
