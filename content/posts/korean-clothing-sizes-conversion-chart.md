Start with measurements and the individual garment, not a fixed conversion between Korean and overseas sizes. A number or letter is a starting point; the fit depends on the brand, cut and item. If a shop allows it, try the item on and check its return conditions before paying.

## What the size information means

| Label or question | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| 가슴둘레 / 허리둘레 / 엉덩이둘레 | Chest, waist and hip circumference. Confirm whether the chart describes the body or the garment. |
| 어깨 / 소매 / 총장 | Shoulder, sleeve and total length. Ask how the seller measures the item. |
| 사이즈 안내 | Size guide. Look for the product-specific chart and its units. |
| Free size | One offered size, not a promise that it will fit everyone. Ask for measurements or try it. |

[Nike Korea’s unisex chart](https://www.nike.com/kr/size-fit/gender-neutral-clothing-mens-based-alpha) illustrates how to read a guide based on body circumferences. If the shop instead gives garment dimensions, compare them with a similar garment you already own. Use the chart for the item and retailer you are buying from.

## At the fitting room

Bring your measurements in centimeters, or measurements of a similar item that fits you. Compare like with like: a flat garment width is not necessarily the same as a body circumference. Ask the seller how each number was measured before comparing it with yours.

If a fitting room is available, try moving and sitting as you normally would. Check the shoulders, sleeve and overall length as well as the waist. For shoes, ask to try both shoes in the size available rather than treating a conversion chart as a fit guarantee.

Useful questions:

- **입어 봐도 될까요?** — May I try it on?
- **이 제품의 실측 사이즈가 어떻게 되나요?** — What are this item's actual measurements?
- **교환이나 환불이 가능한가요?** — Can it be exchanged or refunded?

Before purchase, ask for the shop's actual exchange conditions, deadline and receipt requirements. Do not assume the same policy applies to sale items or alterations. Keep the receipt and tags as instructed.
