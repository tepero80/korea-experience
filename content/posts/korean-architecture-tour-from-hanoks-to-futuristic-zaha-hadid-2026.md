## Pick two or three stops

| Stop | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Anguk | [Unhyeongung](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?menuSn=351&vcontsId=111253) is a former royal residence. Look at the low rooflines and the way gates frame the inner courts. Norakdang Hall is the subject of the photo above. |
| Hanok visit | [Namsangol Hanok Village](https://english.visitseoul.net/attractions/Yeouido-Hangang-Park_/277) near Chungmuro brings several historic houses together. Compare the different courtyard and living arrangements. Keep this stop optional if you prefer a slower visit. |
| DDP | [Dongdaemun Design Plaza](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=67162) makes a contrasting final stop. Check the specific hall or design store before crossing the campus. |

## Start near Anguk

VisitKorea lists Unhyeongung as free, with different summer and winter closing times and last admission 30 minutes before closing. It normally closes on Monday, or the next business day if Monday is a public holiday. [Read the Anguk entrance guide](/blog/unhyeongung-anguk-traditional-house-visit) for a separate arrival plan.

## Choose the afternoon according to access

For traditional buildings, open the [Namsangol visitor guide](/blog/namsangol-hanok-village-family-visit) and check the current opening schedule. If the courtyards are closed or the weather makes them impractical, head to a confirmed indoor stop at DDP. Part of the large campus can be outdoors even if the destination is indoors. The [DDP shop guide](/blog/shopping-for-local-designer-brands-in-dongdaemun-design-plaza-ddp-2026) separates the Design Store from Doota.

With luggage, limited time or difficult stairs, choose one heritage stop and one design stop. Check station elevators and building access independently rather than assuming the shortest map route is accessible.
