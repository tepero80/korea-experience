## Buy for the routine you will actually use

The [American Academy of Dermatology’s leave-in guide](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/hair-scalp-care/hair/leave-in-conditioner-tips) explains what a leave-in conditioner can do. In a Korean shop, **헹구어 주세요** asks you to rinse. A product marked **헹구지 않는** is described as one you leave on. Confirm the meaning in the full directions, especially if the package uses English marketing on the front.

| Use | What to choose |
| --- | --- |
| Quick hotel shower | One rinse-out product if your usual conditioner is easy to rinse and works for you. |
| Tangles after drying | Consider a leave-in that fits your hair and follow directions for amount and use. |
| Heat styling | Check the label for an explicit heat-protection instruction before relying on it. |
| Carry-on | Check container size and the rules for every airport security checkpoint on your route. |

## Try the simpler routine first

Shampoo and condition as you normally would, then check how your hair dries before adding a second finish product. If you use heat, use the **lowest heat setting** suitable for the tool and avoid styling the same section repeatedly. See [the dermatology guidance on damaging hair-care habits](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/hair-scalp-care/hair/habits-that-damage-hair). If you prefer a salon, bring a reference picture and use the [English salon booking guide](/blog/k-pop-hair-salon-booking-in-english-2026) to confirm the finished style, time and any additional charge.
