## Sketch a week for your child

Put the child’s age and current grade, the language they learn in, your planned address and the hours you need covered on one page. Start with one weekday. Mark who can handle the trip to school, pickup and the time before a parent gets home.

The [Seoul Metropolitan Government’s education guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/learning/education-for-foreign-residents/) explains that foreign children can generally attend Korean schools and directs families to their local office of education for entrance qualifications. Ask the office which school to contact for your address. Ask each school which grade, records and document formats it will accept.

| Need | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Class | Which grade can my child enter? What records, translations and language support apply? |
| Hours | When do class and any later program end? Who may pick up my child, and how are changes handled? |
| Care | Are there places on the days we need? What are the hours, fees and rules for our child? |
| Meals | What is provided? How should I communicate an allergy or other dietary need? |

## Check care for the hours the school leaves open

Ask whether a program is part of the school day or requires its own application. If a family considers private lessons, compare the travel and time burden with the child’s interests and room for rest. Agree on a realistic pickup and backup with the adults involved.

In Seoul, the [city’s specialized childcare announcement](https://english.seoul.go.kr/smg-expands-emergency-specialized-childcare-centers-to-meet-urgent-childcare-needs/) describes hourly and year-round care at participating centers and gives a reservation starting point. Ask the relevant center about age, eligibility, documents, availability and the current charge for your child before relying on it.

## Confirm the first week in writing

Save the school or center’s contact, the first day, the actual drop-off and pickup address and the name of the adult authorized to collect your child. Ask how you will be told about closures or schedule changes. Once the routine is clear, plan a [family museum visit or theme-park day](/blog/family-friendly-attractions-theme-parks-and-museums) around the time and energy you have.
