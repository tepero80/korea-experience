## Choose by the part of Seoul you are in

| Area | A place to check |
| --- | --- |
| Jongno | [Yeongchunok](https://english.visitseoul.net/area/Yeongchunok/ENP012404) serves gomtang and other soups near Jongno 3-ga. Visit Seoul lists it as 24 hours, with Seollal and Chuseok closures. Call [02-765-4237](tel:+8227654237) for the kitchen. |
| Seongsu | [Somunnan Seongsu Gamjatang](https://english.visitseoul.net/restaurants/SeongsuGamjatang/ENPlceoxk) serves pork-bone soup on Yeonmujang-gil. Visit Seoul lists it as 24 hours and asks that the full party be present for seating. Call [02-465-6580](tel:+8224656580). |
| South Seoul | [Saebyeokjip](https://english.visitseoul.net/restaurants/Saebyeokjip/ENP005433) is a separate option in Cheongdam. Visit Seoul lists 24 hours with Seollal and Chuseok closures. Call [02-546-5739](tel:+8225465739) before making a detour. |

## Confirm the kitchen before you leave

Look at the latest notice for that location and ask **지금 주문할 수 있나요?** (Can I order now?) or **마지막 주문은 몇 시인가요?** (What time is last order?). A restaurant can list hours that differ from its kitchen. For example, [VisitKorea’s Dongdaemun Bonga listing](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=60481) shows 24-hour operating hours but a 22:00 last order.

Save the Korean address below. Check your return route while you still have a choice, especially if you are relying on the final train. When the kitchen is closed or the wait is too long, look for a confirmed option near your accommodation before crossing town.

## Check which soup you are ordering

[Seolleongtang](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=178349) is ox-bone soup. The Seongsu listing serves gamjatang, a pork-bone soup. If you avoid pork, beef or particular ingredients, ask about the broth and preparation before ordering. Open the [Korean dining phrases](/blog/how-to-order-food-in-korean-restaurants-phrases-guide-2026) if you need to show a question rather than say it aloud.
