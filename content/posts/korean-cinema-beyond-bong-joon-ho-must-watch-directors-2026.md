For a first step beyond contemporary hits, use the Korean Film Archive (KOFA) to find a film, then check whether a screening or authorised stream is actually available. Historical programme pages identify films; their dates do not mean those films are showing now.

The archive's film pages can contain detailed plot descriptions, so skip the synopsis if you prefer to avoid spoilers. KOFA's selected-films publication is a starting point for discovery, not a guarantee that every title is screening or streaming.

## Check the screening, not just the film page

KOFA's [Cinematheque information](https://eng.koreafilm.or.kr/pages/PC_00000102) explains online and in-person ticket procedures. Its [Korean screening calendar](https://www.koreafilm.or.kr/cinematheque/calendar/programs) identifies an **E** symbol for English subtitles. Confirm the symbol and specific screening details: subtitles on one showing do not establish subtitles on another. Programme dates, ticket release and accessibility can change, and special events may have separate arrangements.

For viewing elsewhere, KOFA describes its [Korean Classic Film channel and online archive](https://eng.koreafilm.or.kr/kofa/news/BC_0000056614). Only some works have English subtitles, and availability can vary by territory. Check the actual video and caption menu rather than assuming a title is accessible from home.

If your goal is choosing a venue rather than choosing a director, compare our [Seoul independent cinema guide](/blog/independent-cinema-guide-art-house-theaters-in-seoul). Check the venue's latest programme before travelling; a cinema listing does not guarantee an English-subtitled show that day.
