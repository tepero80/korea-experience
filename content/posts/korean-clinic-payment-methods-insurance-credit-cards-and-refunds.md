Before an appointment, request a written estimate and ask the clinic which payment methods it accepts, whether a deposit is required, and its cancellation and refund terms. Contact your insurer to confirm coverage, exclusions, any prior authorization and whether it will pay the provider directly. Keep enough funds available for charges you may have to pay yourself.

## Questions to settle

| Topic | Ask the clinic or insurer |
| --- | --- |
| Estimate | Which consultation, tests, medication and document fees are included? What could change the total? |
| Payment | Is your particular international card accepted? When is payment due, and in which currency? |
| Insurance | Is prior authorization required? Is direct billing available for this provider and service, or do you pay and claim later? |
| Documents | Can the clinic issue an itemized bill and receipt in the language your insurer requires? Are there fees or waiting times? |
| Cancellation | What deposit is refundable, under what conditions, and how do you request a refund? |

As one provider example, [Seoul National University Hospital’s payment page](https://snuh.org/global/en/patients/EN02002004.do) states that payment can be made in Korean won by cash or credit card and that patients remain responsible for outstanding charges if an insurer does not pay. Its [international patient guide](https://www.snuh.org/global/en/patients/EN02003001.do) asks patients to contact their insurer for a guarantee of payment before the appointment and lists English and itemized receipts. These are that hospital’s procedures, not a promise that every clinic offers the same services or accepts every card.

Before leaving, request the documents your insurer specifies and compare the bill with the estimate. Ask the provider to explain discrepancies and its refund procedure; preserve the written terms and receipts.
