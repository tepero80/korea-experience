## Pick up the conversation you already started

Think back to what you both enjoyed. Someone you talked to about a walking route might enjoy another group walk. Someone who wanted to practice speaking might prefer to meet at the next exchange. Choose a specific follow-up that fits what they actually said.

If you only met briefly, return to the next public session and say hello there. If the person agreed to keep in touch, send a short message through the channel you agreed on. Use the group thread for practical questions about the next session and ask before sharing anyone’s contact details.

## Send an invitation that is easy to answer

Mention where you met, suggest one activity and give a proposed time. For example: “I enjoyed talking about street photography at the walk. Would you like to join next Sunday’s public photo walk? No worries if you’re busy.”

Here are three short sample messages. The National Institute of Korean Language shows how to use [다음 for a later meeting](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=64531&nation=eng&nationCode=6) and [같이 to suggest doing something together](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=26799).

| Moment | What you could say |
| --- | --- |
| Say goodbye | **다음에 또 봐요.** See you again next time. |
| Invite them | **다음 모임에 같이 갈래요?** Would you like to go to the next meetup together? Add the event name and date. |
| If they decline | **알겠어요. 다음에 봐요.** Got it. See you next time. |

## Turn a yes into a plan

Confirm a public meeting point and when to be there. For a group event, share the original event page so you can each check the time or RSVP. If they suggest a different date, settle on one that works for both. If they decline or leave the message unanswered, give them space and keep doing the activity you enjoy.

If you want a shared activity that has a clear purpose, try the [language exchange guide](/blog/language-exchange-meetups-and-apps). If you prefer to meet people around a different interest, [check how to find and evaluate a local online group](/blog/expat-communities-facebook-groups-and-forums).
