## Confirm who will speak with you

The [UK Foreign Office’s South Korea guide](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/south-korea/health) notes that staff language varies and links to a list of providers. A listing gives you a place to start. Ask the provider who can communicate in English during your actual consultation and what happens if that person is unavailable.

| Check | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Location | Which branch, floor and reception desk should I use? |
| Language | Can I speak in English with the doctor, or is there an interpreter? |
| Timing | Is that person available for my department and appointment time? |
| Cost | Is interpretation included? Can you send any separate charge in writing? |

Show **이 시간에 영어로 진료받을 수 있나요?** (“Can I have the consultation in English at this time?”). If you are speaking to an interpreter, ask how to get your visit instructions in a form you can understand later.

## Save a contact and a fallback

Seoul National University Hospital [publishes contacts by language](https://www.snuh.org/global/en/patients/EN02004.do). This is an example of a hospital contact route. Confirm whether it serves your department and date. If the clinic you intended to visit cannot confirm language support, contact a second provider or ask your insurer what options it can arrange. Use the [emergency numbers and response guide](/blog/medical-emergencies-1330-vs-119-2026) when urgent help is needed. Once you choose a provider, use the [appointment or walk-in checklist](/blog/booking-medical-consultations-in-korea-online-vs-walk-in).
