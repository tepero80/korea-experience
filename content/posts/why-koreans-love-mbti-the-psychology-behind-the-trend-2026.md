## Understand the question

If you hear **MBTI가 뭐예요?**, the person is asking what your MBTI type is. A four-letter answer such as **INFP** refers to a combination of preferences in the model. Someone telling you their type could be thinking of the official assessment or an online quiz.

A [2024 study of South Korean media coverage](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART003067083) examined 231 news reports about MBTI and described its use in entertainment and social interactions. That tells you how it appeared in the coverage studied. How often an individual will bring it up today depends on the setting.

## Answer with as much as you want to share

| If you want to | Try saying |
| --- | --- |
| Share your type | **저는 INFP예요.** I’m INFP. |
| Say you’re new to it | **아직 안 해 봤어요.** I haven’t tried it yet. |
| Ask what it means | **그게 무슨 뜻이에요?** What does that mean? |

If the conversation gets stuck on letters, ask about something concrete. For example, if somebody says their type explains how they travel, ask which part of a trip they like to plan ahead.

## Treat a type as one topic in a conversation

The [Myers & Briggs Foundation](https://www.myersbriggs.org/unique-features-of-myers-briggs/is-the-mbti-a-test/) describes the official assessment as a self-report about preferences and distinguishes it from tests of abilities. When you are making plans with someone, ask about the preference that affects the plan. Offer a choice between a large gathering and a small group and let them tell you what they want.
