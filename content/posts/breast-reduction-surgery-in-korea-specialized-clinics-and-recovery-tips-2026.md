## Ask for a visit with the surgeon

Tell the clinic what you want to discuss, including symptoms that affect daily life or goals you want to weigh. Ask who would operate, whether you can meet that surgeon first and what reports or images they want to review. If you share records ahead of time, ask for the clinic’s secure channel. The [UK NHS guide to preparing for surgery](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/having-surgery/preparation/) suggests gathering your health history, previous test results and current medicines. Ask the Korean team which information it needs about prior procedures, anesthesia or allergies.

## Get answers before booking

| When | What to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| Consult | Ask the surgeon what they propose, why, what alternatives you have and which risks apply to you. |
| Quote | Ask which charges include the assessment, anesthesia, interpretation, care after surgery and extra visits. |
| Stay | Ask which checks require you to remain nearby, what could change the dates and whom to call after hours. |
| Home | Ask who will take over care and how they can receive the summary, procedure record and any relevant results. |

The [UK NHS breast reduction guide](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/cosmetic-procedures/cosmetic-surgery/breast-reduction-female/) is a starting point for questions to bring to your surgeon. Ask how the proposed procedure might affect scarring, sensation or breastfeeding if those issues matter to you. Ask how the team deals with concerns after you leave. Confirm your own arrangements with the Korean team, including who can interpret the consent discussion and follow-up.

## Agree how records and care will be handed over

Before fixing your return, check which visits need to happen in Korea and ask the treating clinician what would make you delay travel. Confirm that your usual clinician can review the planned handoff. Ask for the summary, relevant test results and a named contact for questions. [Seoul National University Hospital lists](https://www.snuh.org/global/en/patients/EN02005.do) surgery records and discharge summaries among the documents it issues. Ask your provider which documents it can supply, when they will be ready and whether it can send them securely. Use the [guide to choosing Korean medical records and English copies](/blog/korean-medical-records-getting-english-translations) to discuss what the receiving clinician needs.
