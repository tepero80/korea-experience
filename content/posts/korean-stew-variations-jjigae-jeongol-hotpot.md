Look for **전골** if you want a hot pot to share. A **찌개** can be an individual meal or a larger dish depending on the restaurant. Ask about the serving format and total before the food arrives, especially when a menu lists a price by portion.

## Make a group order in three decisions

| Decision | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Pot or bowl | Ask how many people the smallest pot serves. If the price says 1인분, check whether the kitchen requires a minimum order. |
| Broth and fillings | [Red chili brisket jjigae](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=181083) has beef and chili paste. A [soybean-paste version](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=231405) can contain freshwater snails. The [sundae jeongol example](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=231609) contains pork intestines, and its stock can use beef or pork bones. |
| Extras | Ask whether 공깃밥 (rice), 사리 (add-ons such as noodles), or 볶음밥 (fried rice after the meal) are included, optional or charged separately. |

Show **이 전골은 몇 명이 먹을 수 있어요?** to ask how many the pot serves. Ask staff whether to let it simmer and when it is ready to eat. If anyone cannot eat an ingredient or use a shared pot, discuss that before choosing. A vegetable or mushroom in the name does not establish the stock or rule out other ingredients.

If you only want a snack or a second stop, use the [guide to ordering shared Korean drinking snacks](/blog/korean-drinking-snacks-anju-pairing-guide).
