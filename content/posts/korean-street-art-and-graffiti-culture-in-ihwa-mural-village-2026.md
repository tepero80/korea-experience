Ihwa is a residential neighbourhood as well as a place to see public art. Plan a flexible walk, keep voices low and make room for residents. Murals can change or disappear, so an old photograph is not a promise that a particular piece is still there.

| Before you go | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Starting point | [Visit Seoul](https://english.visitseoul.net/area/IhwaMuralVillage/ENPz90c1l) lists 70-11 Ihwajang-gil, Jongno-gu, and Hyehwa Station Exit 2 as an approach. |
| Terrain | The official description mentions stairs and a climb; check a current map and consider an alternative if steps or steep slopes are difficult. |
| Photography | Photograph public art without entering property or intruding on residents; do not frame private interiors through windows or gates. |
| If a mural is gone | Continue to public paths or nearby Naksan Park instead of searching around homes for the same image. |

## What the neighbourhood is

Visit Seoul connects the area's mural village to the Naksan Project and describes murals along its alleys below the fortress. The official listing gives free admission; individual cafés, workshops and other businesses set their own terms. It does not provide a verified inventory of every artwork currently visible.

The importance of privacy is grounded in local history. A [Jongno District Council record from 2015](https://bookcouncil.jongno.go.kr/record/recordView.do?key=28a577c6748c0a436e72ad30f7366f10718264ba4f20a2eb5f59f8d7d1328f34f1bfc18372af6ef3) recorded residents' concerns about noise, litter and intrusion near murals. That is historical evidence of the issue, not a claim that today's residents share one view or that the same artworks remain.

## Walk gently and stay flexible

Keep a conversation at a low volume, do not lean on doors or walls, and give way on narrow paths. Take rubbish away or use a designated bin. Ask before including an identifiable person in a close-up photograph, and avoid tripods or large groups that block access. Respect any posted restrictions even if an older guide suggests otherwise.

Leave extra time for the hillside and check conditions after rain or in winter. If a step-free route is essential, ask the [Seoul Danurim accessible tourism service](https://www.seouldanurim.net/en) for current route information rather than assuming the alleys are accessible. For a nearby indoor history stop, see [Seoul City Wall Museum](/blog/seoul-city-wall-museum-guide), then verify a specific site's current access before visiting.
