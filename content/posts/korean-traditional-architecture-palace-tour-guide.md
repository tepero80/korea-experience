At **경복궁 · 서울 종로구 사직로 161**, follow the main public approach from Gwanghwamun toward the throne hall. Look through each gate before walking on. The view and the size of the space change as you move from the entrance to the royal courtyard.

## Read the name and look for a physical clue

| Label | A useful observation |
| --- | --- |
| 문 (mun) | A gate. Notice what part of the complex opens beyond it and what can be seen from the approach. |
| 전 (jeon) | A comparatively formal hall. At Geunjeongjeon, look from the courtyard at the elevated platform and the approach to the front steps. |
| 당 (dang) | Often a smaller or more private building. Use the site's sign for its particular role. |
| 정 (jeong) | A pavilion. At Hyangwonjeong, compare the waterside setting with the throne hall’s paved approach. Use the open viewing paths. |

The [palace authority’s architectural guide](https://royal.khs.go.kr/ROYAL/contents/R311050000.do) groups several buildings by the last part of their names. Its [Gyeongbokgung history](https://royal.khs.go.kr/ENG/contents/E101010000.do) describes later damage and restoration, including work around Hyangwonjeong. Read restoration notices when they are available, and use the present buildings to understand the layout.

Check [address, admission and Tuesday closure information](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?vcontsId=87740) before setting out. Avoid making a second palace fit into the same afternoon if you want time for observation. The [Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung comparison](/blog/korean-palaces-guide-gyeongbokgung-to-changdeokgung-2026) explains which visit to choose and where the Secret Garden’s admission differs.
