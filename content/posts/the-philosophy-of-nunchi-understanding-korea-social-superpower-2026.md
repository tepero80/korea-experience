## Check with the people at your table

The [National Institute of Korean Language dictionary](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=41644) defines **눈치** (*nunchi*) in terms of sensing what other people mean or how a situation is going. For a visitor at a group meal, you can notice uncertainty and make it easier for someone to answer. Ask what people want to share, whether anyone needs an individual dish and how hungry everyone is before placing the order.

| When | A question you can show the staff |
| --- | --- |
| You aren’t sure whether one dish is for sharing | **이 음식은 같이 나눠 먹나요?** (“Do we share this dish?”) |
| You want another portion or a side | **추가하면 얼마예요?** (“How much for an additional portion?”) |
| Your group wants to pay separately | **각자 계산할 수 있나요?** (“Can we pay separately?”) |

## Confirm the order before it goes through

Look at the menu or the tablet and confirm how many portions you have ordered. [VisitKorea’s restaurant guidance](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/infoBscView.do?menuSn=460&vcontsId=140727) describes water, side dishes and cases where some items are self-service. Use the posted instructions or ask staff about extras at your table.

Before sharing, tell the others which ingredients you need to avoid. [VisitKorea’s vegetarian guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=476&vcontsId=140732) gives examples of why an apparently suitable dish may require an ingredient check. Ask about the specific broth, filling or dressing. If you cannot verify what you need to know, choose a dish or venue where staff can confirm it.

## Agree how to pay before people leave

Check whether staff can split the payment and whether the restaurant needs the group to pay at the counter. If one person pays, agree how the others will settle up. Use the [restaurant ordering guide](/blog/how-to-order-food-in-korean-restaurants-phrases-guide-2026) for other menu, table and counter phrases.

**Editorial check.** The dictionary explains the word. The table is practical visitor advice, and the venue decides how ordering and payment work.
