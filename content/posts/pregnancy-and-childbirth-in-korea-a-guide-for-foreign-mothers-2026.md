## Ask which service can take your request

Start with the hospital’s published international patient contact. [Asan Medical Center’s service information](https://eng.amc.seoul.kr/gb/lang/patients/contents/ourService.do) is one example of appointment and language assistance. Ask the center to put you in touch with the relevant maternity team and confirm what it offers for your dates. Mention whether you need one consultation, ongoing prenatal visits or delivery planning.

| Ask about | What to get in writing |
| --- | --- |
| The visit | Which department can take the request, whether it needs a referral and whether a date is confirmed. |
| Records | Which prenatal reports, scans and medication information to bring or send, what language is accepted and where to send it securely. |
| Language | When interpreting can be arranged and how to request it if you need unscheduled care. |
| When plans change | Which phone or service to contact when the clinic is closed, and whether to go to a different site. |

If your visit is for a fertility consultation, use the [overseas fertility inquiry guide](/blog/fertility-treatments-ivf-in-korea-success-rates-and-international-patient-care-2026) to find out what the clinic can review before travel.

## Prepare the handoff before travel

The [CDC Yellow Book](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/family-travel/pregnant-travelers.html) recommends carrying prenatal records and the contact details of your treating clinician. Ask your current care team what information should accompany you, and keep a copy you can open offline. Have the receiving service confirm its location, booking reference and instructions. The [Korea clinic appointment guide](/blog/booking-medical-consultations-in-korea-online-vs-walk-in) explains how to distinguish a general inquiry from a confirmed booking. For urgent care, use the [local emergency contacts and handoff guide](/blog/dealing-with-complications-who-to-contact-in-emergencies).
