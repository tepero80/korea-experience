## Read the back of the pack

Start with [KTO’s vegetarian travel tips](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=476&vcontsId=140732). It recommends looking for a certified vegan logo. Read the Korean list marked 원재료명 (ingredients) as well. A translation app can help you identify an unfamiliar word, but do not use it as your only check if one ingredient determines whether you can eat a product.

| Check | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Logo | Check whether a vegan logo names or identifies its certifier. A front label saying vegetable or plant-based gives you a starting point. |
| Sauce | Read the sachet as well as the noodles or rice. Look for 멸치 (anchovy), 어묵 (fish cake) or 새우 (shrimp) in seasoning. |
| Dairy | Check 우유 (milk), 유청 (whey) and 계란 (egg) against your own diet. Requirements differ for people who eat eggs or dairy. |
| Label | If allergies matter, check the allergen statement separately. Choose an alternative if you cannot establish that the product meets your needs. |

## Decide what to buy if the wording is unclear

If you cannot verify a prepared item, look for plain fruit or a single-ingredient alternative you can check. At the counter you can show “원재료명을 어디에서 확인할 수 있나요?” to ask where you can check the ingredients. Staff may be able to point to the label. Retain the package so you can look up a term again.
