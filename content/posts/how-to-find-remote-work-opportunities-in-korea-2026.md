## Search from the place you plan to work

On employer career pages and job boards, search your role plus “remote South Korea” or “remote APAC.” For postings in Korean, try **재택근무** for work from home and look for **근무지**, the work location. Save the employer’s own posting where you can. Check the permitted countries, expected time-zone overlap and any days you must attend in person.

| Setup | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Foreign job | Ask your employer to confirm in writing that it allows work from Korea for your planned dates. Check time zones, duties and travel to the office. |
| Korean hire | Ask which company signs and pays under the contract, where it expects you to work, and who handles any immigration paperwork. |
| Client work | List the clients, their countries, how you will be paid and where you will perform the work. Confirm whether your status permits the full arrangement. |

## Confirm permission for the actual work

The [Korean consulate’s F-1-D overview](https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/us-atlanta-en/brd/m_4849/view.do?seq=18) describes a route for eligible people with an overseas job or business that can be carried out remotely. The [2026 Korea Immigration Service update](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/immigration/214/608294/artclView.do) says the Workcation visa became a formal program on June 30, 2026, with income rules that vary by age and area and a maximum stay of three years. Ask the Korean mission responsible for your application which current conditions and documents apply to you.

[Article 20 of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1035220467) addresses prior permission for activities covered by a different status alongside your current one. Call the [Korea Immigration Service’s 1345 contact center](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/1862/subview.do) with your status, employer or clients, duties, payment and work location. Ask what approval, status change or reporting applies before you begin. Check Korean and cross-border tax questions separately with a qualified adviser.

## Check what the offer commits you to

Keep a copy of the written offer with the contract party, hours, pay currency, work location and start date. Ask who will confirm immigration steps and by when. If you are being hired for work in Korea, use the [pay and employer records checklist](/blog/korean-labor-laws-your-rights-as-a-foreign-employee-2026) to keep the details you may need to check later.
