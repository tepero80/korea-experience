The name of a Korean dish does not establish whether it meets your gluten-free needs. Recipes, seasonings and shared preparation vary. If you have celiac disease, use your clinician’s guidance and ask the kitchen about the exact meal; if staff cannot confirm, use a backup you have already checked. This guide does not certify a dish, product or restaurant as safe.

## Check ingredients in the whole meal

[Coeliac UK](https://www.coeliac.org.uk/grains/) identifies wheat, barley and rye as gluten-containing grains. Ask about every part of the meal, including sauce, broth, marinade, coating, seasoning and side dishes. Do not assume that a rice-based dish, plain-looking broth or unmarinated ingredient is suitable without checking everything added to it. A “wheat-free” answer alone does not address barley, rye or shared preparation.

## Ask how the food is prepared

The [Celiac Disease Foundation](https://celiac.org/dining-and-social-eating/) recommends asking about cross-contact and shared equipment. Ask the kitchen whether the dish shares a grill, fryer, pan, cooking water, utensils or work surface with gluten-containing food, and whether it can be prepared separately. If staff cannot answer or cannot meet your needs, thank them and choose a backup rather than treating a menu label as proof.

## Check packaged food separately

For a packaged product, examine the actual package each time. Korea’s [Ministry of Food and Drug Safety](https://www.mfds.go.kr/eng/wpge/m_14/denofile.do) lists wheat among allergens covered by its food-labeling system and describes warnings for potential allergen mixing in some manufacturing situations. A wheat declaration is useful, but it is not a complete check for every gluten source or cross-contact concern, and a packaged-food label cannot establish restaurant preparation.

## Prepare before you travel

[Coeliac UK’s travel guidance](https://www.coeliac.org.uk/living-with-coeliac-disease/food-and-drink/eating-out-and-travel/travelling-with-coeliac-disease/) recommends advance research and confirming arrangements with food providers. Carry a written explanation reviewed by a fluent Korean speaker familiar with celiac disease; include the ingredients and cross-contact conditions relevant to you. Keep a verified backup or appropriate snack for delays. Recheck ingredients and preparation on arrival because suppliers, staff and recipes can change.

## If you need urgent help

For immediate danger, call 119 for fire or ambulance. [Korea Tourism Organization emergency guidance](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=177&vcontsId=248145) says callers can request an interpreter. The [1330 travel helpline](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/infoHtmlView.do?menuSn=454&vcontsId=140632) provides tourism information and interpretation; it is not an emergency medical service.
