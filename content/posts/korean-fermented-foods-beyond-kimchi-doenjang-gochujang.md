Choose the role you want on the table. A small bowl of paste can be a seasoning; a salted seafood side can include ingredients you do not see. A shared drink can contain alcohol.

## Read the menu or label

| Name | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| 된장 | Doenjang is fermented soybean paste. Check the ingredients of the exact product. |
| 고추장 | Gochujang is red chili paste. Check ingredients and heat before adding it. |
| 젓갈 | Jeotgal is salted fish or other seafood. It can appear as a side dish or in seasoning. |
| 장아찌 | Jangajji is a pickled vegetable side. Ask about its brine or sauce. |
| 막걸리 | Makgeolli is rice wine. Confirm alcohol content or order a different drink. |

[VisitKorea’s jang guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=221365) explains the pastes. The Korean Food Promotion Institute distinguishes [jeotgal in a dish](https://www.hansik.or.kr/magazines/list/magazineDetail/68/3503?menuSn=429) and [jangajji as pickled vegetables](https://www.hansik.or.kr/magazines/list/magazineDetail/225/3952?menuSn=429). [VisitKorea’s regional guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/public/internal/ShoppingSpecial/files/musthaveitems_ebook_english.pdf) identifies makgeolli as rice wine.

## Choose what to ask

Ask whether the sauce includes seafood such as shrimp or anchovy, even when the visible side is vegetables. If you are buying a jar, check its label with the [packaged-food allergy guide](/blog/korean-packaged-food-allergy-labels). If an ingredient matters for your health or diet and the kitchen cannot confirm it, choose something you can verify.

For a bowl of soybean stew rather than small sides, use the [cheonggukjang and meju guide](/blog/korean-fermented-foods-beyond-kimchi-cheonggukjang-and-meju-2026) to distinguish the names.
