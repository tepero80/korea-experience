## Put the two offers on the same timeline

[Seoul’s rental overview](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/living/housing/1-wolse-jeonse/) describes jeonse as putting down a large deposit and wolse as combining a deposit with a monthly charge. For an offer between the two, write down the actual deposit and rent. Use the proposed length of stay when comparing two quotations.

| Check | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | List deposit, first rent, brokerage and other charges due before receiving the keys. Keep any potentially refundable deposit separate. |
| Monthly | Add rent, building fees, utilities and any financing costs you would need during the expected term. |
| Exit | Ask when the deposit is expected back, which notice you must give and what costs the written terms attach to an early departure. |
| Options | Compare two written offers with your cash available. If a deposit is large, review the [HUG guarantee overview](https://www.khug.or.kr/hug/web/en/02/en02000002.jsp) and ask a qualified housing counselor about the specific lease. |

## Make a choice that fits the length of your stay

A lower monthly amount can tie up more money at signing. If you are only staying briefly, get a separate quote for a furnished stay with the same dates. Compare inclusions and the change policy before signing. Seoul’s overview also links to housing assistance resources for foreign residents.

See the [jeonse deposit protection checklist](/blog/korean-jeonse-housing-system-explained-for-foreigners-2026) before choosing the high-deposit option and transferring money.
