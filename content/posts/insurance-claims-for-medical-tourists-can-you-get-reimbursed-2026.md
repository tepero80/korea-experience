## Ask your insurer what counts as proof

The [UK Foreign Office guidance for medical care abroad](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/in-hospital-abroad) advises contacting your insurer promptly and keeping receipts and clinical notes. Ask your insurer which contact channel to use, the deadline and whether it can arrange payment directly. If you already paid, ask how to submit the expenses. Keep the case number with the files.

| Where | What to request or save |
| --- | --- |
| Insurer | Claim number, submission deadline, required documents and language rules. |
| Cashier | An itemized bill and proof of payment. Save both if they are issued separately. |
| Clinician | The certificate or clinical notes your insurer requests. Ask while the visit is still open. |
| Pharmacy | A readable receipt and a prescription copy when relevant to the claim. |

Still comparing cover before paying? The [planned-treatment insurance checklist](/blog/medical-tourism-insurance-for-korea) helps you identify coverage questions before you need to collect a claim file.

## Sort the paperwork before departure

Seoul National University Hospital’s [records instructions](https://www.snuh.org/global/en/patients/EN02005.do) direct patients to request a medical certificate during the consultation. Ask your own provider about pickup, format, fees and remote access before leaving Korea. For copies, translation, or when another person will collect the paperwork, use the [Korean medical records guide](/blog/medical-records-for-international-patients-in-korea-translation-consent-and-copi).

Check the name and visit date on each document. If the insurer wants an itemized bill that you did not get, ask the provider’s billing desk how to request it. Use the insurer’s designated upload channel for personal medical documents.
