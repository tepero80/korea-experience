## Check where the money is now

Start with the account or balance you can actually use to pay. Sending money **to** a Korean account and funding a transfer **from** a Korean account are separate questions. [Wise’s KRW transfer guide](https://wise.com/help/articles/2932331/guide-to-krw-transfers) explains its current restrictions on using an external payment method for KRW and how an existing Wise balance can be used.

| Funds | What to check first |
| --- | --- |
| Korean bank | Ask the bank or an eligible remittance provider if it can send from your account. Wise’s KRW guide says you cannot fund or top up in KRW from an external bank account. |
| Existing Wise balance | Check the available balance and which destination and payout method Wise offers. Its guide describes sending from KRW already held in Wise, including KRW obtained by converting another Wise balance. |
| Other account | Use your actual funding currency in each quote. Confirm you own the funding account and can use the available payment method. |

## Compare offers you can actually use

Enter the same payout country, recipient currency and method. Set the same **total amount leaving your account** when you can. Write down what the recipient would receive, the estimated arrival, and whether a separate recipient or intermediary fee could change the amount. Compare the results before authorizing payment. If you must first move or convert your funds, count that step and its fee too.

Ask what happens if the recipient information is rejected. The [Seoul Metropolitan Government banking guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/living/convenience/1-banking/) lists examples of recipient and bank details to prepare. The sending service should confirm its exact requirements for your route.

If your only funds are held in a Korean bank, use the [Korean-bank remittance checklist and quote comparison](/blog/sending-money-abroad-from-korea-best-methods-2026) to choose among the payment methods you can actually use.
