## Read the exact wording of the vacancy

The language requirement comes from what the employer asks for in that opening. Save the wording and ask about it before deciding whether to book an exam. [Study in Korea’s employment guide](https://www.studyinkorea.go.kr/en/work/aboutKoreaEmployment.do?tab=job-prep) recommends checking the requirements of the company and position before pursuing a certification.

| If the listing says | What to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| TOPIK 필수 (required) | Ask for the minimum level, accepted test format and the date the score report is due. |
| TOPIK 우대 (preferred) | Ask whether you can apply with a work sample or demonstrate your Korean in the interview. |
| 한국어 가능 (able to use Korean) | Ask which tasks use Korean and how the employer evaluates them. |
| The language is unclear | Ask which language the employer uses for interviews, meetings and written work. |

If the recruiter says the role requires “business Korean,” ask for one routine task. A support role might involve a spoken customer call. Another job might require reading a briefing and replying in writing. Choose a practice task that resembles the work and confirm whether the interview includes an assessment.

## Match the score report to the format and the deadline

The government’s [TOPIK overview](https://www.studyinkorea.go.kr/en/plan/examAndKoreanStudy.do) lists listening and reading for TOPIK I, and listening, writing and reading for TOPIK II. It identifies paper-based and internet-based formats and lists the speaking test separately. If an employer asks for proof of spoken Korean, ask which speaking assessment or interview it accepts.

Study in Korea gives TOPIK scores a two-year validity period from the date of score announcement. Compare the score date with the recipient’s cutoff. If you are taking the exam for a new application, check local registration and the score release date through the official schedule link on the overview. Tell the employer if results will come out after its deadline and ask whether you should still submit an application.

## Confirm immigration evidence separately

An employer and the immigration office may ask for different evidence. If a recruiter says a certificate is needed for the visa, ask which exact status or occupation it applies to and which office issued the checklist. Give that information to [Korea Immigration Service’s 1345 contact center](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/1862/subview.do) to confirm the accepted proof and when you need it.

When you have a specific offer and the employer plans to sponsor you, use the [E-7 employer sponsorship checklist](/blog/employer-sponsored-visa-e-7-professional) to confirm the occupation code, documents and filing responsibilities.
