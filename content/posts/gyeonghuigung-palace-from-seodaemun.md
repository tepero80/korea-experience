**Choose Gyeonghuigung for a short outdoor palace visit on the Seodaemun side of central Seoul.** From the Saemunan-ro entrance, pass Heunghwamun and continue toward Sungjeongjeon, the main hall. The [palace operator](https://museum.seoul.go.kr/eng/about/annex/gyeonghuigungPalace.jsp) lists free admission, ordinary hours of 09:00–18:00 and closures on Mondays and New Year’s Day.

## Start with the street address

Save **경희궁, 서울특별시 종로구 새문안로 45**. The museum operator’s map shows Seodaemun Station on Line 5 as an approach. From the station, open pedestrian directions to the palace address and use the legal crossings shown for your route. If you are already near Gwanghwamun or City Hall, compare the walk from your real starting point before taking the metro for a short trip.

Allow time to find the entrance from the street and to get back to your next stop. Ask the operator which gate and approach suit you if stairs or uneven surfaces affect your plan. The museum next door has its own address and opening conditions.

## Follow the gate and courtyard sequence

Look for **Heunghwamun (흥화문)** on the street approach. The [operator’s building descriptions](https://museum.seoul.go.kr/eng/about/annex/gyeonghuigungPalace.jsp) explain the outer gate, bridge and reconstructed palace buildings. Continue past the bridge toward **Sungjeongjeon (숭정전)**. The main hall and its courtyard give the visit a simple focus. Follow the paths that are open on the day and read the signs if work or an event changes the route.

| Your plan | Where to spend your time |
| --- | --- |
| A short walk | Go from the outer gate to the main hall and return by the signed path. |
| More time outdoors | Look for the smaller halls or rock feature on the operator’s palace guide, then check which paths are open. |
| Cold wind or rain | Do the gate and courtyard only if the surfaces and weather suit you. Leave time for a separate indoor stop. |
| Late arrival | Check the day’s final admission before leaving your current stop. Choose another day if the visit window is too short. |

If the main court is open, use the view from the courtyard to see how the buildings frame the hall. The operator identifies Sungjeongjeon as a site for formal events and envoy receptions. If you continue to Taeryeongjeon, read its sign for the connection to King Yeongjo’s portraits before deciding whether to visit the rock feature behind it.

The grounds include exposed paved spaces and changes in level. Give each person time to cross the courtyard at a comfortable pace, especially after rain. The photos show how the buildings sit in relation to the approach. Use current on-site directions for the entrance, route and access.

## Choose whether to extend the visit

Check the [official tourism listing](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=110682) for the address and visitor summary. If you want a longer outing, look at the [Seoul Museum of History city model guide](/blog/seoul-museum-of-history-city-model-visit). It gives you a separate indoor plan with its own entry cutoff. There is enough to see at the palace on its own if you have a short break between other stops.

If your main interest is a larger royal complex or a ticketed program, use the [palace comparison](/blog/korean-palaces-guide-gyeongbokgung-to-changdeokgung-2026) to pick a different visit. Save this compact outdoor route for a daytime gap around Seodaemun and Saemunan-ro.
