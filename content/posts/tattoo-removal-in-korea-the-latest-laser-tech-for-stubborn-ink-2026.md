## Show the tattoo and its history

Bring a current photo and say where the tattoo is, when it was done and whether it covers an older design. If you have the artist’s pigment record or notes from previous removal attempts, bring them. Say when you do not know the ink. The [American Academy of Dermatology’s consultation guide](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/hair-removal/laser-tattoo-removal) lists color, location, medicines and a history of raised scars among the things a dermatologist considers.

| Note before the visit | Ask how it affects the plan |
| --- | --- |
| Body tattoo or permanent makeup | Are there pigment or location concerns you need to assess before proposing treatment? |
| Different colors or cover-up ink | Which parts might be harder to lighten or respond differently? |
| Earlier removal or skin reaction | Which past changes or records would help you assess the area? |
| My goal | What could lightening for another design look like? Is complete removal realistic? |

## Ask what the pigment might do

The [U.S. FDA’s tattoo removal overview](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/tattoo-removal-options-and-results) explains that some white and flesh-colored inks and permanent makeup can turn dark with laser treatment. It also notes that some tattoos cannot be completely removed. Ask the clinician what those uncertainties mean for your tattoo and which unwanted changes they will watch for.

## Get a plan that fits your travel dates

Show the dermatologist your departure date. Ask which appointments must be in person, who can assess an unexpected change and what the clinic can send to the doctor you might see at home. If the full plan needs multiple visits, ask whether an assessment during this trip makes sense before committing to a package or return flights. To check how a provider lists its identity and services, use the [medical institution verification guide](/blog/choosing-a-registered-medical-institution-in-korea-official-verification-steps-f).
