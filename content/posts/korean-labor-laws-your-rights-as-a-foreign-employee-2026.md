## Compare the same pay period

The [Ministry of Employment and Labor’s 2026 announcement](https://www.moel.go.kr/news/enews/report/enewsView.do?news_seq=18144&pageUnit=10) confirms the national hourly minimum of ₩10,320. Its [worked wage equivalents](https://www.moel.go.kr/mainpop2.do) show ₩2,156,880 per month for a 40-hour workweek with eight hours of paid weekly rest, converted at 209 hours per month. That monthly example is before deductions and uses a specific schedule. If your hours vary, some pay is recorded as an allowance, or the pay period crosses into a different year, show the actual dates and line items to the labor office and ask which calculation applies.

Pick one pay period. Put the hours you recorded, the gross amount listed on the payslip, the deductions and the bank deposit side by side. If the deposit is lower than you expected, ask payroll in writing which dates and hours it covers and which deductions it includes. Keep the response with the original records so you can show the difference clearly.

## Record the employer, hours and amounts

| Record | What to save |
| --- | --- |
| Employer | Legal name on the contract or payslip, the address where you actually worked, and the name of any staffing company. |
| Hours | Shift dates and start and finish times, schedules or time records, and messages that changed your hours. |
| Pay | Pay period, agreed rate, payslip, promised payday, and amount and date of each deposit. |
| Timeline | A short list of missed payments or disputed hours and when you asked the employer about them. |

Keep copies somewhere you can access independently of a work account. If the name on your contract differs from the branch sign or the transfer sender, record both names and what each one represents. If you still have an offer to review, use the [written job offer checklist](/blog/korean-work-culture-what-foreigners-need-to-know-2026) to get the rate, schedule and employer details on paper.

## Contact the agency that handles the issue

For missing wages, open the ministry’s [unpaid-wage complaint form](https://labor.moel.go.kr/minwonApply/minwonFormat.do?searchVal=SN001) and look for 임금체불 진정. The portal lists an online application and the customer support desk of the labor office that serves the workplace address. Have the employer details and pay-period records ready. If you need language support for an unpaid-wage issue, the [Ministry of Justice’s 1345 guidance](https://mojhome.moj.go.kr/bbs/immigration_eng/229/608451/artclView.do) directs foreign residents to call 1345 and choose option 1 for help and referral.

If you are also contesting a dismissal, save the notice, the date it was delivered and the date it took effect. Ask the [Regional Labor Relations Commission listed by the national commission](https://nlrc.go.kr/nlrc/en/adjudication.html) promptly whether its dismissal procedure covers the case and which filing deadline applies. Bring up any unpaid wages separately with the labor office. If changing employers could affect your immigration status, ask the immigration contact center about that issue as well.
