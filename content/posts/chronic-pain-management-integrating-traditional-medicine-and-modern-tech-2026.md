## Confirm who will assess you

Describe the main reason for the visit, when the pain began and what you hope to be able to do more easily. Ask which department can assess you, whether it needs a referral and what records it can review before an appointment. The [Medical Korea Information Center](https://www.medicalkorea.or.kr/en/convenient) can point you to medical services or help with arranging an appointment. Confirm the clinician, language and fee directly with the facility.

## Make one summary to take to the visit

A [UK NHS pain service](https://www.esneft.nhs.uk/service/pain-management/what-happens-when-you-are-referred-to-the-service/) asks about the effect of pain on daily life and suggests bringing a current medicine list. Use that example to prepare for the Korean provider’s own assessment. Include previous tests, procedures and what happened with past care. Confirm which reports or images to bring or send, and how to share them securely.

| Bring | What to write down |
| --- | --- |
| Pain | Where it is, when it began and what it limits, such as stairs, sleep or work. |
| Tests | Dates, facility names and the reports or images you already have. |
| Meds | Names, doses and timing for medicines, including patches, supplements and herbs. |
| Route | Ask about step-free entry, lifts, an accessible toilet and who can help from the drop-off point. |

If you want appointments with both a conventional medical service and a Korean medicine provider, ask each how it will review the full list of medicines and supplements and communicate with the other service. Confirm who will answer questions about changes and how your usual clinician will get a written summary. For a Seoul appointment, plan a [step-free route and a transport fallback](/blog/accessible-seoul-best-routes-for-wheelchair-and-limited-mobility-2026) around the exact branch and entrance.

## Check medicines before you fly

Verify the active ingredients in any medicine you plan to bring. The [Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety permit notice](https://www.mfds.go.kr/brd/m_74/view.do?Data_stts_gubun=C9999&company_cd=&company_nm=&itm_seq_1=0&itm_seq_2=0&multi_itm_seq=0&page=118&seq=43906&srchFr=&srchTo=&srchTp=0&srchWord=) explains the rule for entry with medicines that contain controlled substances. If you are unsure how an ingredient is classified, ask MFDS about it before travel. See the [checklist for bringing personal medication into Korea](/blog/bringing-personal-medication-into-korea-traveler-guide) to prepare documents and check restrictions. Ask your treating clinician about continuing care or any change to your medicines while you are away.
