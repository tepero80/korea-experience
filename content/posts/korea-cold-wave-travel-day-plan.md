**If Korea's weather service issues a cold-wave alert for your area, treat it as a reason to review the day—not as a live assessment of every street.** Check the alert's location and timing, shorten long outdoor blocks when needed, and keep an easy route back to warmth.

<GuidePhoto id="essentials-korea-coldwave-decision-illustration" caption="Use the current weather alert and your own ability to stay warm when deciding whether to go out." />

## Make the decision before leaving

1. Open the [Korea Meteorological Administration's current severe-weather page](https://www.weather.go.kr/neng/forecast/warning.do). Check whether a cold-wave alert covers the city or region you will visit and when it is valid. Forecasts and alerts change; this guide is not a live warning service.
2. Recheck your actual outdoor plan: how long you will be exposed, whether the route has indoor stops, and how you will get back if you feel unwell.
3. Replace a long exposed walk with shorter connected stops, a museum or another confirmed indoor visit. Check that the venue is open and that the return transport works for your date.
4. Dress in adjustable warm layers, cover exposed skin and use footwear suited to the conditions. Keep your phone charged and tell a companion if you split up or change the route.

The KMA advises limiting strenuous or prolonged outdoor activity during cold waves and taking periodic warm breaks. Its [cold-wave action guide](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/hazard/safety-guide/coldwave.do) also says to use public transport where possible and tell someone your destination and expected timing. These are general preparedness steps; your own health and tolerance vary.

## Know when a normal itinerary should stop

| What you notice | Next step |
| --- | --- |
| You are cold, tired or uncomfortable and can still make decisions normally | Stop the outdoor block, move to a warm public place, reassess and shorten the route. |
| You feel increasingly unwell, confused, unusually exhausted or have trouble speaking clearly | Do not continue sightseeing alone. Ask someone nearby for help and seek urgent medical attention. |
| Someone is unresponsive or in immediate danger | Call **119** for emergency medical or rescue help. Give your location and follow the operator's instructions. |

These are not diagnostic criteria. The KMA lists severe chills, memory or orientation problems, unclear speech and marked fatigue among signs that need urgent attention. For a medical emergency, use emergency services rather than relying on a travel article.

## Keep the day flexible

Before setting out, save the Korean name or address of your accommodation and identify a warm indoor stop along the route. If weather changes, ask venue staff or your accommodation to help with directions.
