Pansori centres on a singer telling a story with a drummer; samulnori centres on Korean percussion. Choose the experience you want to hear, then check the individual concert listing: a mixed gugak programme does not promise both.

## What to look for

| Music | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| **판소리** (pansori) | For story and voice, ask for a synopsis, subtitles or other language support. |
| **사물놀이** (samulnori) | For percussion, confirm that the named piece is on the programme. |
| Mixed gugak | For a broader introduction, read the running order and duration. |

The [National Gugak Center’s Folk Music Group](https://www.gugak.go.kr/site/homepage/menu/viewMenu?lang=en&menuid=001005005003001) lists both genres in its repertoire. Its [performance listings](https://www.gugak.go.kr/site/main/index001?lang=en&menuid=001) are a starting point for Seoul: open the individual event to check date, venue, ticket, age limits, language support and accessible seating. Repertoire and availability change; confirm before making a special trip.

If the genre is less important than location and transport, compare [gugak performance venues](/blog/korean-traditional-music-gugak-performance-venues).
