Look for **PC방** on the sign. [VISITKOREA describes](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=250544) dedicated gaming cafés where visitors can spend time at a computer and may find food on offer. If the first screen is a membership form, ask staff about guest time before buying anything.

## Use a short first session

| Stage | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Before you pay | Ask **비회원으로 이용할 수 있어요?** to check whether non-members can play. Ask the effective hourly charge and how the timer starts. Check that your group meets the venue’s age and ID rules. |
| At the computer | Confirm that your game, account region and ordinary sign-in method work. If you cannot log in, ask staff about another permitted option before adding time. |
| Ordering | Check whether food is charged separately and whether to order at the desk or from the computer. The menu and ordering system are venue-specific. |
| Before leaving | Sign out of the game and any browser accounts, decline saved credentials and check the final bill. Avoid doing banking or sensitive work on a shared PC. |

The tourism authority [lists T1 Base Camp](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=194959) at **서울 마포구 양화로 147 지하 1층** and distinguishes member and non-member pricing. Confirm the current terms before making it a group plan. If you only have a short gap, move on if guest access or your game is unavailable.

If what you want is to watch professionals compete from a spectator seat, open the [LCK match and ticket guide](/blog/korean-esports-attending-live-gaming-tournaments) and confirm that there is a public fixture. A gaming café can be a separate stop even if the match is sold out.
