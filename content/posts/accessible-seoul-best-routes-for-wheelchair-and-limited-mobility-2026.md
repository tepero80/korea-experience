Seoul has useful accessibility information, but a district-wide label such as “wheelchair friendly” cannot tell you whether a particular station exit, sidewalk, entrance, room, or restroom will work for your needs. Plan each outing from the exact starting point to the exact place you intend to use. Ask the venue or transport operator about the details that matter to your chair and support needs, and check again close to your visit.

The Seoul Danurim accessible-tourism site lets you search attractions and services by access features and publishes individual facility notes. Seoul’s subway accessibility page lists equipment by station. These are useful planning sources; neither should be treated as a live guarantee that every link between two places is step-free or that an elevator will be operating when you arrive.

## A practical planning sequence

1. **Choose one or two stops, not a whole district.** Start with the venue’s own access notes, then check how you will reach its entrance.
2. **Check the complete transit path.** Confirm the origin and destination stations, transfers, platform-to-exit route, and the street exit you need. A station with an elevator may still have an inconvenient route or an elevator outage.
3. **Ask the venue specific questions.** Confirm step-free entrance, door width or threshold, lift access between the areas you want to visit, slope and surface, accessible toilet location, and any limits for your equipment.
4. **Keep a fallback.** Save the venue’s phone number and Korean address. If an elevator, entrance, or route is unavailable, use a confirmed alternative or change the stop.

[Seoul Danurim Accessible Tourism](https://www.seouldanurim.net/en/index) provides destination profiles and suggested itineraries. [Seoul’s subway accessibility facilities page](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/movement/public-transportation/subway-accessibility-facilities/) lists station facilities such as elevators, wheelchair ramps, accessible toilets, chargers, and other equipment. Seoul also describes the [Seoul Donghaeng Map](https://english.seoul.go.kr/seoul-helping-map-app-wins-uitp-awards-2025-for-diversity-and-inclusion/) as offering personalized guidance and alerts about inaccessible walking and public-transport areas. Treat maps and facility lists as planning aids; recheck with the operator for disruptions and the exact route you will use.

## Four places to plan around

These are starting points for a trip plan, not a promise that the entire journey between them is accessible. Use each venue’s current access information and verify the approach from your chosen station, taxi drop-off, or vehicle.

### Seoul Museum of Craft Art: galleries and courtyard

Danurim’s facility information, last modified in November 2024, describes a wide entrance, lifts in the exhibition buildings, and accessible toilets. It also warns that the ramp connecting Exhibition Building 1 and Exhibition Building 3 is steep. Ask staff which lift route connects the galleries you want to see, and whether any exhibition or courtyard route is temporarily closed.

The museum’s access information is available through [Danurim’s Seoul Museum of Craft Art profile](https://www.seouldanurim.net/en/attractions/D/TOURINFOTYPE2/43095). The same guide describes a mix of ramps, lifts, and level differences across the site; do not assume every building-to-building path is level.

### Gyeongbokgung and the palace museum: check the route and grounds

The [Royal Palaces and Tombs Center publishes a Gyeongbokgung visitor map](https://royal.khs.go.kr/ROYAL/contents/R701000000.do) with marked wheelchair routes and facilities, including wheelchair rental and lifts. Use that map to choose a route inside the grounds, then confirm current works, access restrictions, and restroom availability with the palace before travelling.

Historic palace grounds can include stone, slopes, and buildings without step-free access. If you are considering a second palace, note that the official [Changdeokgung visitor FAQ](https://royal.khs.go.kr/ROYAL/contents/R404000000.do?schGroupCode=cdg) says most of the palace’s main building area is flat and some ramps are installed, while the garden includes steep hills and unpaved paths that can become muddy. Choose the grounds route separately from the garden; do not assume the same access conditions apply to both.

Palace admission exemptions are subject to the operator’s eligibility rules and proof requirements. The official [admission-fee page](https://royal.khs.go.kr/ENG/contents/R703000000.do) (currently in Korean) distinguishes registered visitors by disability classification: the listed companion exemption applies to the more severe classification, while other listed visitors qualify individually. The page lists separate restrictions for Changdeokgung’s garden and does not clarify whether a foreign disability document qualifies. Ask the palace in advance and bring the documentation it confirms.

### COEX Aquarium: an indoor option

**Korean address:** 서울특별시 강남구 영동대로 513. The aquarium is inside COEX Mall; ask the venue about the entrance and the complete path that suits your needs.

Danurim’s [COEX Aquarium profile](https://www.seouldanurim.net/en/attractions/D/TOURINFOTYPE2/38721), last modified in December 2021, reports ramps, elevators, accessible toilets, and accessible parking inside the facility. The profile gives nearby station walking directions, but a distance alone does not confirm that the street route, crossings, gradients, or station transfer work for your needs. Ask the aquarium or Danurim to confirm the approach you plan to use, especially if you are travelling independently or have a power chair with specific turning or charging needs.

### Some Sevit and the riverside: confirm the connecting paths

**Korean address:** 서울특별시 서초구 올림픽대로 2085-14. Ask the venue about the best drop-off point and the connecting path for your needs.

Danurim’s [Some Sevit profile](https://www.seouldanurim.net/en/attractions/D/TOURINFOTYPE2/43085), last modified in November 2024, describes a wide, step-free main entrance, lifts in the buildings, and accessible toilets in some buildings and nearby Hangang Park. It also notes small level differences on connecting paths. Opening hours and fees vary by business on the site, so check the attraction you intend to visit and the park path before setting a time-dependent plan.

The profile lists bus and subway approaches, but does not establish that the last segment is step-free for every traveler. If that segment is uncertain, ask about a suitable drop-off point or use a transport option confirmed with the venue. Do not rely on the phrase “flat riverside park” as a substitute for checking the exact route and current construction.

## Public transport and vehicle support

Seoul’s [subway station facilities list](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/movement/public-transportation/subway-accessibility-facilities/) helps identify stations with elevators, portable ramps, accessible restrooms, wheelchair lifts, or charging facilities. Check every transfer, not just the first and last station. The page is a facility inventory, not live elevator-status confirmation.

Seoul says the [Donghaeng Map app](https://english.seoul.go.kr/seoul-helping-map-app-wins-uitp-awards-2025-for-diversity-and-inclusion/) offers accessible navigation information and alerts about barriers. Check whether its current features and language work for your trip, and confirm a route with the transport operator if a lift or walking segment is essential.

Danurim offers a vehicle service and travel-aid rentals through its [reservation pages](https://www.seouldanurim.net/resev-info). The English [assistive-device rental guide](https://www.seouldanurim.net/en/rent-asst-en/info) lists manual and shower wheelchairs, portable ramps, and other aids. It says rentals are generally for three to seven days within Seoul, require an approved reservation and an on-site refundable deposit, and should be booked from two months to at least 14 days before the rental date, subject to stock. Read the live terms for current eligibility, delivery, deposit, and equipment details before relying on a device.

**Current service notice:** Danurim’s [2026 reservation notice](https://www.seouldanurim.net/notice/14385_/14385) says vehicle and assistive-device reservations for 2026 close on 4 December, and use ends on 11 December. December vehicle reservations open at 11 a.m. on 2 November for priority applicants and 3 November for all members. The 2027 resumption will be announced separately. The date was checked on 31 October 2026. If travelling after 11 December 2026, do not assume the service is available; check Danurim’s latest notice or contact the center.

## Book an accessible room with the details confirmed

Hotel labels are not enough to confirm a suitable room. Before paying, ask the property to confirm the specific room and route from drop-off or the lobby. Depending on your needs, request:

- step-free route from the entrance to the room and accessible lift dimensions;
- clear door widths, thresholds, turning space, and bed height;
- roll-in shower or bathtub, shower-chair availability, grab-bar position, and bathroom layout;
- accessible toilet dimensions and whether it is inside the room or elsewhere;
- charging permission and a suitable electrical outlet for your mobility device;
- what happens if the advertised accessible room is unavailable on arrival.

Ask for written confirmation and current photographs or measurements. If a feature is essential, confirm it directly with the property rather than relying only on a booking-site label.

## A checklist you can save

- **Before booking:** confirm the room, entrance, toilet, shower, and lift details directly with the hotel.
- **Before each outing:** check the venue’s individual access profile, transit transfer, exact street exit, and any temporary closure.
- **For a historic site:** select the route and buildings you want; ask about stone surfaces, slopes, works, toilets, and documentation for any admission exemption.
- **For Danurim services:** check current eligibility, stock, reservation window, service end date, chair or vehicle dimensions, deposit, and pickup or return instructions.
- **On the day:** keep the venue address, operator contact, and a nearby alternative available.

A good accessible itinerary is built from verified parts: the room, the route to the station or vehicle, the entrance, the places you want to use, and the return journey. Confirm each part with the organization responsible for it, because conditions and temporary outages can change.
