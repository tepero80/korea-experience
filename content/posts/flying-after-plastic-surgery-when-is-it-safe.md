## Check the decision date before the departure date

Ask the clinic when it will review the return trip with you and who can change the plan if needed. Show your full itinerary, including connections and any long journey from your accommodation to the airport. The [UK travel guidance on treatment abroad](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/in-hospital-abroad) explains why travel plans and airline requirements should be discussed in advance.

| Booking | What to verify before paying |
| --- | --- |
| Return fare | Check the change fee, fare difference, deadlines and what happens to the remaining segments if you miss the first one. |
| Hotel | Ask when you can extend the room and whether you would keep the same room and rate. |
| Follow-up | Ask when the clinic can see you again and whom to contact if flight plans have to change. |
| Insurance | Ask whether a change connected to planned treatment is covered and what approval you would need. |

## Compare the cost of a change you could actually make

Open your cheapest fare and a fare with more flexibility side by side. Compare what you would pay to move both the first and connecting legs. A fare advertised as changeable can still cost more if a different flight has a higher price. If the airline and booking company give different instructions, ask the ticket issuer who can make the change and keep their answer.

Think through one practical change to the itinerary. If the departure moved, identify where you would stay, who would move your tickets and how you would reach the clinic. Keep those details offline. When the clinic can assess your travel plan, follow the [airline clearance and return travel guide](/blog/flying-after-surgery-when-is-it-safe-to-head-home-doctor-advice-2026) for the operating carrier’s paperwork.
