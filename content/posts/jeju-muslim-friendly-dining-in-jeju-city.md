**For a meal in central Jeju City, start with Visit Jeju’s current listings for Mount Everest Restaurant Jeju and Warda Restaurant.** Visit Jeju describes halal food at both; its Mount Everest listing also says the restaurant has a small prayer room. Treat that as directory information to reconfirm with the restaurant before you travel. The listings do not show either place’s Korea Tourism Organization certification category.

This is a small, practical old-town shortlist, not a claim that Muslim-friendly food is easy to find across Jeju Island. If your stay is in Seogwipo or elsewhere, check the current listings for your area before setting out.

**Check before going:** opening hours, reservation needs, menu and ingredient details can change. Ask the restaurant directly if you require a particular certification, need to avoid specific ingredients, or want to use a prayer room. A directory description is not a guarantee about every dish or kitchen practice.

## Understand the labels before you rely on them

The Korea Tourism Organization’s [Muslim-friendly travel page](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/thingsToDo/subMuslimFriendly.do?menuSn=231) explains four restaurant categories: **Halal Certified**, **Self-certified**, **Muslim Friendly**, and **Pork-free**. In the KTO descriptions, “Muslim Friendly” can mean some halal dishes are offered and alcohol may be sold; “Pork-free” means no pork is offered, but there may be no halal menu and alcohol may be sold. The KTO page also warns that hotel restaurants labeled Muslim-friendly may still serve dishes containing pork.

Those categories describe KTO’s system. The Visit Jeju entries above do not display a KTO category, so do not infer a certification from the word “halal” in a venue description. If the distinction matters to you, ask the restaurant what certification it holds and which specific dishes meet your requirements.

Useful questions to ask before ordering:

- “Is this dish halal-certified, and by which certifier?”
- “Does it contain pork, lard, alcohol, or cooking wine?”
- “Are utensils and cooking surfaces shared with other dishes?”
- “Can I use the prayer room today, and when is it available?”

These questions are prompts for direct confirmation; the answers depend on the restaurant and dish.

## Plan a compact old-town food stop

The two listings are both in Jeju City’s old-town area. Use the Korean addresses in a map app and check the route, opening status and current traffic for your travel time. Mount Everest’s Visit Jeju page says it is near Dongmun Market and Tap-dong; this makes it a possible meal to pair with an old-town visit, but it does not establish that the whole area is step-free or that a short walking route suits every traveler.

## Before you leave

1. Open the Visit Jeju listing on the day you plan to go and check for updates.
2. Call the venue if certification, ingredients, hours, reservations or prayer-room access are essential to your plan.
3. Save the Korean address as well as the English place name; map search can work better with the Korean text.
4. Keep another nearby meal option in your saved plan in case a listing is outdated or the restaurant is closed.
