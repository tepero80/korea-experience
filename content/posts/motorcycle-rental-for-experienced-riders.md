A car entitlement alone should not be assumed to cover the motorcycle you want. Check your documents and the exact bike with the rental operator before committing to a trip.

## Before you book

| Check | Ask the provider |
| --- | --- |
| Entitlement | Does my original license and permit authorize this motorcycle and engine size? |
| Rental | Are my documents, age and experience accepted? What payment and deposit are required? |
| Coverage | What are the deductible, exclusions and liability for theft or damage? |
| Equipment | Are a suitable helmet and protective equipment available? |
| Route | Does the route avoid expressways and other roads signed as closed to motorcycles? |

The Korean government’s [foreign-license guidance](https://m.easylaw.go.kr/MOM/SubCsmOvRetrieve.laf?ccfNo=1&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=1&csmSeq=2332&langCd=700101) covers different motorcycle classes and says qualifying permits are usable for up to one year from entry. Confirm the permitted category for your vehicle and circumstances. For example, [one rental operator](https://kmotorental.com/guide/rental-terms-and-conditions/) requires international customers to present a valid permit with an A stamp and passport; its terms are not a universal rental guarantee.

Plan around restrictions: Article 63 of the [Korean Road Traffic Act](https://law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1031179029) limits two-wheelers on expressways and motorways to emergency vehicles. Check signs along the route, including entry ramps. Ask the operator to review your intended route, inspect the bike before leaving, and save its emergency number. If a car better fits the trip, see the [car rental checklist](/blog/rent-a-car-in-korea-international-license-and-navigation-app-guide-2026).
