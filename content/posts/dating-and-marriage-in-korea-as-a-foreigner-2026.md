## Agree on plans at your own pace

Before a date, suggest a place, a time, and how you will get home. Ask directly if you are unsure whether the invitation is one-on-one. As you get to know each other, ask how each person wants to handle bills, photos, messages, or meeting family. Either partner can decline an invitation or ask for more time.

If marriage comes up, discuss where you would both like to live and what each of you expects from a ceremony. Decide together whether you need an interpreter to discuss or sign documents.

## Ask four different questions

| Topic | Ask this |
| --- | --- |
| Ceremony | What kind of celebration do we want, and when can we confirm the date? |
| Report | Which office takes the marriage report for our nationalities and circumstances? |
| Abroad | Does either country require a separate report or certified copy? |
| Residence | Does either of us need to apply separately to keep living in Korea? |

Under [Article 812 of Korea’s Civil Act](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1026989973), a marriage formed through the Korean reporting process takes legal effect through that report. Both parties and two adult witnesses sign the written report. Ask the accepting office who needs to appear in person, which form it wants, and which originals, translations or authentication it requires from each person.

## Check the document list for both nationalities

Tell the office whether you plan to report the marriage first in Korea or have an existing record abroad. For example, the [Korean Embassy in the Philippines](https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/ph-en/brd/m_3282/view.do?seq=727045) lists a Philippine marriage certificate and Korean translation for a Korean–Filipino couple reporting an existing Philippine marriage to a Korean city hall. It says to ask city hall about additional documents. For a marriage involving another country, ask the relevant local registration office and consulate for their requirements.

Write down which office will issue the proof and what each country wants to see. Ask the [Korea Immigration Service contact center](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/1862/subview.do) about any separate residence process and the office that handles it. If you are considering a marriage visa through a Korean spouse, use the [F-6 consulate and document guide](/blog/f-6-marriage-visa-requirements-and-interview) to prepare your questions.
