## Set up for the kitchen you have

Use a skillet on a normal kitchen stove, or use a tabletop electric grill if the manufacturer allows indoor use. The [U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission’s guidance on carbon monoxide](https://www.cpsc.gov/safety-education/safety-guides/carbon-monoxide/carbon-monoxide-fact-sheet) warns against burning charcoal inside and using outdoor camping stoves in enclosed spaces. If the instructions do not establish that the equipment is suitable for your kitchen, use the stove and serve cooked batches at the table.

| Before guests arrive | Set this out |
| --- | --- |
| Make the table ready | Washed lettuce or perilla leaves, rice, a side dish and a bowl of sauce |
| Pick what to cook | Whole-cut pork belly (samgyeopsal) for a straightforward wrap. Ask the butcher to identify the cut if you are unsure. |
| Separate raw and cooked food | Two plates and two sets of tongs. Keep raw meat and its utensils in the kitchen. |
| Check the meat | A food thermometer and the correct temperature for the cut. |

## Cook with a thermometer and serve in batches

Follow [the FDA’s food handling guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/safe-food-handling) and keep raw meat apart from cooked food. A new batch goes into the pan with the raw tongs, then onto the serving plate with clean tongs. Use a food thermometer according to its instructions.

The [U.S. CDC guide](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/prevention/index.html) gives 63°C (145°F) followed by a three-minute rest for whole cuts of pork. Use 71°C (160°F) for ground pork or beef and 74°C (165°F) for poultry. Check the category for what you buy. Thickness and pan heat affect how long it takes.

## Build a wrap with what you can find

The [Korea Tourism Organization’s guide to leaf wraps](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=903&vcontsId=229195) shows how pork belly and ssamjang fit into a shared meal. Put a little cooked pork and rice in a lettuce leaf, add a small dab of ssamjang and fold it. Perilla is another option if you can find it. Serve the sauce separately so people can choose how much they use. Check the label if a guest needs to avoid an ingredient such as soy, wheat or sesame.
