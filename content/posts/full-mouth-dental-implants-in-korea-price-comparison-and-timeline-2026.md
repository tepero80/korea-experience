## Make the quotes comparable

Get both estimates in writing in the currency you will pay. Ask which arches the estimate covers and who issues each bill. The [FDA patient guide](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/dental-devices/dental-implants-what-you-should-know) distinguishes parts of an implant system and recommends keeping the brand and model for your records.

| Line item | What to ask about |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | Does the price cover one arch, both arches or a stated number of implants? |
| Extras | Which scans, procedures and separate professional fees are included? When would the amount change? |
| New teeth | Does the estimate include both a temporary and a final set? Which clinician will fit each one? |
| Aftercare | Where can I get adjustments or repairs? Who coordinates with my dentist at home and who pays for follow-up? |

## Check where each appointment takes place

Ask the dentist to name each stage and which dates depend on an assessment. [Guy’s and St Thomas’ patient guide](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/dental-implants/having-dental-implant) explains that impressions, fittings and delivery of replacement teeth may take several appointments. Before buying tickets, write down which visits are planned in Korea, how the team will tell you if dates have to change, and what records your dentist at home would need. If the quote says “same-day,” ask what it includes using the [first-appointment checklist](/blog/same-day-dental-implants-2026).
