## Read the alcohol number on the actual drink

If a bottle says **제로 슈거** (zero sugar), look separately for **알코올 도수**, the alcohol strength, often shown with a percent sign or **도**. [Lotte Chilsung’s Saero page](https://company.lottechilsung.co.kr/kor/brand/saero/contentsid/970/index.do) describes one zero-sugar soju at **15.7% alcohol**. Use the percentage on the product in front of you even if a different label from the brand looks familiar.

| On the label | Your next check |
| --- | --- |
| **제로 슈거** or a zero-sugar claim | Read the separate alcohol percentage. |
| **알코올 도수** or ABV | Check the actual number for the specific can or bottle. |
| A zero-alcohol claim or **0.00%** | Check that the percentage refers to alcohol and to that exact product. |

The [manufacturer’s announcement for Kloud Clear Zero](https://company.lottechilsung.co.kr/kor/company/news/view.do?detailsKey=869&f=&q=) gives an example of a separate drink described as **0.00%**. Use that as an example of the wording. Check the shelf or menu for the current product.

## Ask to see the label before you order

Show **알코올 0.00%인 음료 있나요? 라벨을 볼 수 있을까요?** (“Do you have a drink that is 0.00% alcohol? May I see the label?”). At a table ordering by screen, ask staff to confirm the item before submitting the order.

If you cannot verify the drink, ask for **탄산수** (sparkling water), **물** (water) or a tea option. If someone is ordering for the table, let them know your choice first. The [restaurant ordering guide](/blog/how-to-order-food-in-korean-restaurants-phrases-guide-2026) covers useful phrases and ways of ordering.

**Editorial check.** The examples illustrate what to look for on a label. Product formulations, packages and availability can differ.
