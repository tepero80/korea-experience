## Start with the job duties and the proposed code

Ask the employer for the proposed E-7 subtype, the occupation code and the legal name of the sponsoring company. Send the recruiter a short list of the work you would actually do and ask them to confirm that it matches the role the company plans to submit. If the offer mentions more than one branch, ask which workplaces the employer plans to list. Save that reply with the written offer.

The government’s [Study in Korea guide to employment status](https://www.studyinkorea.go.kr/en/work/aboutForeignerEmploymentSystem.do) lists examples of E-7 occupations and documents that an in-country status change may require from both the applicant and employer. Use these examples to plan a conversation. Ask the office handling your case for the checklist that applies to your proposed occupation and route.

| Check | Ask the employer or immigration |
| --- | --- |
| Role | Which subtype and occupation code will you submit? How do the written duties and workplace match it? |
| Pay | Which pay rule applies to this code and filing date? Which parts of the written compensation will the office consider? |
| Sponsor | Who handles the filing? Who provides the employer’s documents and any required recommendation? |
| My records | Do I need proof of a degree, prior work, a license or language ability? Which format and issuer are accepted? |
| Filing | Which office handles my case? When can I start? Who will update me if the filing or decision is delayed? |

## Check the contract against the dated wage notice

Compare the pay in the offer with the [Ministry of Justice’s 2026 E-7 wage notice](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/moj/184/601893/artclView.do). Its attachment covers February 1 through December 31, 2026, lists wage standards by subtype and identifies occupations subject to separate standards. If your application falls outside that period, ask for the applicable notice and effective date. Ask how the office treats any bonus, allowance or other variable pay before counting it toward the requirement.

Bring the draft contract and a clear duties list when you ask about the case. The [written job offer checklist](/blog/korean-work-culture-what-foreigners-need-to-know-2026) helps you compare pay, hours and start arrangements. Ask the sponsor to put any revised terms in writing.

## Confirm who files each document and when work can begin

Tell the [Korea Immigration Service contact center](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/1862/subview.do) your current status, where you will apply and the code proposed by the employer. Ask which office can confirm the route, which items the company handles, and whether your own submission requires originals, translations or authentication. Confirm those details before paying for a document or sending an original.

If you already live in Korea, use the [change of status checklist](/blog/changing-visa-status-while-in-korea) to organize the questions for your immigration office. Before agreeing to start work, confirm which permission you need and when it takes effect. If the employer, duties, workplace or start date changes before you begin, show the office the updated details and ask what needs to be filed or confirmed again.
