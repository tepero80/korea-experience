A historical drama may use a real palace, a purpose-built set or a folk village. A screen location can change, and visiting does not mean a production is filming there. Choose a place for the actual visitor experience on your date.

Before buying a package, [check what a production-themed tour actually includes](/blog/behind-the-scenes-k-drama-production-tours).

## Compare the setting

| Site type | Verify before leaving |
| --- | --- |
| Palace | Opening day, admission and restricted rooms or events. |
| Folk village | Admission, performance schedule and access to the area you want. |
| Film set | Whether it still exists and accepts independent visitors. |
| Residential location | Public access and residents' privacy. |

The [VISITKOREA Dae Jang Geum Park guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=192854) identifies a purpose-built MBC set in Yongin and names dramas such as Jumong. That is a record of productions that used the site. For a village visit, check the Korean Folk Village operator’s visitor page separately. Check the operator's [admission and conditions](https://www.koreanfolk.co.kr/ticket/admission-fees) for the visit, since performances and facilities may change.

Save the Korean name and the route to the visitor entrance. Compare travel time, closing time and your return connection. Check whether any special event requires a separate reservation and follow posted photography restrictions. If an area is closed, choose another public part of the attraction rather than entering it.
