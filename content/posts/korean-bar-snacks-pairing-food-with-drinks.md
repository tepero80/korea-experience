## Ask what is on the table

The [Jeonju Makgeolli Alley listing](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=&vcontsId=229053) describes tables where a kettle comes with side dishes. Ask what the venue serves on your date, how large the kettle is and which parts are priced separately.

The tourism organization’s [food pairing guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=600&page=1&sContsTtl=&sort=regDt&vcontsId=189701) lists pancakes among common accompaniments. That gives you an idea to look for on a menu, even at a venue with separately priced dishes.

| Goal | Order question |
| --- | --- |
| Share dinner | How many people does the kettle and accompanying food serve? |
| Try one dish | Is the pancake or other snack sold separately, and how large is it? |
| Skip alcohol | Can you buy a dish and a nonalcoholic drink at this table? |
| Avoid an item | Can the server explain the ingredients in the specific food served? |

## Leave room for the rest of the evening

Ask whether there is a minimum table order before settling in. One set may be a whole meal for your group. If you just want to taste a single dish, keep a second venue with à la carte options in mind. For the Jeonju area and seating choices, use the [makgeolli bar guide](/blog/makgeolli-bars-traditional-rice-wine-culture).
