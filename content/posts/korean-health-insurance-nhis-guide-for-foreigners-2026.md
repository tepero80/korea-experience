Start with the category and effective date on your record. The [official NHIS guide for foreigners](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02900m01.do) describes workplace, local and dependent enrollment separately. Use it to prepare your questions, then have NHIS look up your own record.

## Find out who handles your enrollment

| Situation | What to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| You started a job | Ask payroll which start date it reported and which month the first deduction covers. |
| You received a bill at home | Ask NHIS which period the bill covers, whose account it is, and when it is due. |
| You want to add family | Ask what NHIS needs to verify the relationship, which documents require translation or authentication, and when enrollment could start. |
| You changed or left a job | Ask the employer for the reported end date and check with NHIS which category applies afterward. |

For a dependent application, give NHIS the dates and facts for the family member whose coverage you want to check. If you have foreign-issued documents, get the current submission checklist for that relationship before ordering new certificates.

## Compare the dates on a bill and a payslip

Keep the dates in front of you when you call. A bill delivered this month may cover a different period from this month's payroll deduction. Ask NHIS to identify each covered month, explain any balance and tell you how to check a correction. If you have moved, ask which address or delivery channel is on record. Keep the bill, payroll entry and any receipt until you resolve the difference.

If a payment notice is near its due date, ask NHIS which amount is due while the issue is being checked and how to pay through an official channel. This gives you a separate question to resolve even if the employer needs more time to confirm a filing.

## Check your record before relying on coverage

Use the [NHIS contact page](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02000m01.do) for English consultation. It lists 033-811-2000 within Korea and +82-33-811-2001 from overseas. Ask which office handles your address if you are told to visit in person. Keep the notice date and any reference number available, and use the official channel to share identifying documents.

Before a planned appointment, confirm the active date with NHIS and ask the clinic what it can verify and whether the specific service is covered. For the rest of the move, use the [first-week arrival checklist](/blog/moving-to-korea-checklist-everything-you-need-2026) to coordinate paperwork, temporary housing and payment backups.
