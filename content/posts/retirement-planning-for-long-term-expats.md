## Put the transition dates on one page

Write down your last workday, each household member’s current status and expiry date, your lease end date, and any trips you expect to take. Ask payroll which date it expects to report for the end of your employment or employer coverage. Use the same dates when you speak to each agency.

| Check | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Stay | Does the end of this job affect my status or a family member’s status? Is there a report or application to make, and what date governs each step? |
| Health cover | When does workplace cover end for each person? Could local or dependent cover apply, from what date, and what proof would we need? |
| Pension | Which contribution months are on record? How do any overseas contributions or agreements affect the benefit options available to me? |
| Housing | What deposit, monthly charges, lease terms and exit costs are in writing? How would I reach my usual clinic and pharmacy without a car? |

## Verify status, coverage and pension independently

Call [Korea Immigration Service at 1345](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/1862/subview.do) with your status, expected work-end date and family situation. Ask about each person’s permission to stay and any travel you have planned while an application is pending. Record the office and the dates it identifies.

The [National Health Insurance Service guide](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02900m01.do) describes workplace, local and dependent coverage. Ask NHIS which route applies to each person once payroll stops, what the contribution would be, and whether there is any gap between the old and new coverage. If you use ongoing care, confirm how you will keep appointments and refill prescriptions during the transition.

Use the [Korean pension deduction and enrolment checklist](/blog/korean-pension-system-understanding-deductions) to gather your record. Ask NPS which benefit options it can assess and how they would change if you stayed or left. Its [agreement overview](https://www.nps.or.kr/eng/soclsecuagre/overview/getOHAI0024M0.do) explains why the answer depends on the country and agreement. Before making a claim, ask whether taking a refund would affect the contribution months you plan to use for future benefits.

## Use confirmed details before making a housing commitment

Compare realistic monthly costs from actual lease and insurance quotes. Include building charges, transport, routine care and an allowance for a timing gap. Visit the area at a time you would normally travel to a clinic and try the route from the building. If two countries may be involved in your tax or estate planning, take your actual dates and income sources to a qualified adviser before acting.

If leaving Korea remains an option, the [NPS refund guidance](https://www.nps.or.kr/eng/ntnlpnsplan/frgnrlpsmrfnd/getOHAI0015M0.do) explains why a departure-based claim depends on individual eligibility. Read the [Korean pension refund checklist before departure](/blog/korean-retirement-system-for-foreigners-national-pension-refunds-2026) to see what information and documents to gather. If you live abroad and are planning an extended visit instead, use the [long-visit checklist for retirees](/blog/long-term-tourist-visa-options-for-retirees) before booking.
