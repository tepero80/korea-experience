## Decide which family route to ask about

Ask the receiving office about each traveler. A couple and their children may need to prepare more than one set of forms. Start with two facts: the sponsor's nationality or Korean status code, and how each applicant is related to that person.

| Family situation | First question |
| --- | --- |
| A spouse or unmarried minor child will join a foreign national working or studying in Korea | Does the sponsor's exact status and situation support F-3, and what must happen before the dependent applies? See the [Ministry of Government Legislation's dependent visa explanation](https://m.easylaw.go.kr/MOB/CsmInfoRetrieve.laf?ccfNo=1&cciNo=2&cnpClsNo=1&csmSeq=1703). |
| A foreign spouse will join a Korean citizen to live together in Korea | Is the spouse-of-a-Korean-national route within F-6 appropriate, and which mission or immigration office can receive the case? See the [government's marriage-related status guide](https://m.easylaw.go.kr/MOB/CsmInfoRetrieve.laf?ccfNo=2&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=1&csmSeq=47). |
| The applicant is a parent or adult child, or the sponsor's stay status is changing | Ask which family route applies to that relationship and whether the sponsor's pending change affects the order of applications. |

## Confirm the filing order and documents

If you are applying abroad, check which Korean diplomatic mission covers your legal residence and whether appointments are available. If you already live in Korea, ask immigration whether the exact change can be filed inside the country before relying on a consulate checklist. For a dependent application, ask whether it can be filed with the principal's case or only after the principal has a visa or residence record. Check what proof the sponsor must provide and whether you need a separate consent or custody document for a child travelling with one parent.

For each certificate, ask which format is accepted, how recently it must be issued, whether a translation is needed and if authentication or an apostille is required. Check that names and birthdates match across records. For marriage-based applications, the [Ministry of Justice publishes a notice on evidence and screening exemptions](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/moj/184/601864/artclView.do). Ask the receiving office which requirements apply and which documents are needed to show any exemption.

## Plan around the actual decisions

Ask when each family member can travel, whether entry dates can differ and which steps are needed after arrival. If work or school depends on the move, confirm what that person's own status will allow and what permission or registration they need before committing to a start date. Ask what happens to each dependent application if the sponsor changes jobs or status while the cases are pending.

If you have confirmed that you will apply overseas as a spouse of a Korean citizen, use the [F-6 marriage visa checklist](/blog/f-6-marriage-visa-requirements-and-interview) to work through the mission's forms, proof of relationship and follow-up questions.
