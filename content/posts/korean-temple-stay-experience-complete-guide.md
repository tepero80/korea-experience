A temple stay is a structured visit to a working Buddhist temple. Choose between a program with activities and one with more independent time, then read the particular temple’s instructions: a stay is not a standard hotel package. The [Templestay FAQ](https://eng.templestay.com/en/MI000000000000000063/board/BO00000071/list.do) discusses experiential and freestyle programs and says conditions differ between temples.

## Decide what fits your trip

| Decision | Check before reserving |
| --- | --- |
| Activities | What is included, when does the day start, and can you opt out of an activity? |
| Language | Is the program conducted in English or translated, and on your date? |
| Room | Will you share, is a private room offered, and what are the temple’s room rules? |
| Access and food | Ask about stairs, floor sleeping, walking, dietary restrictions and allergies. |
| Booking | Confirm the total price, payment, arrival time, cancellation and how to reach the temple. |

[Myogaksa’s overnight program](https://eng.templestay.com/en/MI000000000000000019/reserve/view.do?templeId=Myogaksa&templestaySeq=25588) is an example that lists English or English translation, possible room sharing, a uniform and an early start. Its schedule and booking rules apply to that program, not all temples. Check open dates and current details before planning transport.

Tell the staff if an activity is unsuitable; the FAQ says participants who are unwell or tired can rest after informing staff. Ask what to pack and follow the host’s instructions in worship spaces. For language-specific options, see [temple stays for English speakers](/blog/temple-stay-programs-for-english-speakers).
