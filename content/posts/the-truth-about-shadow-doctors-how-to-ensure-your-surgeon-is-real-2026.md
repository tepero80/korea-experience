## Check the clinician before you consent

Ask the facility for the named clinician, department, location and contact for the procedure. Ask who performs each part and how the clinic will notify you of a change. Request an explanation you understand. If you cannot reconcile different answers, pause and ask the facility to clarify the plan before proceeding.

| Check | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Ask the clinician to confirm their name and role. Write down the exact Korean name and department when available. |
| Change | Ask how the facility documents a change in personnel and how it discusses that change with you. |
| Records | [Article 21 of the Medical Service Act](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1028699889) governs requests for access to or copies of your own medical records. Ask the facility how to submit the request and what identification it needs. |
| Video | [Article 38-2](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1032850561) sets out recording requests for surgeries while the patient is unconscious, statutory exceptions and separate rules for viewing or copies. Ask about those processes before the procedure. |

## Keep a contact for follow-up

Save the documents and names you receive, and ask the facility which office handles medical-record requests after you leave Korea. Ask for the operating record separately if your goal is to know what was documented. If you have a question about a recording, explain the date and ask promptly about the applicable request and retention procedure.

See the [Korean medical records checklist](/blog/medical-records-for-international-patients-in-korea-translation-consent-and-copi) to prepare the details of a record request and ask about translation.
