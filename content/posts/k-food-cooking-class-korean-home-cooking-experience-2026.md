A market tour, demonstration and hands-on lesson are different experiences. Check what you will prepare yourself, the instruction language and whether a meal is included.

## Compare the format

| Goal | Ask before booking |
| --- | --- |
| Hands-on cooking | Which dishes will I make, and are ingredients included? |
| Market plus cooking | Is the market walk included, and where does it begin? |
| A specific diet | Can the provider handle the ingredient and shared-surface requirements? |
| Solo or family booking | Is there a minimum group or age limit? |

The [Korean Food Promotion Institute’s reservation calendar](https://resv.hansik.or.kr/main/main.do?language=en_US) lists HANSIK Space E:eum sessions by date and lets you look up a reservation. Open the individual session to check the format, language and meeting point before you book. If you want to combine a class with other food stops nearby, the [Korea Tourism Organization’s Bukchon food route](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/hdrdslt/hdrdsltView.do?crsSn=565466) can help you plan the rest of the visit.

Send your date, party size, language and dietary needs to the provider; confirm total price, cancellation and minimum attendance. A vegetarian description does not establish every broth, sauce or shared utensil is suitable. If an allergy requirement cannot be confirmed, choose another option. Save the confirmation and Korean meeting address offline. For an independent food stop, see [Gwangjang Market](/blog/gwangjang-market-complete-food-guide).
