A rental can make a spread-out itinerary easier, but check your right to drive and the company’s conditions before paying. A booking confirmation does not replace the required documents.

## Before reserving

| Check | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Driving entitlement | Confirm the permit, issuing authority, validity and vehicle category for your circumstances. |
| Rental terms | Send the company your documents and ask about driver age, experience and accepted payment. |
| Total | Compare the quote, deposit, damage waiver, exclusions and charges for extra drivers. |
| Route | Check parking, tolls, pickup and return, and set a navigation route before driving. |

[KOROAD](https://www.safedriving.or.kr/diGuide/selectDiGuide10.do) says Korea recognizes qualifying valid international permits issued by Geneva and Vienna convention countries for up to one year from entry. Confirm your particular document with the authority and your rental company; unofficial documents marketed as international licenses are not equivalent. Bring your passport, original home license and permit if applicable, and ask the company what else it requires.

[VISITKOREA](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/infoBscView.do?vcontsId=140664) notes that companies can set additional age and experience requirements and that vehicle-damage coverage can be optional. Read the actual coverage, deductible and exclusions; “full” is not a substitute for the contract. Photograph existing damage at pickup and keep the company’s emergency contact.

Test your chosen navigation app and destination before departure. Ask whether the car has an active Hi-Pass device and how tolls are billed; without it, use an appropriate staffed payment lane.
