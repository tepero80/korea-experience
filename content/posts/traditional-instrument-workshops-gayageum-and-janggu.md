A gayageum is a string instrument; a janggu is a double-headed drum. Decide which sound you want to make, then look for a class that names it rather than assuming every traditional-music activity includes both.

## Choose the lesson

| Interest | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Gayageum | Is an instrument provided for each participant and what will beginners play? |
| Janggu | Is hands-on drumming included, and can beginners join? |
| Mixed music | Which instruments will you actually play and for how long? |

One [VISITKOREA-listed Bukchon gayageum class](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/thingsToGo/tvExhibit/tourGdsPrView.do?gdsId=2000367) describes a lesson, equipment and hanbok. The listing warns that availability and details may differ: confirm the date, language, duration, price and cancellation terms with the provider. Do not assume it teaches janggu.

In Suncheon, [Naganeupseong's listing](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?vcontsId=80266) names gayageum and instrument experiences and asks visitors to call about hands-on programmes. Confirm the actual instrument and operating day before making the trip. Ask about seating, stairs and age requirements if relevant. To hear professional performers, compare [gugak venues](/blog/korean-traditional-music-gugak-performance-venues).
