For an emergency in Korea, **call 119** for an ambulance. Say where you are, give your callback number and ask for an interpreter if needed. The [official emergency guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=140042) separates emergency dispatch from general visitor assistance.

## Match the next call to the problem

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Medical emergency | Call 119. Give the address of the place you are in now and follow the dispatcher’s directions. |
| Routine question after treatment | Call the treating team or the clinic’s after-hours contact on your discharge paperwork. Ask where and when they want you seen. |
| Bill, coverage or travel changes | Contact your insurer as soon as practical. Ask which documents they need, whether the type of treatment is covered and how to handle any rebooking. |
| Other help | [Medical Korea](https://www.medicalkorea.or.kr/en/convenient) lists 1577-7129 and daily phone service from 09:00 to 18:00 Korea time. Ask about non-urgent information or help with a complaint. |

Once immediate care is arranged, use the [medical dispute and records contact guide](/blog/medical-tourism-complaint-and-support-channels-in-korea-official-contacts-and-do) to identify the organization that handles your concern.

For a change to your return journey, see the [airline forms and return travel guide](/blog/flying-after-surgery-when-is-it-safe-to-head-home-doctor-advice-2026) for questions to ask your treating team and each operating carrier.

If you are still organizing the trip, compare the costs of [keeping your return booking flexible](/blog/flying-after-plastic-surgery-when-is-it-safe) before committing to a date.

## Prepare a handoff to the next clinician

Before leaving a facility, ask for the discharge summary, medication list with doses and times, procedure or treatment details, current instructions and the clinical team’s contact information. Keep itemized bills and proof of payment in case your insurer asks. Where possible, ask for a language the next clinician can use. The [FCDO hospital guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/in-hospital-abroad) explains why receipts and clinical notes matter and why elective treatment can affect coverage.

Before a flight or a long transfer, ask your treating clinician what follow-up they want first and what restrictions affect your plans. Ask the airline which documents it uses and share a copy of relevant records with the clinician who will see you at home. The [treatment-abroad checklist](https://travelaware.campaign.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/189/2025/08/RECENT-PDF-MT-Checklist-July-2025.pdf) covers arranging aftercare and planning for difficulties. Save the [Korea emergency contacts](/blog/medical-emergencies-1330-vs-119-2026) offline with your clinic details.
