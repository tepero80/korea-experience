Look for **청국장찌개** if you want the stew. If you see a tub marked **청국장** at a shop, check whether it is a paste or a prepared food. The [Korean Food Promotion Institute’s food guide](https://www.hansik.or.kr/magazines/list/magazineDetail/30/3008?menuSn=429) explains the difference between cheonggukjang and meju.

## Read the stew and ingredient labels

| Korean | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| 청국장찌개 | Cheonggukjang stew. Use the full name to order a bowl. |
| 청국장 | A preparation made from cooked soybeans with a short fermentation. Check the pack for how the product is sold. |
| 메주 | A fermented soybean block used to make traditional jang. |
| 된장 | Doenjang, a soybean paste. 된장찌개 is a distinct stew. |

## Ask what is in the bowl

The [institute’s stew page](https://www.hansik.or.kr/story/storyView.do?searchId=117) describes tofu, chili or meat as possible additions. [VisitKorea’s doenjang stew example](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=178309) includes clams. A menu word alone does not establish which broth the kitchen uses. Ask about the broth as well as what you can see.

At a market, ask whether it needs refrigeration and read the full ingredients. For other items served with a meal, see the [fermented flavor guide](/blog/korean-fermented-foods-beyond-kimchi-doenjang-gochujang).
