A prescription issued in Korea does not by itself establish permission to bring the medicine into another country. Check the active ingredient, quantity, destination and each transit country with the relevant customs or medicines authority before travel. Ask the prescribing clinic or pharmacy for documentation identifying the medicine and your prescription; retain the original labelled packaging where possible.

## Check the rules for your own journey

| Check | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Medicine | Name and active ingredient, strength, quantity and whether the destination controls it. |
| Border | Whether import is permitted for your residency and purpose, and whether a declaration, permit or prior contact is required. |
| Documents | Which prescription or clinician letter, language and packaging the authorities require. |
| Route | Rules for every transit stop, plus airline and airport screening rules for liquids or equipment. |

Examples show why the details matter. The [US Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/industry/import-basics/personal-importation) says personal imports of unapproved drugs are generally illegal for US citizens and that discretion can apply in limited situations. Its instructions for people traveling to the US from abroad are separate. Read the guidance that describes your situation and check any controlled ingredient with the listed authority before flying.

The [UK Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/controlled-drugs-personal-licences) says its personal import policy for controlled medicines does not apply to UK residents receiving treatment abroad: they should contact its Drug and Firearms Licensing Unit before returning with controlled drugs prescribed abroad. Visitors have different conditions. The same page directs travelers to check other countries’ own rules. These are examples, not a complete country list.

Ask the authority if anything is unclear before you travel, and declare medicines as required. For rules on the outbound leg into Korea, see [bringing personal medication into Korea](/blog/bringing-personal-medication-into-korea-traveler-guide).
