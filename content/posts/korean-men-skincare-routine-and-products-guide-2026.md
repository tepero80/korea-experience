## Start with the item you actually use

Keep the familiar products that fit your plans. If you forgot a product, show the shop a photo of its full name and type and look for the closest replacement.

| Korean label | When to look for it |
| --- | --- |
| **클렌저** | You need a cleanser like the one you use at home. |
| **크림** or **보습제** | You need your usual cream or moisturizer. |
| **선크림** | You need a sun cream to use as labeled for your outdoor plans. |

If a set looks appealing, compare the quantities and read each item on the back. [Round Lab's travel kit](https://roundlab.com/products/dokdo-travel-kit) lists four products, including toner and ampoule as well as cleanser and cream. The word “travel” describes a format. Buy the set if its contents make sense for your trip, or look for one labeled item on its own.

## Check the actual size on the package

A small bottle may be easier to finish, but verify its capacity and whether it is sold separately. [Incheon Airport’s international carry-on FAQ](https://airport.kr/ap_en/1434/subview.do) explains that the **100 mL limit for each liquid or gel container** is based on capacity. A partly used larger tube still has its original capacity. Check the rules on each leg where you go through screening.

## Shop once and return to your plans

Use **작은 용량 있어요?** (“Do you have a smaller size?”) if you need less. Keep the label until you have checked the directions and packed the item. If a product you rely on is hard to replace, bring it with you or confirm the exact shop before leaving. Our [packing guide](/blog/packing-korean-shopping-liquids-and-souvenirs-for-your-flight) explains what else to check on the return trip.

**Editorial check.** The kit is a packaging example. Sizes, versions and branch availability can change.
