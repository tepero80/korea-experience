**For:** visitors already in Seoul who need to decide whether to change a bus or subway journey during snow. **Checked:** 15 November 2026. Seoul’s cited winter plan covers 15 November 2026–15 March 2027; later seasons may use different measures. This is a how-to, not a live disruption feed.

## First check the route you will actually take

Open Seoul’s official [TOPIS transport information system](https://topis.seoul.go.kr/) and check incidents, road controls, bus information and subway information for your route. The interface is primarily in Korean; the labels **돌발** (incidents), **통제** (controls), **버스** (bus) and **지하철** (subway) identify the relevant menus. A citywide snow response does not tell you whether a particular bus, station or train is operating normally. Check again before you leave and before you make a tight connection.

Seoul’s [2026 winter plan](https://scpm.seoul.go.kr/seoul-policy/evt0323), announced on 11 November, describes these city-level measures:

| Snow response stage in the plan | What the city says it will do | What you still need to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Snow-removal stage 2 | Extend the bus and subway peak-dispatch period by 30 minutes. | Your exact line, station, route and departure. The plan does not publish a guarantee for each service. |
| Snow-removal stage 3 | Extend the bus and subway peak-dispatch period by 60 minutes. | Current TOPIS information and the transport operator’s notice. |
| Subway service suspended | Put replacement buses into service. | Whether a suspension applies to your station/line and where replacement buses board. |

The “peak-dispatch period” extension is a city response measure, not a promise that every route has the same frequency or that the last train or bus will run later. Do not plan to catch a usual final service based on the extension alone.

## A practical snow-day decision sequence

1. **Check timing and area:** review the current snow alert and whether it covers your part of Seoul. For the broader alert-to-itinerary process, see [how to respond to a snow advisory in Korea](/blog/korea-snow-advisory-travel-plan).
2. **Check TOPIS:** look for a road control or incident along your bus route and check the bus/subway information panels. Use the operator’s own notice if TOPIS and an operator notice differ.
3. **Check the trip end-to-end:** search the station exit or bus stop nearest your destination, then check the return route. Snow can make a short outdoor transfer or an uncovered stop inconvenient even when a route appears available.
4. **If a subway is suspended:** follow the current replacement-bus instructions for the affected line. Do not assume a substitute bus starts at your usual stop or follows the normal route.
5. **Keep an alternative:** choose a nearby indoor stop or postpone a cross-city journey if the route remains unclear. Ask your accommodation to help interpret an official notice if needed; do not rely on an old screenshot or a social post as current status.

## Where to check official information

- [TOPIS](https://topis.seoul.go.kr/): current Seoul traffic controls, incidents and bus/subway information.
- [Seoul winter measures](https://scpm.seoul.go.kr/seoul-policy/evt0323): the city’s dated winter response plan and information channels.
- **120 Dasan Call Center:** Seoul’s plan lists 120 as a source for public-transport operation information. The page does not promise a particular language service; ask your accommodation for help if you need it.
- [Seoul Safety Nuri](https://safecity.seoul.go.kr/): the city identifies this as a place to check snow-removal status. Road clearing information is not the same as a confirmation that public transport is operating.

TOPIS is the place to check the current situation; the city plan explains response measures. Neither one guarantees a specific journey. If your route includes a flight, intercity train, attraction or reservation, check that provider separately before changing the booking.
