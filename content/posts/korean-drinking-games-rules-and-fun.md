## Explain the rules in one minute

Two people are enough to start. Pick a first player and, for a larger group, choose the order around the table. Start the count at one. Each player says one, two or three consecutive numbers and stops. The next person picks up from the very next number. Whoever says **31** loses the round. The [Chungbuk National University counting-game exercise](https://contents.kocw.net/KOCW/document/2014/Chungbuk/owontae/13.pdf#page=57) describes this version on PDF page 57.

Choose one language everyone is comfortable counting in. If someone uses **순번** (*sunbeon*), the [National Institute of Korean Language](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=64901) lists it as “turn” in the sense of a rotation.

## Play a sample turn

| Where you are | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| It is the first turn | A says **1, 2** and stops after two numbers. |
| B goes next | B can say **3** and stop, **3, 4** and stop, or **3, 4, 5** and stop. |
| Later, A ends on **26** | If B says **27, 28**, A can answer **29, 30**. |
| The previous player ends on **30** | The next player has to say **31** and loses. |

If two people are playing, ending on **26** sets up your own next turn. When the other player calls one, two or three numbers, you can call three, two or one, respectively, to reach **30**. This works because the same two players alternate turns.

## Try a timing game with a larger group

The [Gyeonggi Office of Education’s keyword nunchi game](https://www.goe.go.kr/resource/old/BBSMSTR_000000030136/BBS_202412180301513750.pdf#page=21) has players stand and call a word, with no set turn order. Two people who go at once are out, as is whoever goes last. For a seated version, choose a theme such as foods and use a hand raise instead of standing. On the start signal, anyone can raise a hand and name one food. Each person goes once. If two people go together, restart the round, or agree in advance that they sit that round out.

## Set the stakes at the table

Ask who wants one round and show everyone a practice turn. If you want to keep score, mark one point for the loser and play three rounds, with the lowest score winning. You can also start over after each round and rotate the first player. Let people sit out a round or watch until they are ready.

If the host wants to raise a glass before the game, try a [short Korean toast](/blog/korean-drinking-etiquette-pouring-and-toasting). For a longer dinner with mixed drink preferences, see [how to join a Korean group dinner without drinking](/blog/korean-drinking-etiquette-2026).
