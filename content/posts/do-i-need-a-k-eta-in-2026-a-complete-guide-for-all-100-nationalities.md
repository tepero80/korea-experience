**There is no single answer for every passport.** First check whether your nationality, passport type and purpose of travel qualify for visa-free entry. Then check whether a K-ETA is required or an exemption applies to your circumstances. Use the official services and confirm your documents with your airline or other carrier before departure.

| Check | Where to begin |
| --- | --- |
| Visa or visa-free entry | [Korea Visa Navigator](https://www.visa.go.kr/openPage.do?MENU_ID=10102) |
| K-ETA or temporary exemption | [Official application guidance](https://www.k-eta.go.kr/portal/guide/viewetaapplication.do?locale=EN) |

## Check the current temporary exemption

On **23 November 2026**, the Ministry of Justice [announced an extension](https://www.k-eta.go.kr/portal/board/viewboarddetail.do?bbsSn=332884) of the temporary K-ETA exemption for countries and regions already covered. The announced extension runs from **1 January through 31 December 2027, Korea Standard Time**. The notice does not itself list every covered passport or determine your individual eligibility. For a trip on either side of that period, check the latest official instructions for the passport and travel date you will actually use.

The notice says an eligible applicant will see an exemption message when scanning the passport information page during the official application process. If the message or your circumstances are unclear, use the K-ETA site's inquiry service and consult the Korean mission responsible for you. Do not assume that a temporary exemption gives a right to work, extends the permitted stay, or replaces a visa required for your intended activity.

If you plan to stay in Korea for an extended period after retirement, use the [long-visit planning checklist](/blog/long-term-tourist-visa-options-for-retirees) to check the full itinerary with the consulate.

## Work through your own case

1. **Identify the travel document and purpose.** Use the nationality and type of the passport you will present, your arrival date and the actual reason for travel. If your purpose requires a visa, consult the [Korea Visa Portal](https://www.visa.go.kr/openPage.do?MENU_ID=10102) and the appropriate Korean embassy or consulate before applying for a K-ETA.
2. **Check the official K-ETA service.** Start at [the official K-ETA application guide](https://www.k-eta.go.kr/portal/guide/viewetaapplication.do?locale=EN). Read the current eligibility guidance and notices; if using the application process to check exemption, review the passport-scan message carefully before paying. An intermediary's page cannot settle a disputed case.
3. **If you need to apply, leave time for review.** The official site says assessment generally takes up to 72 hours, but timing is not a guarantee. Apply well ahead, check the actual result through the official service and verify that the details match your travel passport. Approval does not guarantee admission; the immigration officer makes the entry decision.
4. **Before departure, check again.** Confirm the latest official notice and your document requirements for the route with your airline or other carrier. A carrier's boarding check and Korea's immigration inspection are separate stages.

## K-ETA and the arrival card are different

A K-ETA is a travel authorization; an arrival card is an arrival declaration. Being exempt from the K-ETA requirement does not automatically exempt you from the arrival declaration. The [official declaration guide](https://e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/guide/eacTargetGuide.do?locale=EN) includes temporarily K-ETA-exempt travelers among those subject to a declaration, while identifying K-ETA holders among exclusions. It also notes a qualification for someone holding a K-ETA but entering with a visa. Check the eligibility page for your own documents.

The [e-Arrival Card guide](https://e-arrivalcard.go.kr/portal/guide/eacGuide.do?locale=E) says the online declaration is free and is separate from K-ETA. The Ministry's exemption notice says an eligible traveler may still choose to apply for K-ETA to use its benefits, with an application fee. Check whether a declaration is needed before deciding; an optional paid application is not a substitute for checking your entry basis.

If your arrival details change after a declaration, see our [e-Arrival Card changes guide](/blog/korea-e-arrival-card-when-plans-change). Recheck all instructions near departure because notices and individual circumstances can change.
