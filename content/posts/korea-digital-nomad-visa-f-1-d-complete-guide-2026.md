Start by identifying the Korean mission that accepts applicants where you legally reside. Use the [Korea Visa Portal](https://www.visa.go.kr/openPage.do?MENU_ID=10101) as a starting point and check the mission’s current instructions for F-1-D. If already in Korea, ask local immigration which office can take your application and whether your current status permits a change. Confirm this before you make travel or housing commitments.

## Mark up the checklist before ordering anything

| Item | Confirm with the office |
| --- | --- |
| Work | Employer or business proof, required history and remote-work authorization |
| Income | Accepted payroll, tax or bank documents and the period they must cover |
| Insurance | Coverage, dates, proof format and any separate evidence for family |
| Address | Whether your chosen region needs a lease or a qualifying booking |
| Records | Background checks, translations, legalization and document issue dates |

The [Singapore mission checklist](https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/sg-en/brd/m_2444/view.do?page=1&seq=761493) illustrates why jurisdiction matters: it specifies proof connected to employment there and lists accommodation evidence for people using the lower regional income threshold. The [Philippines checklist](https://overseas.mofa.go.kr/ph-en/brd/m_3277/view.do?seq=738705) asks for specific income and background documents for its own applicants. Use these examples to see what can vary, then use the list from your own mission to assemble the file.

## Check the file in the order you will submit it

1. Confirm the income and address band with the [F-1-D eligibility guide](/blog/digital-nomad-visa-korea-requirements-and-application) and your receiving office. Ask how it applies the [immigration service update](https://www.immigration.go.kr/sites/immigration/file/ebook/gongzone/2026/2026_gongzone_vol77.pdf) to your intended address.
2. Mark each document as ready, needs an original, or unclear. Combine questions for the office before paying for certificates, translations or accommodation.
3. Verify names and dates across the form, passport, work proof and insurance. Ask whether accompanying relatives submit separate paperwork and how to handle an address change before a decision.
4. Confirm the submission method and how to check the result. Keep the receipt and a copy of the completed file. After approval, read the dates and any registration requirements stated for your own stay.

If you cannot confirm a checklist item, send the mission the item name and ask which document it will accept. Keep personal records out of any public message used to ask for advice.
