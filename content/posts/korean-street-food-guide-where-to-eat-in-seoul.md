If a Seoul stall menu is unfamiliar, pick one snack that suits your appetite and ask about the filling before ordering. You can always order another after seeing the portion. Write down the Korean word if the stall has no translated menu.

## Choose your first order

| Sign or menu word | What to expect and check |
| --- | --- |
| **떡볶이** (tteokbokki) | Rice cakes. The familiar red version is usually spicy. Ask what comes in one portion and what is in the sauce. |
| **어묵** (eomuk) | Fish cake, often served on a skewer with broth. Ask whether the marked price is for one skewer. |
| **김밥** (gimbap) | Seaweed and rice with a filling that varies by item. Ask which roll the displayed price refers to. |
| **호떡** (hotteok) | A filled pancake. Check sweet versus savory and let a hot filling cool. |

The [VISITKOREA bunsik guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=205&vcontsId=140730) explains the familiar dishes. Their [seasonal food guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=1595642) lists Myeongdong, Gwangjang and Namdaemun as Seoul starting points. Opening times and specific items still depend on the stall.

## Keep the stop easy

For a moving snack, check if the seller can wrap the item first. At a spot with seats, ask whether standing and eating near the counter is possible or whether the seats are reserved for orders. Show the seller what you are ordering and confirm the total before paying.

Show or say **이거 하나 주세요** (one of these, please), or ask **이 안에 뭐가 들어 있어요?** (what is inside?). If a dietary requirement is important and staff cannot confirm the ingredients or preparation, choose an alternative you can confirm.

For a seated or covered food-aisle option, open the [Gwangjang Market food guide](/blog/gwangjang-market-complete-food-guide) before moving across town.
