## Match the document to the recipient

Ask what the person receiving it needs in order to act. You may need more than one document. At [Seoul National University Hospital](https://www.snuh.org/global/en/patients/EN02005.do), an English medical certificate is requested during the appointment, while medical record and imaging copies have separate instructions.

| You need | Ask the provider about |
| --- | --- |
| Confirm the visit or diagnosis | An English medical certificate, what it includes and when the doctor has to prepare it. |
| Continue care at home | The relevant notes and reports. For imaging, ask whether they need the written report, the image files or both. |
| Document a claim | The itemized receipt, evidence of payment and any separate certificate or form requested by your insurer. |

## Ask while you can speak to the treating team

Show the doctor's office the recipient's requested document names. Confirm the spelling of your name, the visit dates and the intended method of delivery. If the document is only available in Korean, first ask whether the recipient accepts an English summary or requires a translation and which translation format is acceptable.

Ask for the price, expected completion and who can collect the document in case you have left. The [SNUH Rare Disease Center FAQ](https://raredisease.snuh.org/en/patients/faq/) gives its own paperwork instructions, so ask your provider for its procedure. Send health documents through the secure channel the recipient specifies. Use the [record copies and consent guide](/blog/medical-records-for-international-patients-in-korea-translation-consent-and-copi) if you expect to arrange collection from abroad.
