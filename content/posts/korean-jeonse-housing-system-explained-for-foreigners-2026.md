## Check the property and your eligibility

Seoul’s [rental guidance](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/living/housing/1-wolse-jeonse/) describes jeonse as an arrangement with a large deposit. HUG’s [English guarantee page](https://www.khug.or.kr/hug/web/en/02/en02000002.jsp) lists a deposit-return guarantee for tenants. Its [foreign tenant FAQ](https://www.khug.or.kr/hug/web/ig/dr/igdr000003.jsp?gotoPage=2) describes the specific product’s eligibility and mentions evidence such as registration and address reporting, possession and a fixed date. Ask HUG or an appropriate counselor to explain what applies to your contract.

| Check | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Lease | Check that the legal address, lessor name, deposit, term and payment account are consistent across the proposed documents. |
| Risk | Ask a qualified rental counselor to help interpret the property register and existing claims before relying on the promised return date. |
| Cover | Ask the actual guarantee provider about eligibility, covered amount, application deadline, lease evidence and exclusions. Keep any confirmation of issuance. |
| Proof | Keep the signed lease, receipts and any address, possession or fixed-date records the provider says it needs. |

If a problem occurs during the lease, the [rental repair reporting guide](/blog/dealing-with-korean-landlords-common-issues) explains what to document and where to ask for help.

## Prepare for the return before you move

Confirm the required notice, expected payment date and contact while there is still time before move-out. If payment may be delayed, ask your counselor or guarantee provider about the case before returning keys or changing address. Save financial records and dated correspondence together.

See the [jeonse and wolse comparison](/blog/jeonse-vs-wolse-korean-rental-system-explained) if you are still deciding how much money to commit upfront.
