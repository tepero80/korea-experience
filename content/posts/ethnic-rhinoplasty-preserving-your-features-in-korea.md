## Describe features in your own words

Start with what you notice in a mirror or an ordinary photo. A note such as “I like how my nose looks from the front” gives the surgeon something specific to discuss. You can mention whether a feature matters to you for personal or family reasons, or leave that out. The [NHS guidance before a cosmetic procedure](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/cosmetic-procedures/advice/before-you-have-a-cosmetic-procedure/) recommends explaining why you are considering a change and what you expect from it.

| Note | What to bring up |
| --- | --- |
| Keep | Point out a feature you want to retain and the view where it matters to you. |
| Change | Describe one concern and when you notice it. Mention any breathing concern separately. |
| Unsure | Name a decision you want to think through before agreeing. |

If you choose to bring pictures, use them to point to a feature from a particular angle. Ask what the clinician can judge from an image and what they need to assess in person. If a simulation is shown, ask which parts are illustrative and what remains uncertain about the result.

## Check that the explanation survived translation

Speak to the surgeon in a language you understand. Ask an interpreter to stay for the part where you discuss options, risks and consent as well as the opening conversation. The [NHS guidance for cosmetic surgery abroad](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/cosmetic-procedures/advice/cosmetic-surgery-abroad/) emphasizes finding out whether the surgeon can explain the procedure and understand what you are asking. Use the [guide to confirming consultation language in Seoul](/blog/finding-english-speaking-doctors-in-seoul) to check who will interpret and whether that person is available for your actual appointment.

Before deciding, ask the surgeon to explain which points you agreed on and which choices are still open. Try saying, “I want to keep this feature. What would your plan change about it, and what could happen to my breathing?” Restate the answer in your own words and have the surgeon correct anything you misunderstood. Ask for a written summary in a language you can review. If you still have questions, use another appointment or take the summary for a second opinion.
