**Before a beach, breakwater or rocky coast walk, check the local forecast and current safety notices.** A calm-looking viewpoint or dry sky does not guarantee that waves cannot reach the area. If conditions are unsafe or access is closed, stay away and choose an inland alternative. Swell can occur outside winter too; check the forecast whenever visiting the coast.

## Plan the visit

1. Check the [KMA severe-weather page](https://www.weather.go.kr/neng/forecast/warning.do) for the affected coast and effective time. Its Korean-language [coastal swell forecast](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/forecast/ocean/swell.do) lists selected locations, including Jeongdongjin in Gangneung. If a location or forecast is missing, that does not establish safety. Ask the local tourism office or accommodation about current closures and transport to your actual viewpoint.
2. Choose a route with a safe fallback. Do not plan a photo that requires stepping onto wet rocks, a closed breakwater or an area where waves are reaching the shore.
3. Take warm layers and appropriate footwear; allow enough daylight and a way back if buses are infrequent. Weather, walking surfaces and services may change.
4. Follow local barriers and staff instructions. When the sea or wind is hazardous, move to a safer area rather than waiting close to the water for a photograph.

The KMA's [Korean-language marine safety guide](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/hazard/safety-guide/wind-wave.do) discusses strong waves and the danger near breakwaters. Its [coastal swell information](https://www.weather.go.kr/kma/news/press_01.jsp?mode=view&num=1194561) describes forecasts for coastal areas; use current local information, not an old forecast embedded in a travel post.

| Before you leave | On arrival |
| --- | --- |
| Confirm local weather and transport. | Read current signs and stay out of closed areas. |
| Keep an indoor or inland alternative. | Change the plan if waves, wind or surface conditions are unsafe. |
| Check the return journey and sunset for your date. | Do not treat a photograph or online route as current access information. |

## If the sea becomes dangerous

Move away from the water and follow local staff and barrier instructions. For an immediate emergency in Korea, call **119** for fire and emergency rescue; [VISITKOREA’s emergency FAQ](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/faq/faqMainView.do?menuSn=404&page=1&pstSn=94) also lists **112** for police. Give your location, including the named beach, road or landmark if you can. Do not enter the water or a closed area to retrieve belongings.

This is a seasonal planning checklist, not a live safety assessment.

**Next:** if the coast is unsafe, choose an inland activity and check that venue’s official opening hours and transport before setting off. If your coastal trip includes a ferry, confirm the actual sailing with its operator.

Sources checked 9 November 2026. Forecasts, closures and transport change; local authorities and operators decide current access.
