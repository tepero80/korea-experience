Before leaving the facility, check the [discharge contact checklist](/blog/post-surgery-swelling-timeline-what-to-expect-week-by-week) so you know whom to call if swelling or another concern changes.

## Ask the treating team for a written plan

Before paying for a room, ask what help you will need at discharge and where your follow-up will take place. The [NHS discharge guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/hospitals/going-into-hospital/being-discharged-from-hospital/) gives an example of planning transport, support and contacts before leaving hospital. Ask your own team how those arrangements apply to you. Keep its instructions in a language you understand.

| Check | Question for the lodging provider |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | Is there a step-free route from the drop-off point to the exact room? Confirm the lift and bathroom access if those matter to you. |
| Arrival | When will the room be ready? Can a companion collect the key or wait indoors with you? |
| Changes | If the treating team changes your schedule, how late can you extend, move or cancel the booking? |
| Help | What practical help can staff arrange, and whom can you reach after the desk closes? Confirm medical support separately with the treating team. |

If your stay relates to a breast implant consultation, use the [breast implant follow-up questions](/blog/breast-augmentation-in-korea-cost-clinics-and-recovery-2026) to ask what records and visits you may need after leaving.

## Keep the room, return journey and follow-up connected

Write down how you will reach the clinic, who will help with the transfer and how to contact the treating facility outside normal hours. The [UK guidance on treatment abroad](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/in-hospital-abroad) recommends planning for healthcare after treatment and transport home. If you also have a flight, review the [airline and return-travel checklist](/blog/flying-after-surgery-when-is-it-safe-to-head-home-doctor-advice-2026) with the treating team before relying on your departure date.
