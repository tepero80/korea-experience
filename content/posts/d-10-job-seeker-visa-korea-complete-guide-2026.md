## Decide which question immigration needs to answer

[Study in Korea’s foreign employment guidance](https://cdn.studyinkorea.go.kr/popup/employment_regulations_full.jsp) distinguishes job seeking, internships and other preparatory activities. Its [residence and status overview](https://www.studyinkorea.go.kr/ko/life/residenceAndStayInfo.do?tab=job-seeker-visa) describes D-10-1 professional fields and examples of the documents it may ask for. Write down your current status and expiry, degree and proposed job before calling [the Immigration Contact Center](https://www.immigration.go.kr/immigration_eng/1862/subview.do).

| Check | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Field | Describe the work you would seek and ask which professional category and job-search route would apply. |
| Proof | Check which qualifications, proof of address and other supporting evidence your category requires. |
| Plan | Prepare the job-seeking activity plan, including concrete past and future steps if requested. |
| Work | Ask whether a specific internship or proposed job needs a report or a change in status before beginning it. |

If an employer makes an offer, use the [written job offer checklist](/blog/korean-work-culture-what-foreigners-need-to-know-2026) to review its terms and confirm permission before starting.

## Leave time for the actual application

Confirm which office or service accepts the case. Ask which deadline and extension rules apply to your category and what to do while the request is pending. Keep the reply, submission record and any requested follow-up documents. Tell immigration about travel outside Korea before making firm bookings.

See the [D-2 to D-10 guide for Korean university graduates](/blog/d-10-job-seeker-visa-after-graduation) if you are moving from student status after finishing a Korean degree.
