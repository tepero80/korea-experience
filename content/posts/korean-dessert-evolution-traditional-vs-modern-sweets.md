## Match the dessert to the day

| Stop | What to order and check |
| --- | --- |
| Market stall | Ask for a small portion of tteok (떡, rice cake). Check the filling and how the seller recommends storing it before you buy a box. |
| Tea house | Pick a drink by taste, then share a rice cake or another dessert the shop has that day. Ask whether your group can use chairs if floor seating is difficult. |
| Modern dessert café | Check whether a cake, bingsu or drink is priced per person or serves several. Read the actual menu instead of choosing from the display photo. |

## Two examples to save

In Jeonju, the official listing for Jungang Market identifies a Tteok Alley. Use 떡골목 as the cue to ask for the lane. Store hours vary, so shop earlier in your time in Jeonju if taking a box is important.

In Seoul, the tourism directory lists Tteulan in Ikseon-dong with traditional tea, rice cakes and floor seating. Check the current arrangement before taking someone who needs a chair. For more walks and places to pause in the neighboring area, see the [Insadong tea-house guide](/blog/tea-houses-in-insadong-2026).

## Buy for the ingredients and the journey

Ask which items contain nuts, sesame, dairy or another ingredient that matters to your group. A drink order, a piece from the case and a takeaway package may each use different portions. Buy what you can eat on the day unless the shop confirms the packaging and keeping instructions suit your onward trip.
