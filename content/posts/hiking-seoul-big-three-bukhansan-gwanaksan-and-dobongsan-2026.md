Pick the exact route, not just a mountain name. Bukhansan and Dobongsan belong to the same national park, but the areas and access points differ. Gwanaksan has both gentle paths and steeper, rocky routes.

## Match the route to your group

| Start with | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Bukhansan | If you plan the Ui Valley approach, open the Bukhansan Hiking Tourism Center pin and the map for your particular route. A summit climb needs more preparation than a forest stroll. |
| Dobongsan | Check the Dobong sector in the national park notices. Confirm the entrance and return before assuming the Ui Valley guidance applies. |
| Gwanaksan | From Gwanaksan Station, ask about the Dorimcheon stream trail for a gentler option or the Yeonjudae course for a more demanding day. |

## Check three things before leaving

Open the [national park page](https://www.knps.or.kr/front/portal/visit/visitCourseMain.do?menuNo=7020002&parkId=121500) for route and access notices when planning Bukhansan or Dobongsan. Confirm the actual segment, especially when there are multiple routes to a summit. Look at conditions again on the morning of the hike.

The [Bukhansan branch](https://english.visitseoul.net/seoul-hiking/seoul-hiking-tourism-center-bukhansan/ENN50nn0f) describes the Ui Valley start and equipment services. [Gwanaksan’s center](https://english.visitseoul.net/seoul-hiking/seoul-hiking-tourism-center-gwanaksan/ENNllgajy) is inside Gwanaksan Station on the Sillim Line and publishes examples for different levels. Check the branch opening and rental arrangements in advance if you depend on them.

Write down the planned turn-back time and the last practical connection before you start. Carry enough water, footwear that suits the terrain, and an offline route. If your footing, conditions or timing become uncertain, turn back on the marked route.

If a summit is no longer a good fit, use [these shorter Seoul walks](/blog/hidden-hiking-trails-near-seoul-less-crowded-alternatives) to choose a named route with a simpler return.
