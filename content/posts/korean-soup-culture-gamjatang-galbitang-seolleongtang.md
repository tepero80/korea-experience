Start by deciding whether you want one bowl with rice or a pot to share. **탕** on its own does not tell you the size of the order. Read the restaurant’s portion labels alongside the dish name.

If the menu lists 김치찌개 and 된장찌개 as well, [compare the two stews and what goes into them](/blog/korean-soup-culture-a-guide-to-kimchi-jjigae-doenjang-jjigae-and-more-2026).

## Match the soup to your appetite

| Look for | What comes with it |
| --- | --- |
| 설렁탕 | [Beef-bone broth](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/thingsToDo/foodTrip/special_view.do?vcontsId=187425), sometimes with beef and noodles. Ask whether rice comes separately. Taste before adding any salt provided. |
| 갈비탕 | [Beef rib soup](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?vcontsId=68086). Check whether rice is included and whether there are bones in the bowl. |
| 감자탕 | [Pork backbone stew](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?vcontsId=46518). Ask how many the pot serves. If you are dining alone, ask whether the shop has a single-bowl option such as 뼈해장국. |

Show **이 메뉴는 1인분인가요?** to ask if the dish is one serving, and **밥도 나오나요?** to ask whether rice comes with it. If the board shows **소·중·대**, ask what the smallest size serves before paying. Tell the seller about any ingredient you need to avoid and ask what is in the stock, not just which meat is visible.

Use the [restaurant ordering guide](/blog/how-to-order-food-in-korean-restaurants-phrases-guide-2026) for additional menu words, refills and payment.
