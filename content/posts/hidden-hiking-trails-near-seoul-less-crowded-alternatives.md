When you want trees or city views without committing to a major summit, start with a named path and choose where to turn back before setting out. A map pin for a mountain can lead to the wrong side or a steeper approach.

## Choose by terrain and the way back

| What you want | Path to look up |
| --- | --- |
| A flexible forest walk | **안산자락길** (Ansan Jarak-gil). Seoul describes it as a barrier-free loop. Confirm your route from transit and turn back early if the full loop is too long. |
| A gentler Namsan walk | **남산 북측순환로** (North Circular Road). This is one part of Namsan’s trail network. Save your own entrance pin rather than routing automatically to N Seoul Tower. |
| A city wall section with climbing | **인왕산 한양도성** (Inwangsan wall section). Check the side you want to approach and the route back before setting out. |

## Save your start and fallback

The [Seoul government’s green trails guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/green-trails-everywhere-seoul/) identifies Ansan Jarak-gil. [Visit Seoul’s Namsan trail notes](https://english.visitseoul.net/area/Namsan-Trails/ENP9jn7zj) distinguish the North Circular Road from the other paths. Ask about specific detours or access conditions if you rely on a step-free approach.

The [city government’s Hanyangdoseong guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/amusement/hanyangdoseong/) traces the Inwangsan section from the old Donuimun site toward Changuimun. The area has both wall paths and streets. Set a turnaround before steep ground if there is rain or frost, and stay on marked paths.

A shorter route gives you a reasonably simple fallback when daylight is limited. For an indoor history stop check the [Seoul City Wall Museum guide](/blog/seoul-city-wall-museum-guide). For a summit-oriented plan, see [the three-mountain comparison](/blog/hiking-seoul-big-three-bukhansan-gwanaksan-and-dobongsan-2026).
