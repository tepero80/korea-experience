## Check the kind of reply you received

Seoul National University Hospital’s [online request page](https://www.snuh.org/global/en/patients/appointmentMain.do) is one example of a hospital asking visitors to submit a request and wait for a coordinator’s response. Look for a reply that gives the appointment date, time and department. Save it and ask when to check in.

| Route | What to confirm |
| --- | --- |
| Online | The confirmation, name on the booking and how to change it. |
| Phone | Whether you have a slot, the correct department and where to check in. |
| Walk-in | Whether new patients can register that day and when the counter closes. |
| Referral | Whether the provider needs a referral or records before accepting the visit. |

For pregnancy-related appointments, use the [maternity intake checklist](/blog/pregnancy-and-childbirth-in-korea-a-guide-for-foreign-mothers-2026) to ask about prenatal records and whether the hospital can accept your request.

If your travel depends on a confirmed appointment, use the [medical visit visa checklist](/blog/korea-medical-tourism-visa-how-to-apply) to ask which clinic documents the consulate requires.

## Make the visit work on the day

Save the Korean name of the clinic, its exact branch and your booking response. Ask which ID to carry, what the consultation may cost and whether language support covers the consultation itself. When a clinic asks for medical records, use a channel you have verified with the provider and check which documents are needed.

The [Medical Korea Information Center](https://www.medicalkorea.or.kr/en/convenient) offers information on institutions and booking assistance. For help identifying a provider in the first place, see [how to check English-language care in Seoul](/blog/finding-english-speaking-doctors-in-seoul).
