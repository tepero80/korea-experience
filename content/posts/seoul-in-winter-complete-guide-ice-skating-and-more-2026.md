Seoul’s winter event calendar has a confirmed citywide anchor for 2026–27: the Seoul Metropolitan Government lists the **Seoul Winter Festival from December 4, 2026, through January 31, 2027**. Its announced locations include the Hangang River, Gwanghwamun Square, Cheonggyecheon Stream, Seoul Plaza, Bosingak Pavilion and Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP). The city notice gives the festival period and locations; check the current program for what is actually running on your visit date. ([Seoul Metropolitan Government](https://english.seoul.go.kr/the-fun-seoul-to-become-a-global-cultural-hub-hosting-festivals-all-year-round/))

For the confirmed Cheonggyecheon evening event, the [Seoul Lantern Festival guide](/blog/seoul-lantern-festival-2026-planning-guide) covers its exact schedule and viewing span. This page helps you choose a winter visit window and avoid relying on last season’s rink dates, fees or operating hours.

## Choose one evening area

The citywide festival list covers several separate venues, so build your evening around one area rather than assuming the sites form a single walking route. For Cheonggyecheon, use the event’s published endpoints as orientation and follow current signs and staff directions. For Gwanghwamun Square, check the Seoul Light organizer notice separately. For Seoul Plaza or DDP, confirm that the particular program you want is scheduled on your date.

If the lantern display is your priority, use the dedicated [Seoul Lantern Festival planning guide](/blog/seoul-lantern-festival-2026-planning-guide) for its listed hours, endpoint span and evening decisions. Do not treat the citywide festival period as proof that a specific installation is open each night.

## Ice skating: verify this season’s operation

Seoul’s [rink listing](https://festival.seoul.go.kr/festival/main/festivalView.do?festacode=355) schedules the Seoul Plaza rink for **December 18, 2026–February 9, 2027**, near City Hall Station Exit 5. It lists hours of 10:00–21:30 Sunday through Thursday and 10:00–00:30 Friday and Saturday. December 24 and 31 are listed until 01:00 the following morning.

The city lists **₩1,000 per one-hour session, including skate and helmet rental**. Weather and air quality can pause operation. Check the rink’s booking instructions and availability before setting out, especially if you are going after dinner or on a holiday.

## Make a winter plan that can flex

- Check the event’s own notice on the day, especially for outdoor programs and weather-related changes.
- Confirm a live transit route to and from the chosen venue; a published event window is not a promise of your preferred last train or bus connection.
- Leave room to shorten an outdoor stop or switch to an indoor activity if conditions or access change.
- Pack for the amount of time you expect to spend outside. See the [Korea winter packing checklist](/blog/korea-winter-packing-checklist), [snow advisory travel plan](/blog/korea-snow-advisory-travel-plan) and [icy sidewalk guide](/blog/walking-on-icy-sidewalks-in-korea) for practical preparation.
- If wet weather changes your plans, use the [Seoul museums and exhibitions planning guide](/blog/seoul-museums-and-exhibitions-planning-guide) as an indoor-oriented alternative.

The city’s current announcement makes early December through January a useful planning window for Seoul’s winter festival season. Treat each event’s own current notice as the final source for dates, operating hours, access and closures.
