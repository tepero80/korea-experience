If a self-service kiosk declines your overseas card, pause before trying again. Check whether the order or payment went through, then ask staff whether you can pay at a counter or use another method. A decline message does not tell you whether the problem is your card, the issuer, or that particular terminal. Your card issuer can investigate the specific decline.

## At the kiosk: a quick recovery sequence

1. **Check the screen and your bank app.** Confirm whether the order was cancelled, still pending, or completed before starting over. Keep the receipt or take a photo of the error message without exposing card details.
2. **Try the terminal’s other supported option.** If it offers another language, select it and follow the on-screen instructions. Use only the payment methods and card-entry method that the terminal displays.
3. **Ask a staff member for a staffed checkout.** You can say, “Can I pay at the counter?” or show this Korean: `계산대에서 결제할 수 있나요?` (Can I pay at the counter?). A counter may use a different terminal, but it is not guaranteed to accept the same card.
4. **Use a backup method.** If available, try a second card or pay cash. The Korea Tourism Organization says international cards are widely usable at many businesses, but some stores may not support card payment; it advises checking availability before purchase. Seoul likewise describes credit cards as usable at most businesses, not every payment terminal. [KTO: Korean Currency & Exchange Info](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=140040) · [Seoul: Currency Exchange and Payment Methods](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/entry/currency-exchange/)
5. **If more than one merchant declines the same card, contact the issuer.** Visa says the issuing bank is best placed to explain a decline; Mastercard also directs cardholders to their card-issuing institution. Use the secure number or contact route on your card or issuer’s app. [Visa Consumer Support](https://usa.visa.com/support.html) · [Mastercard FAQs](https://www.mastercard.com/hk/en/personal/get-support/frequently-asked-questions.html)

## A card-friendly transit machine is a specific exception

Since March 17, 2026, Seoul says internationally issued cards can buy short-term Climate Cards and single-journey tickets at 440 new machines in 273 stations on subway Lines 1–8. The city reports an average service fee of about 3.7% for those purchases and recharges. This applies to those listed transit-card machines; it does not mean every retail, restaurant, or ticket kiosk accepts overseas cards. Check the machine’s prompt and displayed fee before paying. [Seoul Metropolitan Government: International Cards at Climate Card and Single-Journey Ticket Machines](https://english.seoul.go.kr/climate-cards-and-single-journey-transit-tickets-now-accepting-international-credit-cards-no-cash-needed/)

For T-money, the Korea Tourism Organization’s 2026 transportation guide says purchase and top-up at the listed convenience stores and subway locations are cash-only for foreign-issued cards. Keep that separate from Seoul’s newer credit-card option at selected Climate Card and single-journey ticket machines. [KTO: International Visitors’ Use of Public Transportation and Booking/Payment Services (May 2026)](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/public/contents/travel/KoreaTransportationGuide_enu.pdf)

## If the terminal offers currency conversion

Some card terminals or ATMs may offer to charge in your home currency instead of Korean won. Visa says the screen should show both amounts, the exchange rate, and any added fees, and that the customer must be able to choose. If those details are missing or you feel pressured, Visa recommends declining the conversion and contacting your issuer. Read the offer; accepting or declining is your choice. [Visa: Dynamic Currency Conversion](https://www.visa.com/en-gb/personal/travel/dynamic-currency-conversion)

## Before you travel

- Pack at least two payment methods if practical, and make sure each is available to you if your phone or one card is unavailable.
- Install or bookmark your issuer’s app and save its international support number. Ask your issuer whether international purchases, cash withdrawals, and app-based verification are enabled for your card; settings differ by bank and card.
- Carry a modest amount of Korean won for cash-only purchases or an unexpected terminal problem. Do not rely on a fixed amount from an old guide: your needs depend on your itinerary and available backup methods.
- For a large or time-sensitive purchase, check the merchant’s payment options before ordering. For Seoul subway travel, distinguish the newly supported ticket machines from T-money purchase/top-up and from tap-to-pay at gates.

### Does a kiosk decline prove that my card is blocked?

No. A kiosk message alone does not identify the cause. If the same card fails at multiple merchants, check the issuer’s app or contact the issuer for the actual decline reason.

### Will switching to English make my card work?

It may make the instructions easier to follow, but it does not change which payment methods the merchant’s terminal accepts.

### Can I use an overseas card to top up T-money?

The KTO’s May 2026 transportation guide lists T-money purchase and top-up at the named locations as cash-only for foreign-issued cards. It separately lists new Seoul subway machines where international cards can be used for certain Climate Card and single-journey ticket transactions. Check the exact machine and service before relying on it.

### What if the card is still declined?

Ask the merchant to confirm the order status, keep the decline details, and contact the card issuer, who can help investigate a card-specific decline and suggest what to do next.
