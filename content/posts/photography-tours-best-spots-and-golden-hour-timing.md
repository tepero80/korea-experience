**Start with your location and date, then plan around the last entry and the ride home.** Sunset over Seoul, lights on a pond in Gyeongju and dawn off Jeju each need a different plan. Use the examples below for the place you will already be visiting.

## Pick the light and the place together

| What you want to photograph | Where to start and what to check |
| --- | --- |
| City shapes before and after sunset | **Seoul · Namsan.** Check the path or lift options up and save a way down. Allow time to reach the viewpoint before the light changes. |
| Lit pavilions and reflections | **Gyeongju · Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond.** Confirm last admission and whether the site will be lit. Wind can break up reflections. |
| The coast before or after dawn | **Jeju · Seongsan Ilchulbong.** Compare local sunrise with the confirmed opening time and travel from your accommodation. Keep a daytime alternative if you cannot enter in time. |

## Work backward from the time you need

1. Open the [Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute’s local rise and set tool](https://astro.kasi.re.kr/life/pageView/6). Choose the closest location and your date. Its calculated time is a reference; cloud, local terrain and your view direction determine what you actually see.
2. Check the exact gate, last admission and any tripod or photography rules for that site. Choose a legal public approach and leave room for ticketing, queues or an uphill walk.
3. Record your downhill route, station or pickup point before dark. Keep one earlier or later alternative if weather or an access change makes the planned frame impractical.

## Seoul · Try the Namsan panorama at dusk

Use the signed public approach near [N Seoul Tower](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?vcontsId=88026). Check the [operator’s route for the cable car and onward walk](https://www.nseoultower.co.kr/eng/visit/traffic2.asp) if you plan to go that way, and check return operating hours separately. Give yourself time to look for where the sun appears from your chosen direction. For a lower, river-level route, see the [Seoul golden-hour guide](/blog/photographer-guide-to-seoul-best-golden-hour-spots-2026).

## Gyeongju · Check the gate before waiting for the reflections

The [city attraction listing](https://www.gyeongju.go.kr/tour/page.do?area_uid=50&cmd=2&mnu_uid=4716) publishes hours and ticketing information for Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond. Enter early enough to pick a position along the designated path. A calm surface can reflect the lights; if it is windy, photograph the lit architecture from a permitted spot. Use the [Wolji and Woljeonggyo walk](/blog/gyeongju-donggung-wolji-woljeonggyo-evening-walk) for the order and return route.

**Save:** 동궁과 월지 · 경상북도 경주시 원화로 102. ## Jeju · Compare sunrise with the actual entrance hours

[Visit Jeju](https://www.visitjeju.net/en/detail/view?contentsid=CONT_000000000500349&menuId=DOM_700000000010774) distinguishes the paid walking route from the lower visitor area at Seongsan Ilchulbong. Confirm the summit path and same-day conditions before counting on a climb. If opening or your ride will be too late, move the visit to daylight or pick a legal public viewpoint you can safely reach. The [Seongsan bus and return guide](/blog/seongsan-ilchulbong-by-bus-from-jeju-city) covers the day-trip approach.
