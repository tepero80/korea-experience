The [KTO makgeolli description](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=231646) explains that makgeolli is rice wine made with a fermentation starter called nuruk. At a bar, decide whether you want a small taste, a bottle for the table or a meal centered on a shared kettle. Ask what the venue actually offers before taking a seat.

## Choose the order that matches your evening

| Goal | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Taste one drink | Is a glass or small sampler available? Can you order one food item? |
| Share two styles | What is the volume of each bottle? Can you see the label before deciding? |
| Make it dinner | Is food bundled with the kettle? What is the smallest order for your group? |
| Skip alcohol | Can you order food and a non-alcoholic drink at this table? |

If your priority is an organised tasting and time to learn about production, [compare the Ganghwa brewery and Pocheon museum visits](/blog/makgeolli-brewery-tastings-2026).

## Choose a starting point in Jeonju

The [Jeonju tourism overview](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=&vcontsId=229053) describes a kettle with a spread of side dishes. The [Samcheon-dong street listing](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=351&vcontsId=229025) places an area of makgeolli bars along Geomasan-ro and says opening hours vary by business. Searching **삼천동 막걸리골목** will help you identify the area. Save your actual restaurant and return route separately. The neighborhood is a starting point for choosing somewhere to go; the meal and charge depend on the particular menu.

At the door, ask **막걸리 한 주전자에 안주가 포함돼요?** to check whether food is included with a kettle. If ordering another kettle, ask whether it comes with extra food. For a daytime stop near the historic center, use the [Jeonju Nambu Market food plan](/blog/jeonju-nambu-market-eating-plan) and leave room for the evening if the bar serves a full spread.
