**Start with the eating instructions from your treating team, then find meals that fit.** Pumpkin porridge, abalone porridge and herbal drinks are not a substitute for care or a proven universal way to reduce swelling. Your procedure, swallowing ability, allergies, medicines and other conditions can change what is suitable.

## Before leaving the clinic

Ask which textures and ingredients you may have, whether there are restrictions, whom to contact if eating or drinking becomes difficult, and when to seek urgent help. Write the instructions in a language you understand. A hotel or restaurant should not have to infer your clinical diet.

The [Royal National Orthopaedic Hospital's nutrition guide](https://www.rnoh.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-guides/eating-well-before-and-after-surgery-patients-guide) explains the role of adequate food and protein in recovery. It is general patient information, not a diet prescription for your operation.

## Turn instructions into an ordering checklist

| Meal option to discuss                 | Practical checks before ordering                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Juk / 죽 (porridge)                    | Texture, temperature, portion and actual ingredients; abalone and other seafood may be present            |
| Soup with rice                         | Stock, seasoning, salt restrictions if prescribed, and whether it is a complete enough meal for your plan |
| Tofu, eggs or another familiar protein | Allergens, preparation and whether the texture fits your instructions                                     |
| Ready-made or delivered meal           | Ingredient label, refrigeration, reheating and who receives it                                            |
| Juice, tea or supplements              | Ask the care team before using them as a treatment or alongside medicines                                 |

These are shopping questions, not recommendations that every recovering patient can eat these foods. Broth and sauces may contain ingredients not visible in the dish name. If a requirement cannot be confirmed, choose another option instead of relying on a translation guess.

## Arrange food access before the appointment

Choose accommodation with a practical lift/entrance, permitted delivery access and the storage or reheating facilities your meal plan needs. Confirm those facilities directly. Save one meal option near the accommodation and a backup that does not require you to walk across an unfamiliar neighborhood.

Keep food and delivery costs in your [trip budget](/tools/trip-budget). If a companion is helping, agree who orders and collects meals. A package called “recovery food” or “healing juice” does not establish medical supervision.

## Useful words, with limits

- **재료를 확인하고 싶어요.** — I would like to check the ingredients.
- **의료진의 식사 지침이 있어요.** — I have eating instructions from my care team.
- **이 재료가 들어가나요?** — Does this contain this ingredient? Show the ingredient in Korean.

These phrases support a conversation; they do not certify an allergy-safe kitchen. Carry a clear written ingredient list and use the [dietary-requirements guide](/blog/navigating-korean-restaurant-menus-with-allergies-translation-and-cross-contact) for more questions.

## If the plan stops working

Contact your treating team using the instructions they gave you. Do not use a porridge, tea or supplement to treat a complication. For emergency help, [call 119 for ambulance/fire or 112 for police](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=177&vcontsId=248145).

Next, compare the concrete support you need in the [recovery-accommodation checklist](/blog/airbnb-vs-recovery-hotels-for-medical-tourists).
