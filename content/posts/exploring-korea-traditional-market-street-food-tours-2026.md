Book a market food tour when you want help ordering, translation or someone to explain what you are tasting. Go on your own when you want to choose every dish and pause when you like. Either way, start with one market and check the food section rather than assuming every stall keeps the same hours.

## Decide what you want a guide to do

| Your priority | Check before choosing |
| --- | --- |
| **Tastings** | Ask what tastings are included and whether portions are individual or shared. For your own visit, name two dishes to look for and leave room to change your order. |
| **Language** | Check the tour language and how much time is spent with vendors. If you go on your own, save Korean dish names and your order. |
| **Food needs** | Tell the operator your requirements before payment and ask about broth, sauces and shared equipment. If nobody can confirm what you need, choose a different meal. |
| **Pace** | Ask for the walking distance, seats, toilets and step-free meeting point. Choose your own route if the tour cannot confirm the conditions. |

Read the tour’s meeting point, total price and cancellation terms before paying. Ask whether transport, drinks and extra food cost more. Compare the price including what you would actually order if you went alone.

## Two Seoul markets for different plans

**Gwangjang Market (광장시장)** is a useful first stop if you want to look for bindaetteok, the mung bean pancake. The [Seoul Tourism Organization’s Gwangjang listing](https://english.visitseoul.net/Tourist-Attractions-list1/Gwangjang-Market/ENP000286) identifies the food alley and its dishes. Ask which entrance or landmark the tour uses. If you are visiting independently, check the stall and order size before you sit.

For a self-guided stop, use the [Gwangjang and Tongin ordering guide](/blog/jeong-culture-in-markets-2026) to check portion prices and see how the two markets handle payment.

**Tongin Market (통인시장)** fits a lunch break near Gyeongbokgung. [The official Seoul tourism listing](https://english.visitseoul.net/attractions/Tongin-Market/ENP000281) gives the address as 18 Jahamun-ro 15-gil, Jongno-gu, and lists Gyeongbokgung Station Exit 2. It describes a lunchbox program where tokens can be used at participating stores. The listing’s café hours and closure text conflict, so confirm the café for your date before planning around the program. Regular market traders may keep their own hours.

## Make a fallback plan

Save the exact market entrance in your map app and carry a backup way to pay. If the stall you wanted is closed, choose another dish or a nearby meal instead of trying to fit in a second market. The [market ordering guide](/blog/ordering-food-traditional-markets-papago-2026) has phrases for portions and prices. Use the [Seoul street food price guide](/blog/seoul-street-food-prices-2026) to practice reading units and menu prices before you order.
