## Match the device to your written plan

A package name is a starting point for a question. Ask the clinic to identify the proposed procedure and the manufacturer and model of the device it plans to use. The [ZEISS device page](https://www.zeiss.com/meditec/en/products/refractive-lasers/femtosecond-laser-solutions/zeiss-visumax-800.html) names the VISUMAX 800 and SMILE pro and notes that approved labeling may vary by country. Use the manufacturer page to check the spelling of the device and procedure. Ask the Korean clinic to explain the labeling for its own device and proposed use.

| Record | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Written plan | Procedure name, which eye or eyes, named device model and the treating clinician. |
| Info and consent | The clinic can explain the plan and alternatives in a language you understand, and you can take time to review both documents. |
| Baseline exam | Date, eye measurements, and which reports or scans can be copied. |
| Care summary | If treatment goes ahead, the actual procedure and date, follow-up findings, and a contact for questions after you leave. |

## Resolve differences before signing

If the wording differs between the quote and consent form, ask the doctor to explain the discrepancy and update the documents to reflect the final plan. The [U.S. FDA’s LASIK checklist](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/lasik/lasik-surgery-checklist) illustrates why reviewing the patient booklet and consent together can help. Request material specific to the procedure offered.

## Ask what will reach your home eye doctor

The [FDA’s LASIK information card](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/lasik/fdas-lasik-program) gives a LASIK example of why earlier eye measurements can matter in later eye care. Ask your own eye doctor what to request for the procedure under discussion. Clarify separately what the clinic can issue in English, how to obtain original test reports, and whether you can collect them before departure. The [Korean medical records guide](/blog/korean-medical-records-getting-english-translations) explains the difference between a certificate, the underlying records and copies of images.
