## Turn “quickly” into a deadline you can use

The National Institute of Korean Language [defines 빨리](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/kor/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=57305) as doing something in a short time. In a request, 빨리 or the repeated 빨리빨리 signals speed. Ask when the work is needed and what the other person needs at that point. A draft for an internal review, a number for a meeting and a finished deliverable need different amounts of time.

| Clarify | Try saying |
| --- | --- |
| Deadline | **언제까지 필요하세요?** When do you need it? |
| Scope | **오늘은 초안만 보내면 될까요?** Would a draft today be enough? |
| Priority | **어떤 일부터 하면 될까요?** Which task should I do first? |
| Final copy | **최종본은 내일 오전에 드려도 될까요?** May I send the final version tomorrow morning? |

If the answer is 오늘 중으로, meaning sometime today, ask for a specific time if it affects another commitment. A question such as **오후 4시까지면 될까요?** means “Would 4 p.m. work?” Confirm the time zone if the conversation spans countries.

## Ask which task moves when two deadlines collide

The dictionary entry for [우선순위](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/kor/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=93473) gives the Korean word for priority. In a busy chat, name the two deliverables and ask whoever sets priorities which one should go first. For example, say you can send the cost table before the meeting if the slide revisions move to tomorrow morning. Ask who reviews the first version and whether it will be shared further. That choice determines which details you need to verify before sending it.

Once the colleague agrees, repeat the handoff in one message. **오늘 오후 4시까지 표만 먼저 보내고, 내일 오전 10시까지 최종본을 드리겠습니다.** means “I’ll send just the table by 4 p.m. today, then the final version by 10 a.m. tomorrow.” Use a date as well if the message may be read later. If someone changes the scope again, revise the handoff and confirm the new time.

## Handle requests that change your work hours

If a request reaches beyond your scheduled hours, ask when the recipient will use the work and whether the first version can go out during your next shift. If a team lead agrees to move another task or asks you to log extra time, save the decision with the date and hours.
