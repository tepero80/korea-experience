The small plates with a Korean meal are **반찬** (banchan). They can be as simple as seasoned vegetables or include fish, eggs or meat. Ask what the order includes before assuming every dish on the table is free to refill.

## Read the table and the menu together

| Word | What it helps you identify |
| --- | --- |
| **반찬** | Side dishes accompanying your order. Ask about an ingredient if the plate is unfamiliar. |
| **나물** | Often seasoned vegetables. Check seasoning or garnish if you have a dietary requirement. |
| **백반** | A set-meal format that often pairs rice and soup with sides. Check which main dish comes with the particular set. |

The [Korea Tourism Organization’s table guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=229195) explains banchan and namul. Its [baekban overview](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=189672) describes the set-meal structure with examples. Neither assigns the same sides to every restaurant.

If you want to put together a meal to take away, [find prepared banchan stalls in Seoul or Suwon](/blog/korean-side-dish-culture-understanding-banchan).

If you want the side dishes to come with a whole meal, [choose a Korean soup bowl and ask about rice](/blog/korean-soup-culture-gamjatang-galbitang-seolleongtang).

## Ask before taking a second serving

For an extra portion, point to the dish and ask **이 반찬 더 받을 수 있어요?** (May I have more of this side?). If you see **셀프** (self-service), check which things guests can take and whether the station is for your meal. Ask **추가 요금이 있어요?** (Does it cost extra?) if the answer is unclear.

If you avoid fish, shellfish, animal stock or another ingredient, ask about the seasoning as well as the visible food. Choose a different dish when staff cannot confirm what you need.
