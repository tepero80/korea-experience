**The Seoul Museum of Craft Art is a useful indoor stop in Anguk when you want to explore Korean craft without buying a general-admission ticket.** The museum lists free admission for its exhibitions, but a rented exhibition may charge. Individual visitors can generally explore freely; the separate Children's Museum in the Education Building requires an advance online reservation.

Checked against the official museum pages on **November 15, 2026**. A temporary late-opening schedule is in effect through **November 30, 2026**: Exhibition Building 1 stays open until 21:00 on Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday; Thursday remains open until 18:00, and Monday is closed. Buildings 2 and 3 and the Education Building (including the Children's Museum) do not share those late hours. From December 1 onward, check the current notice instead of assuming the extension continues. The museum says final entry is 30 minutes before closing.

## What to do there

Use the museum's current [exhibition list](https://craftmuseum.seoul.go.kr/main) to decide what interests you: the schedule can include permanent displays, temporary exhibitions and special programs. Treat a workshop as a separate timed event, not something automatically included with a gallery visit. Program participation can have its own audience, application window and reservation conditions.

For a low-pressure visit, pick one or two exhibitions you want to see, then leave room for a break. As a planning estimate, allow around **one to two hours** for a self-guided visit; the museum does not prescribe this duration, and a workshop or children's session adds separate time. During the late-opening period, do not assume every building or facility stays open until 21:00; the extension is specific to the listed building and dates.

## If you are visiting with a child

The Children's Museum is not simply a walk-in extension of the galleries. It is in the Education Building and the museum requires an online reservation even for an individual family. The current official page says it is free, runs four daily sessions between **10:00 and 17:50, Tuesday through Sunday**, and is designed for children ages **5–9**. Its 2026 eligibility wording says a child must be age 9 or younger, accompanied by an adult; verify the displayed session and age wording when booking because rules can change.

Reserve through the museum's official booking flow and check the confirmation for the building and session. The Children's Museum closes every Monday, January 1 and on other days designated by the museum. Do not assume that the general museum's public-holiday opening exception applies to the Children's Museum.

The museum lists its address as **4 Yulgok-ro 3-gil, Jongno-gu** and says Anguk Station on Line 3, Exit 1 is about 60 metres away. Copy the Korean address into your map app:

You can pair the visit with another nearby stop only after checking that venue's hours and the weather: central Seoul's short map distance does not mean every approach is sheltered.

## Rules and practical limits

- General gallery admission is free, but a rented exhibition can be ticketed.
- Entry closes 30 minutes before the relevant building closes. Archive Room hours and access differ from the galleries.
- The temporary late schedule ends November 30, 2026. It does not apply to all buildings; verify current hours before going in December or later.
- The Children's Museum has separate Monday closures and requires online advance booking.
- Food is not allowed in the museum. Keep voices low, switch phones to vibrate and do not touch or damage works.
- Flash, tripods and commercial photography are prohibited in the exhibition halls. Follow room signs and staff instructions.
- The official site carries the latest exhibition and reservation information; do not rely on an old event listing for workshop availability.
