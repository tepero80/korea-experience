Bring a one-page history to the consultation. Describe what you have noticed and when it changed. Include any breakouts, marks or changes in texture that you want the clinician to assess. You can describe these changes in your own words and ask which issues the clinician would address first.

## Prepare the history before you travel

The [Cornwall NHS acne referral guidance](https://rms.cornwall.nhs.uk/primary_care_clinical_referral_criteria/rms/primary_care_clinical_referral_criteria/dermatology/acne) asks clinicians to record prior treatment, its duration and whether there is scarring. It is a UK referral example. Use it to organize your notes, then check what your Korean clinic needs.

| Bring or write down | What makes it useful |
| --- | --- |
| Skin changes | When they started, which areas changed and what you want the clinician to assess. Existing dated photos are optional. |
| Previous care | Names and approximate dates of treatments. Note what you noticed afterward and any unwanted effects you remember. |
| Current medicines and products | List prescription medicines, supplements and skin products you use, and any known allergies or reactions. If you do not know a name, keep a photo of the original label. |
| Travel dates and language | Note your departure date, where you will be after the visit, and the language you need for the discussion and written summary. |

If the clinic wants records in advance, ask for its designated channel and what it needs to review. Bring the timeline even if a name or exact date is missing. Ask the clinician who manages a medicine to clarify any questions about it.

## Separate the consultation from a commitment

When booking, ask whether the first appointment can be a consultation only. During it, ask the clinician to explain their assessment, what can be discussed now and what would require an additional visit. If the dates are too tight to put a clear follow-up plan in place, you can take home the assessment and discuss next steps with your usual clinician.

## Arrange the handover before you leave

The [NHS checklist for medical care abroad](https://www.nhs.uk/using-the-nhs/healthcare-abroad/going-abroad-for-treatment/treatment-abroad-checklist/) highlights the transfer of medical notes and coordination of aftercare. Ask the Korean clinic for a summary you can share with your usual clinician. If you proceed with treatment, check who can review you before departure, how to contact the clinic afterward and where to seek care if an in-person assessment is needed. If a remote check is offered, [confirm how follow-up from outside Korea would work](/blog/telemedicine-follow-ups-2026) before relying on it.
