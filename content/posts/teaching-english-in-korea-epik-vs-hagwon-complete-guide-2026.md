## Choose which application to start

[EPIK’s application guide](https://www.epik.go.kr/web/epik/contents/How) sets out a process tied to the public-school calendar. Review the current intake and select an application route listed by the program. A successful interview leads to document review. The office of education makes the placement decision, so keep your plans flexible until the coordinator confirms the result.

A hagwon is a private academy. Use a named opening to ask who employs you, which branch you would work at and when that position starts. If a recruiter introduces you, ask to speak with the person who manages teachers at that campus. An advertisement for a city can still leave a substantial commute.

| EPIK | Private academy |
| --- | --- |
| **Timing.** Check the current intake and ask when placement and orientation will be confirmed. | **Timing.** Confirm the proposed start and what happens if employer paperwork runs late. |
| **Location.** Ask when you will know the education office, school and housing details. | **Location.** Get the branch address and check whether you will work at other sites. |
| **Teaching.** Ask who plans lessons, shares classroom duties and runs camps. | **Teaching.** Ask for the age groups, sample timetable and materials. |
| **Terms.** Ask which terms apply to the proposed placement. | **Terms.** Get total hours, pay dates, housing charges and leave in writing. |

## Ask for one ordinary week

[EPIK’s duties page](https://www.epik.go.kr/web/epik/contents/Duties) includes teaching alongside Korean teachers, preparing materials and helping with camps or other activities. Ask how the proposed school divides lesson planning and classroom duties and how you will get help if the routine changes.

At a hagwon interview, ask the manager to walk you through an ordinary week from arrival to departure. Include preparation, meetings, breaks, grading, parent messages and any lessons at another branch. A count of teaching periods leaves out the clerical and preparation time you need to compare offers. Ask for the building address and check the commute at the proposed finish time before deciding about housing.

## Move from interview to a written offer

Independent of the route, write down your limits for the commute, start date and time at work. Compare the terms in the [written job offer checklist](/blog/korean-work-culture-what-foreigners-need-to-know-2026) when you receive a contract. Ask for an amended copy if it differs from the interview. Treat a promised campus or apartment as provisional until the employer can identify it.

Use [EPIK’s current application document guide](https://www.epik.go.kr/web/epik/contents/ReqDocGen) for its own paperwork and check which items apply to your circumstances. For an academy, get the employer’s instructions for the specific role. When you have a written offer and know who is coordinating the immigration paperwork, use the [E-2 application guide](/blog/teaching-english-in-korea-e-2-visa-process) to identify what to verify with the visa office.
