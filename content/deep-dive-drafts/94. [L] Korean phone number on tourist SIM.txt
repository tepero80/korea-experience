2.  **Activation Lag**: Unlike the airport roaming desks where the staff hands you a working phone in 30 seconds, MVNOs sometimes have a 1-2 hour activation window where you're sitting in the arrival hall waiting for service to kick in.

## ## STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE

**Guide Title:** How to Get a Verified 010 Number SIM as a Tourist
**Total Time:** 20 Minutes (plus pre-order time)
**Difficulty:** Medium

### Step 1: Pre-Order Before You Fly
Don't wait until you land. Go to a site like **Trazy**, **Creatrip**, or the direct sites for **Chingu Mobile** or **Woori Mobile** about a week before your trip. Select a "Prepaid SIM with Voice/Text + Data" (ensure it says 010 number included).
> **Pro Tip:** If you're a student, look for "University Plan" promos on Woori Mobile; they often throw in free extra months or discounts if you show an acceptance letter.

### Step 2: Locate the Pick-Up Counter
Upon landing at Incheon (T1 or T2), clear customs and head to the arrival hall. If you chose a major carrier (SKT/KT), their desks are everywhere. If you chose Chingu/Woori, they usually have specific meeting spots or third-party counters (like a bookstore or travel center) listed on your voucher.
> **Pro Tip:** Take a screenshot of the pick-up map *before* you fly. Incheon WiFi is fast, but you don't want to be fumbling with loading a webpage while dragging luggage.

### Step 3: The Facial Scan (The 2026 Update)
Hand over your passport and your reservation voucher. As of March 2026, the staff will ask you to look into a camera or tablet. This scans your face to match it against your passport photo in real-time. It's mandatory to prevent voice phishing scams.
> **Pro Tip:** Don't wear a mask, hat, or heavy sunglasses during this part. If the scan fails multiple times, it triggers a manual review which takes way longer.

### Step 4: Test Immediately
Do not leave the counter until you see LTE/5G bars and receive the "Welcome" text message. Try calling the person next to you or sending a text to your home number.
> **Pro Tip:** If you have an iPhone, check your "Settings > Cellular" to make sure the new line is set to "Primary" for data and voice, so you don't accidentally roam on your home SIM.

Some cheap data-only eSIMs give you a number starting with **012**. ## ## FAQ

### Q1: Can I use Samsung Pay or Apple Pay with a tourist SIM?
Not for local transport cards usually. While Apple Pay is slowly rolling out in Korea, the "transport card" function (T-money) on phones typically requires a SIM linked to a carrier with "Identity Verification" (which tourists can't do). Stick to a physical **T-money** or **Climate Card** for the subway.

### Q2: Will my tourist number expire if I leave Korea?
Yes. Most prepaid SIMs are valid only for the duration you purchased (e.g., 30 days). If you leave and don't top up, the number is recycled quickly (usually within a few days to weeks of expiration). You cannot "park" a number easily without an ARC.

### Q3: Why is the shop asking to scan my face? Is that normal?
As of late 2025/early 2026, yes. The South Korean government implemented mandatory facial recognition for all new mobile activations to crack down on "voice phishing" scams. It matches your live face to your passport photo. It’s a legal requirement, not a scam.

### Q4: Can I buy a SIM at a convenience store (CU/GS25)?
Yes, but it's harder. Convenience store SIMs often require you to self-register online by uploading passport photos, and the instructions are sometimes only in Korean. It's much safer and easier to buy from a dedicated roaming center at the airport or a shop like Chingu Mobile.

### Q5: Does a tourist SIM work for "Adult Verification" on YouTube/Naver?
No. Age verification in Korea uses the "PASS" system or carrier billing verification, which is linked to your Resident Registration Number (ARC). A passport-based prepaid SIM cannot verify your age online, so you'll be blocked from age-restricted content.

## ## ARTICLE BODY

### Section 1: Answer the Main Question First!

So, you're asking the million-won question: "Can I get a Korean phone number on a tourist SIM?" The short answer is **YES**, but there are some strings attached that you really need to understand before you land.

Here is the deal: You can easily buy a prepaid SIM card (or eSIM) that comes with a local **010** number. You absolutely *should* do this instead of getting a data-only card. Why? Because without a local number, you are basically playing "Korea on Hard Mode." You won't be able to queue up at popular restaurants (which use tablet waiting systems), you might struggle with food delivery drivers who call you for directions, and you can't receive 2FA texts for certain local services.

However—and this is huge—getting a number on a tourist SIM (registered with your **passport**) is NOT the same as having a "verified" phone number. In Korea, a phone number is like a digital SSN. Until you have an Alien Registration Card (ARC) and link your number to it, your "tourist 010 number" is mostly just a "burner" phone. You can call and text, but you cannot use it for **Identity Verification (본인인증)**. That means no setting up online banking, no signing up for the full version of delivery apps, and no buying concert tickets on Korean sites that require identity checks. But for a 2-week trip? It's exactly what you need.

### Section 2: The Background Story

To understand why getting a SIM card in Korea feels like applying for a security clearance, we have to look at the history. Back in the early 2010s, Korea had a massive problem with "Voice Phishing" and burner phones used for crimes. The government cracked down hard. They introduced the "Real Name Verification System," making it illegal to own a SIM card that isn't tied to a verified identity.

Fast forward to **2026**, and things have gotten even stricter. As of December 2025, the Ministry of Science and ICT piloted a new rule that became mandatory in March 2026: **Facial Recognition**.

That's right. Because criminals were finding ways to activate SIMs with stolen passport scans, the government now requires a **live facial scan** when you activate a new SIM card. Whether you are at Incheon Airport or a 7-Eleven in Hongdae, the activation process now involves a tablet scanning your face to match it against your passport photo in real-time. It feels a bit sci-fi, but it’s the new reality. This security paranoia is also why your tourist SIM dies the moment your visa expires—everything is tightly linked to immigration data.

### Section 3: Breaking Down Your Options

Now that you know the rules, let's look at how to actually buy one. You essentially have three paths, and the price difference is massive.

**Option A: The "Big 3" Roaming Desks (SKT, KT, LG U+)**
If money is no object and you just want convenience, this is it. These are the official carrier desks right in the arrival hall.
*   **The Vibe:** Reliable, fast, English-speaking staff.
*   **The Cost:** Expensive. Expect to pay around **₩71,500 ($53)** for a 30-day unlimited data plan with a number.
*   **Best For:** Business travelers or short-term tourists who don't care about saving $20.

**Option B: The "Budget Kings" (Chingu Mobile / Woori Mobile)**
These are Mobile Virtual Network Operators (MVNOs). They use the exact same towers (usually KT or SKT networks) but charge way less.
*   **The Vibe:** Scrappy but efficient. You usually pre-order these online (via Trazy/Creatrip) and pick them up at a specific counter or bookstore at the airport.
*   **The Cost:** Much better. A 30-day plan here is often around **₩35,000–₩40,000 ($26–$30)**. That's nearly half price.
*   **Best For:** Students, budget travelers, or anyone staying longer than a week.

**Option C: Data-Only eSIMs (Airalo, Nomad, etc.)**
These are super convenient because you can install them while still in your home country.
*   **The Catch:** Most of these are **DATA ONLY**. No phone number.
*   **The Problem:** You save money, but then you arrive at "London Bagel Museum," realize you need a Korean number to join the digital queue, and cry silent tears of regret. Only choose this if you have a friend with a Korean number who can queue for you.

### Section 4: The Complete How-To

Let’s walk through the exact process for **2026**, assuming you want the smart option (Option B: MVNO with a number).

**Step 1: Pre-Order Online**
About 3-4 days before you fly, go to **Trazy** or **Creatrip**. Search for "Chingu Mobile" or "Woori Mobile SIM." Select the plan (e.g., "30 Days + 010 Number"). You will pay upfront. This saves you time and ensures they have stock.

**Step 2: Arrival & The Facial Scan**
Land at Incheon. Clear immigration. Head to the arrival hall. Check your voucher for the location—it's often a counter near a bookstore or a specific "Roaming Center."
*   **The New Step:** When you hand over your passport, the staff will point a camera at you. Smile (or don't). The system runs a biometric check to ensure you are the person in the passport. This is the **2026 regulation** in action.

**Step 3: Activation & "The Wait"**
If you go with the Big 3 (SKT/KT), activation is instant. If you go with Chingu/Woori, there might be a "batch activation" delay. Sometimes the SIM works instantly; sometimes they tell you, "It will work in 1 hour."
*   **Practical Advice:** Use the airport WiFi to message your family, grab a coffee at Paris Baguette, and wait for the bars to appear on your phone before you hop on the AREX train.

**Step 4: Setting Up Apps**
Once your number is live (e.g., 010-1234-5678), screenshot it.
*   **Download:** Naver Map (Google Maps is trash here), KakaoTalk (messaging), and **CatchTable Global** (for restaurants).
*   **KakaoTaxi:** Set this up immediately. You don't need a Korean credit card—just select "Pay to Driver" in the payment settings.

### Section 5: The Stuff Nobody Tells You

Here are the "Pro Tips" that usually take a few failed attempts to learn.

**The "No-ARC" Wall is Real.**
Even with your shiny new 010 number, you are a second-class digital citizen. You cannot order delivery on the main "Yogiyo" or "Baemin" apps easily because they require "Identity Verification" (linking your number to a Resident ID).
*   **The Hack:** Use **Shuttle Delivery** (fully English, takes foreign cards) or **Uber Eats** (which operates as **Uber/UT** for taxis but sometimes has food options). Or, use the "Guest Order" function on Baemin if you can read a bit of Korean, but you'll likely need a Korean card.

**The Restaurant Queue Hack.**
Many trendy spots use a tablet at the door where you input your phone number to queue.
*   **Scenario:** You input your new 010 tourist number.
*   **Critical Step:** Watch your phone like a hawk. Some systems send a KakaoTalk message; others send a regular SMS. If you miss the notification, they skip you instantly. If you *don't* have a number, some friendly staff might let you input a dummy number, but don't count on it.

**The Recycling of Numbers.**
Korean phone numbers are recycled aggressively. You might get a SIM and immediately start receiving texts for "Mr. Kim's dental appointment." It's annoying but normal. Just ignore them. Also, once your SIM expires (e.g., after 30 days), if you don't top up within 3-7 days, that number is *gone*. You can't get it back. If you plan to return to Korea, ask the provider about "Parking" the number (keeping it alive for a small fee), though this is rare for tourist SIMs.
