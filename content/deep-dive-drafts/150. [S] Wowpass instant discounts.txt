## FAQ

### Q1: Can I use WOWPASS outside of Seoul, like in Busan or Jeju?
Yes, absolutely! The shopping side of the WOWPASS functions like a standard Korean debit card, so it is accepted anywhere in the country that takes card payments (which is 99% of businesses in Korea) [cite: 2, 10]. The T-money transit side also works nationwide on buses, subways, and taxis in Busan, Jeju, Daegu, and beyond [cite: 23, 25, 29]. 

### Q2: Is the Climate Card better than WOWPASS if I'm using the subway a lot?
It really depends on your trip. The Climate Card gives you unlimited transit rides in Seoul for a flat fee (like 15,000 KRW for 5 days) [cite: 7, 22, 28]. If you are riding the subway 5+ times a day, the Climate Card saves you money [cite: 22]. However, the Climate Card cannot be used to buy things in stores, and it doesn't work outside of Seoul [cite: 7, 23, 25]. Most savvy travelers actually get *both*—a Climate Card for intense subway days, and a WOWPASS for shopping, dining, and emergencies [cite: 7, 31].

### Q3: Why can't I just use my home country's Visa, Mastercard, or Apple Pay?
You can try, but you will quickly run into frustrating roadblocks. Many smaller Korean merchants, subway reload machines, and specific payment terminals do not accept foreign-issued credit cards, and Apple Pay/Google Pay is still rarely accepted outside of major convenience stores [cite: 17, 23, 30]. Plus, using your home card often incurs foreign transaction fees [cite: 9, 10]. WOWPASS acts as a local Korean card, so it is accepted everywhere with zero surprise fees [cite: 2, 9]. 

### Q4: How exactly do the Olive Young cashback e-vouchers work?
It is surprisingly simple. You go into the WOWPASS app and buy a digital "Discount e-Voucher" for Olive Young (they come in amounts like 30k, 50k, 100k) [cite: 16]. You pay for this voucher using your WOWPASS balance. Instantly, a percentage of that cost (often 4% to 5%) is deposited back into your WOWPASS wallet [cite: 2, 16]. At the Olive Young checkout, you just show the cashier the barcode of your e-voucher on your phone screen to pay [cite: 16, 32]. 

### Q5: What happens to my leftover money when I leave Korea?
If you have leftover shopping balance, you can go to any WOWPASS kiosk (except the ones inside Incheon Airport past the gates) and withdraw it as physical Korean Won cash [cite: 2, 10, 11, 29]. There is a 1,000 KRW fee per withdrawal, and the machine only dispenses 10,000 and 50,000 won notes [cite: 9, 11, 33]. You cannot refund your T-money transit balance at the kiosk; for that, you have to go to a convenience store and ask the cashier for a T-money refund (which has a tiny 500 KRW fee) [cite: 1, 8]. Alternatively, since the card is valid for 6 years, you can just keep it for your next trip! [cite: 1, 2].

### The Stuff Nobody Tells You

Now we get to the juicy stuff—the pitfalls, the hacks, and the mechanics that trip up 90% of first-time visitors. 

First and foremost, we need to talk about **The Two-Wallet Trap**. I cannot stress this enough: your WOWPASS card is actually two completely separate financial ecosystems trapped inside one piece of plastic [cite: 1, 18]. 
Wallet A is your "Shopping Balance." This is the money you loaded at the orange kiosk using foreign currency [cite: 1, 18]. You use this by inserting the chip into a credit card reader at cafes, restaurants, and shops [cite: 1]. 
Wallet B is your "T-money Transit Balance." This is the chip used to beep your way onto subways and buses [cite: 1, 18]. 

If you put $100 into the orange kiosk, it *all* goes into Wallet A (Shopping) [cite: 18]. If you walk straight to the subway turnstile and tap your card, it will aggressively beep at you and flash a red light because Wallet B (Transit) is empty [cite: 1, 18]. To ride the subway, you must put money into the T-money balance [cite: 1, 8]. Historically, you had to go to a convenience store, pull out physical Korean Won cash, hand it to the cashier, and say "T-money charge, please" [cite: 1, 10]. 

However, in 2025, WOWPASS released an app update that changed the game. You can now use your phone's NFC reader to transfer money from your Shopping balance to your Transit balance [cite: 2, 8, 30]. You simply open the WOWPASS app, go to the T-money tab, hit "Top-Up," and literally hold your physical card to the back of your smartphone [cite: 2, 8]. The app reads the card, deducts money from your shopping balance, and beams it into the T-money chip [cite: 2, 8, 18]. Just remember, if you have an Android, you must go into your phone settings and ensure NFC is turned on to "Read/Write" mode, or it won't work [cite: 2].

Secondly, let's talk about **Street Food and Bank Transfers**. You are in Gwangjang Market eating mung bean pancakes, and the sweet elderly lady points to a piece of cardboard with a Korean bank account number written on it. She doesn't have a credit card machine. If you don't have cash, you are usually out of luck. But the WOWPASS app has a "Send to Korean Bank Account" feature [cite: 2, 30, 31]. You just open the app, type in her account number and the bank name, and you can instantly wire money from your WOWPASS balance directly into her Korean bank account for a tiny 500 KRW fee [cite: 2, 31]. It is an absolute superpower for exploring traditional markets without carrying paper money.

Finally, the **Withdrawal Catch**. If it is your last day in Korea and you have 80,000 KRW left on your card, you can go to a WOWPASS kiosk and withdraw it as physical cash [cite: 2, 11]. But be aware: there is a 1,000 KRW fee for every cash withdrawal, the machine only gives out 10,000 and 50,000 won notes (so you can't withdraw exactly 84,300 KRW, the remaining 4,300 will stay on the card), and you cannot withdraw money at the machines located past the security gates at Incheon Airport [cite: 1, 2, 10, 11]. Furthermore, you *cannot* get a refund for your T-money transit balance at the orange kiosks; you have to go to a convenience store cashier to refund your transit balance [cite: 1, 2, 8].
