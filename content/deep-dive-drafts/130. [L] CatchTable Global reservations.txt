## FAQ

### Q1: Can I use the regular Korean CatchTable app if I just use a translation tool?
No, unfortunately you can't. The regular Korean CatchTable app requires strict identity verification linked to a Korean resident registration number (ARC) and a local telecommunications contract [cite: 2, 13]. Even if you translate the text, you physically cannot bypass the login screen without those credentials. 

### Q2: What happens if I am running late for my reservation?
You need to be very careful here. Most Korean fine dining restaurants and popular cafes operate on incredibly tight schedules and offer a strict 15-minute grace period [cite: 5, 6]. If you arrive 16 minutes late, they have the right to cancel your reservation entirely and legally charge you the maximum no-show penalty. If you are stuck in Seoul traffic, try calling the restaurant directly, but don't count on them holding the table.

### Q3: Why can't I find a specific famous restaurant on the Global app?
While CatchTable Global is expanding rapidly, it only has about 2,000 to 3,000 verified listings compared to the 8,000+ on the local version [cite: 1, 9]. Some smaller neighborhood spots or highly exclusive places choose not to deal with foreign credit card gateways. If a place isn't on the app, check their official Instagram—many chefs allow tourists to book via Direct Message (DM) as a workaround [cite: 1, 12].

### Q4: Are the deposit fees an extra charge on top of my meal?
No, the deposit is simply a hold to guarantee that you will actually show up, preventing costly "no-shows" that hurt the restaurant's bottom line [cite: 5, 16]. When you finish your meal, the restaurant will either deduct the deposit amount from your total bill, or they will charge you the full price of the meal and refund the deposit back to your card within 3 to 5 business days [cite: 6, 10].

### Q5: Can I get SMS text alerts for my waitlist status instead of email?
Currently, CatchTable Global only sends notifications via email or push notifications directly through the app [cite: 1, 11]. If you want traditional SMS or KakaoTalk text alerts, you would need a local Korean "010" phone number and the local version of the app. Because of this, it is highly recommended that you keep the app open and periodically refresh your waitlist status if you are waiting for a table.

### The Background Story  
How did we even get to this point where dining out requires a specialized global app and credit card deposits? Let me tell you, the evolution of Seoul's dining scene over the past few years has been nothing short of a rollercoaster. Make no mistake, South Korea has always had an incredible food culture, but the logistics of actually getting a seat used to be chaos. 

Back in the day, if you wanted to eat somewhere famous, you either had to call a busy landline (and hope someone spoke English) or physically stand in line on the street with a clipboard [cite: 1, 2]. By 2017, a company called WAD Corp launched the original CatchTable to digitize this messy system [cite: 1, 2]. It was a massive hit among locals. But then the pandemic hit, and something fascinating happened: "waiting culture" became a massive trend in Korea. It became a flex, a status symbol, to wait three hours for a pastry at a trendy cafe. Tech giants rolled out digital kiosks everywhere, but to prevent scalpers from stealing reservations, they introduced "Real Name Verification," linking all bookings to government IDs [cite: 2]. 

While this protected the locals, it completely locked out the massive wave of international tourists returning to Korea. Travelers were showing up to incredible restaurants only to be turned away because they didn't have a Korean ID to use the kiosk. Recognizing this massive missed opportunity, CatchTable launched its Global version in 2023, specifically tailored for tourists [cite: 1, 2]. 

But the story gets spicier. The restaurant boom brought a dark side: the "no-show" crisis. Diners, including tourists, would book five different high-end omakase restaurants for the same night, decide which one they felt like attending at the last minute, and completely ghost the other four. Because ingredients for omakase and fine dining are pre-ordered and incredibly expensive, small restaurant owners were losing millions of won. It became a national scandal. 

To fix this, the Fair Trade Commission (FTC) stepped in with a heavy hand. In late 2025, moving into 2026, the government officially raised the legal cap on cancellation penalties from a mere 10% to a staggering 40% for fine-dining and reservation-based establishments [cite: 3, 4]. They basically said, "We will make it easy for anyone in the world to book these restaurants, but if you flake, you are going to pay for it." This is why CatchTable Global now requires your credit card upfront [cite: 1, 2]. It is a system built on mutual trust: they guarantee your seat, and you guarantee you will actually show up.
