Navigating South Korea's KTX (Korea Train Express) high-speed rail network is straightforward once you understand how seating layouts and luggage rules operate. Unlike airplane travel, high-speed rail relies heavily on self-management at boarding gates and platform areas. International travelers can streamline their journeys by understanding train models, carriage configurations, and where to secure belongings.

## KTX Luggage Policies and Onboard Storage

Passengers are generally permitted up to two pieces of luggage that they can carry independently. For larger or checked-style luggage, the standard size guideline is capped at 158 cm (length + width + height) with a 20 kg weight limit per piece. For standard carry-on items, a size of approximately 55 × 40 × 20 cm with a 10 kg limit is recommended. 

Because KTX does not feature formal check-in counters or strict scale-weighing processes at boarding gates, enforcement focuses primarily on passenger self-management. However, train staff can intervene if oversized items block aisles or emergency exits.

### Where to Store Bags Onboard
*   **Overhead Racks:** Ideal for smaller backpacks, daypacks, and medium bags up to roughly 24 inches.
*   **End-of-Car Luggage Racks:** Dedicated racks located near the doors of the train car, best suited for oversized or large suitcases ranging from 28 to 32 inches.

*Note on Availability:* Storage spaces near train car doors and overhead shelves operate strictly on a first-come, first-served basis. They cannot be reserved in advance. End-of-car rack space fills up rapidly during peak travel times, such as weekends, holidays, and high-demand routes like Seoul to Busan. Arriving at the platform 10 to 15 minutes before departure is strongly recommended for travelers with bulky bags.

### Luggage Security and Station Alternatives
Traveler consensus indicates that South Korea maintains an exceptionally low rate of opportunistic theft. This makes it common practice to store large suitcases in the end-of-car corridor racks out of one's immediate line of sight from the seat. 

For travelers who prefer not to bring heavy bags onto the train, station storage lockers (such as T-Locker) are widely available at major transportation hubs like Seoul Station. Fees vary depending on the box size and rental duration.

## KTX Train Models and Seat Layouts

Seat configurations, power access, and travel directions vary significantly depending on which KTX train model you board:

*   **Original KTX (KTX-1):** Features fixed seating arrangements where rows face each other, meaning some seats face backward and others face forward. Passengers cannot rotate these seats. Power outlets are restricted primarily to specific window seats.
*   **KTX-Sancheon & KTX-Eum:** Newer, modern train models where all seats face the direction of travel. These models are equipped with individual power outlets and USB ports at every seat row.

### Standard Class vs. First Class
*   **Standard Class:** Typically arranged in a comfortable 2-2 layout.
*   **First Class:** Features a more spacious 1-2 layout with wider reclining seats and complimentary amenities provided during the journey.

## Operational Reminders for Foreign Travelers

When planning a KTX journey, keep these operational factors in mind:
*   Official ticketing rules, passport or identity check requirements for foreigners using rail passes (such as the Korea Rail Pass / KR Pass), and formal tariff or indemnification rules for lost baggage via Korail should always be verified directly through official operator channels.
*   Always double-check your platform assignment and car number (often marked on your ticket or digital reservation app) prior to boarding, as high-speed trains maintain strict, brief station stop times.
