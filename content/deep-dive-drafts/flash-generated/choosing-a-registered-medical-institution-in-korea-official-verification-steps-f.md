Choosing a medical institution in South Korea as an international visitor requires understanding the country's specific legal framework for healthcare tourism. Because translation discrepancies, commercial broker listings, and unverified online reviews can complicate the selection process, checking official government databases is the most reliable way to confirm a clinic or facilitator's legitimacy. 

International patient care and facility registration are regulated under the *Act on Support for Overseas Expansion of Healthcare System and Attraction of International Patients*, which took effect on June 23, 2016. Administration and registration oversight are managed through the Korea Health Industry Development Institute (KHIDI) alongside local and municipal governments, such as the Seoul Metropolitan Government.

---

## Medical License vs. Foreign Patient Attraction Registration

A common point of confusion for visitors is the difference between a standard local medical practice license and a specialized foreign patient attraction registration. 

Every legal medical clinic operating in the country must hold a baseline medical license under the Korean *Medical Service Act*. However, a **Foreign Patient Attraction Registration** is a separate, mandatory legal requirement specifically for institutions that actively recruit, market to, and coordinate care for international patients. 

* **Standard License:** Authorizes a doctor or clinic to treat domestic patients within the general parameters of Korean healthcare law.
* **Attraction Registration:** Mandates strict statutory requirements, including mandatory malpractice insurance coverage tiers (such as a minimum of ₩100 million for clinics) to protect international patients.

While walk-in foreign visitors can technically receive treatment at any licensed clinic, commercial brokers, agencies, and clinics engaging in active overseas marketing must hold official attraction registration. Operating as a foreign patient attraction agency without this registration is illegal and punishable by up to 3 years of imprisonment or fines up to ₩30 million. Due to post-pandemic business changes, database entries for registered entities fluctuate; verifying active status via official registries is essential.

## Core Official Verification Tools

To independently verify a clinic, practitioner, or medical device, visitors can utilize three main free government databases:

| Database / Agency | Primary Function | What You Can Verify |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **KHIDI Foreign Patient Attraction Information System / Medical Korea Registry** | Institution & Agency Lookup | Whether a hospital, clinic, or medical tourism agency is officially registered to attract foreign patients (searchable by business registration number, institution name, or region). |
| **HIRA** (Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service - hira.or.kr) | Practitioner Credentialing | Individual physician licensing credentials and legal medical practice authorization. |
| **MFDS** (Ministry of Food and Drug Safety - mfds.go.kr) | Product & Technology Safety | Official permits and approvals for medical devices used in treatments. |

Additionally, the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) and its Medical Tourism Business Center provide official support pathways, lists of reliable certified entities, and workspace registration tools via platforms like TOURAZ.

## How to Perform a Reverse Lookup on KHIDI

When evaluating a clinic or agency, searching the official registries can sometimes present challenges if you only have partial names, English translations, or promotional brand names rather than official corporate entities. Follow these steps to perform an effective verification check:

1. **Obtain the Official Business Registration Number:** Ask the clinic or facilitator for their official Korean business registration number (사업자등록번호). This 10-digit number eliminates translation ambiguities caused by similar English clinic names.
2. **Access the Medical Korea Registry / KHIDI Portal:** Navigate to the official foreign patient attraction database system. 
3. **Filter by Region and Name:** Enter the exact legal name or business registration number. If searching by name, check both the Korean spelling (if available) and the official English trading name registered with local authorities.
4. **Confirm Active Status:** Ensure the registry lists the entity as actively maintained rather than closed, suspended, or canceled.

## Recourse, Insurance Mandates, and Dispute Mechanisms

Registered medical institutions and certified attraction agencies are bound by statutory requirements that offer structural protections to international visitors. 

* **Mandatory Insurance:** Registered entities must maintain professional liability or medical malpractice insurance. This provides a formal framework for compensation if a medical complication arises due to negligence.
* **Unregistered Facilitators:** Working with unregistered intermediaries strips away these statutory safety nets, leaving patients with limited legal recourse if a dispute occurs regarding brokerage fees, botched coordination, or substandard care.
* **Formal Dispute Resolution:** If a serious conflict or medical dispute arises with a registered provider, patients can turn to the **Korea Medical Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Agency (K-MEDI)**, which handles formal patient-clinician disputes, statutory mediation, and arbitration.
* **Regulatory Violations:** Regulatory non-compliance and administrative breaches by registered entities can be investigated directly through the **Ministry of Health and Welfare (MOHW)**.

*Note: For complex legal disputes or financial claims, visitors should consult formal legal counsel specializing in Korean medical law.*
