Under Article 21 of the South Korean Medical Service Act, patients hold a clear statutory right to inspect or obtain copies of their own medical records, and medical institutions cannot refuse these requests without justifiable grounds. Whether you are seeking documentation for overseas insurance claims, continued medical care back home, or personal records following a medical procedure in Seoul, navigating South Korea's administrative framework requires understanding specific legal rights, strict privacy controls, regulated fee structures, and language realties.

## Legal Rights and Statutory Privacy Controls

The South Korean Medical Service Act balances patient access rights with strict confidentiality safeguards. While patients maintain a direct legal right to their own data, third-party disclosure is heavily regulated.

*   **Patient Access:** Medical facilities are legally mandated to fulfill requests by patients to view or copy their files unless a specific, justifiable legal or clinical exception applies. Individual clinics may implement standard in-person verification steps or specific document workflows based on their internal operational setup.
*   **Third-Party Restrictions:** Article 21 strictly prohibits medical institutions from disclosing medical records or providing copies to third parties—including employers, insurance providers, or family members—without the patient's explicit prior written consent accompanied by required verification documents. Facilities explicitly do not release sensitive personal medical details or transmit copies over the phone, email, or fax due to these privacy regulations.

## Issuance Procedures and Required Documentation

Obtaining physical or digital copies of your health records involves specific administrative protocols at the hospital level. 

*   **In-Person Applications:** Patients must generally apply in person at the hospital's medical records desk or International Healthcare Center. You must present valid photo identification, such as a passport or an Alien Registration Card (ARC).
*   **Representative Applications:** If a third-party representative must apply on your behalf, they are required to submit original handwritten consent forms, powers of attorney, and official proof of relationship documents issued within the last six months. 
*   **Special Protocols for Mental Health Records:** For neuropsychiatry and mental health records, Korean regulations typically require a direct consultation and formal approval or request from the attending physician before copies or summaries can be issued.

## Statutory Fee Ceilings for Copies and Certificates

To prevent arbitrary pricing, the Ministry of Health and Welfare sets official fee ceilings for medical record duplication and certificates. While individual clinics determine their exact pricing, they cannot exceed these statutory maximum caps and must post any fee changes publicly in advance.

*   **Paper Records:** Capped at 1,000 KRW per page for the first 5 pages, and 100 KRW per page from the 6th page onward.
*   **Digital Imaging:** Capped at 5,000 KRW for physical film, 10,000 KRW for a CD, and 20,000 KRW for a DVD.
*   **Medical Certificates:** An English-language general medical certificate has a fee ceiling set at 20,000 KRW.

## Translation and Language Realities

Language differences present a practical hurdle for international patients. Most local clinics and hospitals generate foundational medical records, nursing notes, and clinical charts natively in Korean. 

While major university hospitals featuring international healthcare centers can issue specific English documents—such as general medical certificates or visit summaries—upon direct request to the doctor during a consultation, comprehensive chart records frequently require external professional certified medical translation. Relying solely on basic hospital printouts may prove insufficient for foreign insurance reimbursement or domestic physicians abroad, making independent certified translation services a common necessity.
