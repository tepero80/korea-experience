## KEY TAKEAWAYS

**1.** **The short answer is: YES, you absolutely need a reservation.**
If you want to reach the actual summit (Baengnokdam Crater), you must book a slot for the Seongpanak or Gwaneumsa trails. No reservation equals no hiking, and they are strict about checking QR codes at the entrance. However, if you just want a scenic hike without hitting the absolute peak, trails like Yeongsil or Eorimok don't require any booking at all.

**2.** **The magic number is 1,500.**
That is the total number of hikers allowed to summit per day (1,000 on the Seongpanak side and only 500 on the Gwaneumsa side). In a country where hiking is practically a national sport, these slots disappear faster than K-pop concert tickets during peak season.

**3.** **Actionable advice: Book exactly one month in advance.**
Reservations open at 9:00 AM KST on the first day of the month *prior* to your hike. So, if you want to hike in October, you need to be on the website on September 1st. And if you are a foreigner without a Korean SIM card, use the email verification option—but check your spam folder immediately, as the system is notoriously finicky.

## PROS AND CONS

### Pros (of the recommended option: Booking Gwaneumsa)
1.  **The Views are Insane**: Look, Seongpanak is easier, but it's basically staring at trees for 4 hours. Gwaneumsa offers dramatic cliffs, suspension bridges, and that "Jurassic Park" vibe you came to Jeju for.
2.  **Less Crowded**: Because the daily limit is capped at 500 (vs 1,000 for Seongpanak), you feel less like you're in a conga line of hikers and more like you're in nature.
3.  **Guaranteed Entry**: Once you have that QR code, you are golden. There's no waking up at 4 AM to beat a queue; your spot is secured.

### Cons (be honest!)
1.  **The "Stairway to Heaven" is Real**: Gwaneumsa is brutal on the knees. It is endless stairs. If you have bad joints, you might regret this choice halfway down.
2.  **Booking Anxiety**: Trying to snag one of only 500 spots as a foreigner dealing with a Korean website verification system can be incredibly stressful.
3.  **Transport is Tricky**: While getting *to* the trailhead is fine, catching a taxi *back* from Gwaneumsa can be a nightmare because it's more isolated than Seongpanak.

## STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE

**Guide Title:** How to Master the Hallasan Reservation System (Foreigner Edition)
**Total Time:** 15-20 Minutes (plus waiting for email)
**Difficulty:** Medium (The website can be finicky)

### Step 1: Navigate to the Official Portal
Go to the official Hallasan National Park reservation site (visithalla.jeju.go.kr). Do not use third-party tour sites unless you are paying them to do this for you.
> **Pro Tip:** Use a desktop computer (Chrome browser) rather than a mobile phone if possible. The pop-up translation plugins work better on desktop, and the calendar interface is less likely to glitch out.

### Step 2: Select "Foreigner" and Your Trail
Click the "Reservation" button. You will likely see a prompt for "Kakao Login" or "Phone Verification." ignore those if you don't have a Korean ARC. Look for the tab or small link often labeled "Foreigner" or "Email Authentication." Select your desired trail (Seongpanak or Gwaneumsa) and date.
> **Pro Tip:** If the date is greyed out, it's fully booked. Refresh the page occasionally; people cancel all the time, especially 2-3 days before the date due to weather forecasts.

### Step 3: The Email Verification Dance
Enter your email address to receive a verification code. This is where 90% of tourists panic. The email might take 5 minutes, or it might go straight to spam.
> **Pro Tip:** Use a Gmail address. Hotmail and Yahoo often block these automated Korean government emails entirely. If you don't get the code in 10 minutes, try a different email address. Do not close the browser window while waiting!

### Step 4: Input Details and Save the QR Code
Once verified, enter your name *exactly* as it appears on your passport. You do not need to pay anything. Once confirmed, a QR code will be generated. Screenshot this immediately. Print it out if you can. Do not rely on finding the email later when you are on the mountain with spotty 5G.
> **Pro Tip:** Forward the confirmation email to a travel partner or save the screenshot in your "Favorites" album so you aren't fumbling at the gate while a line of impatient hikers forms behind you.

## WARNINGS AND TIPS

### 💡 Pro Tip
**Bring Your Own Ramen Water!**
This is huge. Years ago, you could buy instant cup noodles and hot water at the shelters (Jindallaebat). **You cannot do this anymore.** They stopped selling food to protect the environment. You must bring your own cup noodles and a high-quality thermos with hot water if you want that iconic "Ramen at the summit" photo.

### ⚠️ Warning
**The Checkpoint Cut-off Time is Strict**
There are checkpoints (Azalea Field Shelter for Seongpanak; Samgakbong Shelter for Gwaneumsa) that you *must* pass by a certain time (usually 12:00 PM or 12:30 PM depending on the season). If you arrive at 12:31 PM, the ranger *will* turn you around. No exceptions. Start hiking by 7:00 AM to be safe.

### 📱 No Korean Phone/ARC? Here's What To Do
This is the number one panic point for travelers. If the website's email verification is simply not working (it happens):
1.  **Ask your Hotel Concierge:** They can often book it using their own local number or help you navigate the Korean interface.
2.  **Call 1330:** This is the Korea Travel Hotline. They have English speakers who can sometimes guide you or troubleshoot.
3.  **Last Resort (Risky):** Some travelers report going to the ticket booth early (6 AM) with their passport and pleading ignorance. *Sometimes* they issue a manual permit if there are no-shows, but **do not rely on this**. You could travel all the way there and be turned away. Stick to the email method or get a Korean friend to help.

## FAQ

### Q1: Can I book a reservation on the same day I want to hike?
Honestly, it's very unlikely. The system technically allows it if there are slots, but these trails are usually fully booked weeks in advance. Your only hope for a same-day hike is if the weather is bad and mass cancellations happen, but checking the site continuously is your only option.

### Q2: Is the reservation really free?
Yes! The reservation itself costs 0 won. It is purely a crowd control measure, not a revenue generator. However, if you drive, parking at the trailhead costs a small fee (around 1,800 KRW for a standard car), and if you want the paper certificate printed at the kiosk, that costs 1,000 KRW (cash only).

### Q3: What happens if I book but don't show up?
You will be blacklisted. If you pull a "no-show" without canceling, you are banned from making another reservation for 3 months. If you do it twice, it's a 1-year ban. Since the reservation is free, please be a good traveler and cancel if your plans change so someone else can grab the slot!

### Q4: Which trail is better for a beginner?
Seongpanak is "easier" because the slope is gradual, but it is *long* (9.6km one way). It feels like a never-ending walk in the woods. Gwaneumsa is shorter (8.7km) but much steeper. If you have good cardio but bad knees, go up Gwaneumsa and down Seongpanak. If you are generally unfit, stick to Seongpanak (or skip the summit and do the Yeongsil trail instead).

### Q5: How do I get the Summit Certificate?
Previously, you had to pay 1,000 won at a kiosk. Now, you can download the "Jeju IoT" app. When you are at the summit, turn on your GPS and the app will unlock your digital certificate for free. You can still pay for the paper one at the bottom if you want a physical souvenir, but you need to show the photo proof on your phone.

## ARTICLE BODY

### Section 1: Answer the Main Question First!
So, you're planning a trip to Jeju Island in 2026 and the big question on your mind is: "Do I actually need a reservation to hike Hallasan, or is that just internet rumors?"

Here is the deal: **Yes, you absolutely, 100% need a reservation.**

If you plan to hike to the summit—meaning the famous Baengnokdam crater lake at the very top—you cannot just show up. The reservation system is mandatory for the two summit trails: **Seongpanak** and **Gwaneumsa**. Every single day, rangers stand at the trailheads scanning QR codes. If you don't have one, you don't hike. It's that simple.

The quota is strict: only **1,000 people** are allowed on the Seongpanak trail and **500 people** on the Gwaneumsa trail per day. That’s a total of 1,500 lucky hikers daily. If you show up without a QR code, you will be turned away, likely after you’ve already woken up at 5 AM and taken a long taxi ride. Don't be that tourist. The good news? It’s free. The bad news? Slots fill up fast. But don't worry, I'm going to walk you through exactly how to secure your spot, even if you don't speak a word of Korean.

### Section 2: The Background Story
You might be wondering, "Why is it so strict? It's a mountain, not a nightclub." Well, let me tell you, the history here is actually pretty interesting.

Back in the day, Hallasan was a free-for-all. Thousands of hikers would trample up the slopes every day, causing massive soil erosion and damage to the unique volcanic ecosystem. It got so crowded that it felt less like a nature retreat and more like a rush-hour subway in Seoul. To save the mountain, the Jeju government stepped in around 2020 and said, "Enough." They implemented this reservation system to cap the number of footsteps hitting the trails.

Fast forward to late 2024, and things got a little confusing. The government actually ran a "pilot period" where they briefly lifted the reservation requirement for a month to see what would happen. Spoiler alert: it was chaos, and the reservation system was promptly reinforced.

Then there is the "Ramen Incident." For years, eating instant cup noodles at the Witseoreum or Jindallaebat shelters was a rite of passage. But people were dumping gallons of salty, spicy ramen broth onto the ground, which was literally killing the protected plants (salt + alpine plants = death). So, in a controversial but necessary move, they banned the sale of food at shelters. Now, in 2026, the mountain is cleaner, quieter, and recovering—but it means you need to be more organized than ever before.

### Section 3: Breaking Down Your Options
Okay, so you know you need a reservation. Now you have a big choice to make: **Seongpanak** or **Gwaneumsa**?

Let's break it down like we're choosing a character in a video game.

**Option A: Seongpanak (The Endurance Test)**
This is the most popular choice, mostly because it's considered "easier." But let me be clear: "easier" just means "less steep." It is *long*—about 9.6km one way. The path is well-maintained, mostly wooden decks and rocky paths, but for the first 3 hours, you are basically walking in a green tunnel of trees with no views. It requires patience. If you aren't super fit, this is your safest bet.

**Option B: Gwaneumsa (The Thigh Burner)**
This is the scenic route. It’s shorter (8.7km) but significantly steeper. We're talking staircases that seem to go up into the clouds. However, the payoff is immediate. You get jagged cliffs, a suspension bridge over a valley, and views that will make your Instagram followers jealous. It’s harder on the cardio going up and brutal on the knees coming down.

**Option C: The "Pro" Combo**
Here is what I tell my friends to do: **Book your entrance for Gwaneumsa.** Suffer through the steep climb while your legs are fresh, enjoy the insane views, reach the summit, and then **descend via Seongpanak**. The gentle slope of Seongpanak is much kinder on tired knees. Plus, you get to see both sides of the mountain. Just remember, you only need a reservation for the *starting* point. You can descend whichever way you want!

### Section 4: The Complete How-To
Ready to book? Let's do this. Grab your passport and your laptop.

**Step 1: The Timing**
Reservations open on the **1st of the month** for the *following* month. For example, if you want to hike on October 15th, you need to be on the website on September 1st. Slots for weekends and holidays can vanish in minutes.

**Step 2: The Website**
Go to `visithalla.jeju.go.kr`. Switch the language to English (there’s a button top right). Click "Reservation."

**Step 3: The "Foreigner" Trap**
This is where people get stuck. The standard login asks for a Korean phone number or a "Kakao" verification. **Ignore that.** Look for a tab or button that says **"Foreigner"** or **"Email Verification."**

You will enter your email address. The system will send you a code. **Warning:** If you use a Hotmail, Outlook, or iCloud address, the code often disappears into the void. **Use a Gmail address.** It works 99% of the time. Once you get the code, type it in.

**Step 4: The Details**
Select your trail (Seongpanak or Gwaneumsa), the date, and the number of people. You will need to enter the names of everyone in your group exactly as they appear on their passports.

**Step 5: The Golden Ticket**
Once you confirm, you will get a reservation number and a link to a QR code. **Screenshot this immediately.** Do not assume you can pull up the email at the trailhead—cell service can be spotty, and the website goes down for maintenance. That screenshot is your ticket to the mountain.

### Section 5: The Stuff Nobody Tells You
Here is the insider info that guidebooks usually miss.

**The "No-ARC" Panic:**
If you are a tourist without an Alien Registration Card (ARC) or a Korean number, and the email verification is just *not* working (it happens, the site is buggy), don't panic. Ask the front desk staff at your hotel to help. They can often book it for you using their local number, or they can call the park office to troubleshoot. As a last resort, some travelers have successfully gone to the trailhead booth at 6:00 AM with their passport and pleaded their case—but this is risky. Try to sort it out digitally first.

**The Ramen Situation:**
I mentioned this earlier, but it bears repeating: **There is no food for sale on the mountain.** None. Zero. If you want to eat lunch, you must pack it. Most hikers bring "Gimbap" (rice rolls) bought from a convenience store in the city, plus a thermos of hot water and a cup noodle. There is something magical about eating hot noodles in the cold mountain air, but you have to BYO everything. And please, bring a ziplock bag to carry your trash (and leftover broth) back down.

**The Taxi Nightmare:**
Getting *to* the mountain is easy—taxis are everywhere in the morning. Getting *home* is the hard part. At the Gwaneumsa trailhead, there are rarely taxis waiting. You might finish your 8-hour hike and find yourself stranded. **Download the Kakao T app** before you go. It allows you to call a taxi to your location. If you don't have the app, you might have to walk a long way to the bus stop or hitch a ride.

### Conclusion: Your Action Plan
Hiking Hallasan is one of the most rewarding experiences in Korea, but it punishes the unprepared.

**Here is your plan:**
1.  Set a calendar reminder for the **1st of the month** prior to your trip.
2.  Book the **Gwaneumsa trail** for an early slot (around 7:00 AM).
3.  Pack a bag with **water (at least 2L), Gimbap, a thermos of hot water, cup noodles, and a trash bag**.
4.  Screenshot your **QR code** and keep your passport handy.

Do this, and you’ll be standing on the roof of South Korea, looking down at the clouds, wondering why you ever stressed about it. Trust me, the view is worth every single step (and every minute of website frustration). Happy hiking!
