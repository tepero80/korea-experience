**혼밥** is eating alone. For a quick meal, start with a place that lists individually priced dishes such as **국밥** (rice soup) or **국수** (noodles). Ask about a seat when you arrive. If the restaurant only offers a large shared dish or is full, the next individually priced menu may fit your day better.

## Pick a fallback before joining the line

| Case | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| One bowl on the sign | Ask whether one person can sit now. Check whether orders go through staff or a kiosk. |
| A line outside | Ask if the counter uses the same line. Follow the staff's instructions. |
| Short break between trains or visits | Keep a nearby takeaway option saved. Look for 포장 on the menu. |
| Shared grill | Confirm the minimum number of portions before committing. |

## Two shops to look up on the way

[Visit Seoul's Seongsu Gamjatang listing](https://english.visitseoul.net/restaurants/SeongsuGamjatang/ENPlceoxk) identifies designated solo seats at this location and places it near Seongsu Station Exit 4. Save **소문난성수감자탕 · 서울 성동구 연무장길 45**. Ask staff where to wait that day.

For Busan, [VISITKOREA’s pork and rice soup guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=221285) names Songjeong Samdae Gukbap as a place suitable for one person and places it near Seomyeon Station. Save **송정3대국밥 · 부산 부산진구 서면로68번길 33**. These examples can anchor an existing neighborhood plan, and you can look for the same dish near your hotel if either location adds a long detour.

At the door, show **한 명인데 지금 먹을 수 있어요?** to ask if they can seat one person to eat now. If you want a barbecue meal, check the [solo barbecue ordering guide](/blog/k-bbq-for-solo-1-person-restaurants-2026) for the extra questions to ask about portions and set menus.
