Ordering Korean fried chicken involves specific terminology, preparation styles, and delivery procedures that differ from Western fast-food traditions. Whether you are dining in at a local *chimaek* spot or ordering to a hotel room or a designated zone at a Han River park, understanding how portions, flavor variations, accompaniments, and delivery platforms work ensures a smooth experience.

---

## Portions and Terminology

Korean whole chicken is traditionally ordered and counted using the native Korean counter ***ma-ri* (마리)**. 

* ***Han ma-ri* (한 마리):** This means one whole chicken.
* **Half portions (*ban-mari*):** Many major chains allow you to order a half chicken if a whole bird is too large for your group.
* **Combo parts:** Numerous franchises also provide options for specific cuts, such as wings or drumsticks only.
* **Boneless (*sunssal*, 순살):** Many brands give you the choice between traditional bone-in pieces and boneless chicken meat. 

*Note: Exact piece counts vary by individual franchise and whether you choose bone-in or boneless preparations.*

---

## Sauces and Preparation Styles

The flavor profiles of Korean fried chicken go far beyond standard crispy coating. Understanding the core taxonomy helps when navigating a menu:

* ***Huraideu* (후라이드):** Plain, double-fried chicken with a remarkably crisp skin and no sauce.
* ***Yangnyeom* (양념):** The classic sweet and spicy gochujang-based sauce coating.
* **Soy-garlic / Soy sauce (간장):** A savory, soy-based glaze popularized by prominent chains like Kyochon.
* **Powder and alternative flavors:** These include options like cheese powder (such as Bburinkle), green-onion *padak*, or oven-roasted styles offered by brands like Goobne.
* ***Ban-ban* (반반):** A popular "half-and-half" option that allows you to select two different flavors in a single box.

*Note: Exact heat levels for spicy variants, such as "Red" or "Mala" sauces, vary significantly across different brands.*

---

## Sides and Accompaniments

A proper Korean fried chicken order comes with traditional accompaniments designed to balance the rich, fried flavors:

* ***Chikin-mu* (치킨무):** Standardly included with every order, this consists of cubed pickled white radish that cleanses the palate between bites.
* **Dipping sauces:** Small complimentary containers of salt, mustard, or sweet chili sauce are typically provided depending on the brand.
* **Beverage pairings:** Culturally, fried chicken is paired with beer—a combination known as ***chimaek* (치맥)**, merging chicken (*chi*) and beer (*maekju*).

---

## Delivery Terms, Apps, and Logistics

Getting Korean fried chicken delivered relies on localized mobile infrastructure. 

* **Major platforms:** The primary food delivery applications in South Korea include Baedal Minjok (Baemin, 배달의민족), Yogiyo (요기요), and Coupang Eats.
* **Foreign user access:** Recent updates on select apps—such as Baemin’s English feature support for visitors—facilitate easier navigation, alongside expanded compatibility for international credit cards on certain platforms.
* **Delivery locations:** Orders can be sent to residential addresses, hotels, and even designated delivery zones in public spaces like Han River parks.

*Note: App interface capabilities, accepted payment methods (such as overseas credit cards versus local payment gateways like KakaoPay or NaverPay), and exact delivery drop-off rules can change per app version or venue location.*
