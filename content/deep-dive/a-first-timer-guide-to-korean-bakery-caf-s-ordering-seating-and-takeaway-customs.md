Korean bakery cafés are a cornerstone of the country's vibrant food and social scene, offering everything from artisanal pastries to meticulously brewed beverages in thoughtfully designed spaces. For first-time visitors, navigating the local customs can feel slightly unfamiliar. Understanding how seating, ordering, and takeaway rules work will ensure a smooth and enjoyable experience.

## Securing Seating: The "Seat First" Culture

In many busy or chain-affiliated Korean cafés, it is common and culturally accepted practice to secure or "claim" a table before getting in line to order. Patrons frequently place personal items—such as a jacket, shopping bag, phone, or laptop—on a table to mark their territory while they approach the counter or kiosk. 

While this practice relies on low theft rates and the widespread presence of CCTV, travelers should exercise common sense. It is still wise to keep ultra-valuable items, such as passports and large amounts of cash, securely on your person. Furthermore, note that independent, highly structured "themed" cafés or small boutique spaces on weekends may require you to order first or manage a digital waitlist before seating.

## The "1 Drink Per Person" Policy (*1인 1음료*)

Most Korean cafés enforce a strict "1 drink per person" rule (*1인 1음료*). This policy means that every individual occupying a seat is expected to purchase at least one item individually. 

In a bakery café setting, purchasing a high-value bakery or dessert item instead of a beverage is sometimes accepted, but smaller or tourist-heavy boutique cafés frequently mandate an individual *beverage* purchase per seated customer. Sharing a single dessert or drink among multiple people while taking up multiple seats is heavily frowned upon or outright rejected by staff.

## For Here vs. Takeaway Customs (*Maejang* vs. *Pojang*)

When ordering at either a physical counter or a digital touch-screen kiosk, customers must explicitly specify whether they are dining in (**매장**, *maejang*) or taking away (**포장**, *pojang*). 

| Choice | Term (Hangul) | Romanization | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dine In** | 매장 | *Maejang* | For consuming food and drinks inside the venue. |
| **Takeaway** | 포장 | *Pojang* | For packaging items to-go. |

*Note: Environmental ministry regulations regarding single-use plastics and disposable cups fluctuate by store size and policy updates. Because policies can vary regarding whether dine-in customers receive ceramic mugs or paper cups based on venue capacity, clearly communicating maejang versus pojang at checkout is essential.*

## Ordering Hardware and Additional Etiquette

Modern bakery cafés widely utilize digital touch-screen kiosks—many of which feature English language options—alongside physical ordering counters. When visiting, keep the following core guidelines in mind:

* **Outside Food:** Bringing external food or snacks into a Korean bakery café is universally restricted and considered rude.
* **Tipping:** Tipping is not practiced or expected in Korean bakery cafés.
* **Tray Return:** Leaving empty trays, plates, and cups behind on tables is considered poor manners. Customers are expected to clear their own spaces and drop off items at the designated "Return" (*퇴식구*) station before leaving.
