## Start with the spelling the person uses

A name such as **김민준** can be read as family name **김** followed by given name **민준**. Treat it as an example and ask the person how they write and pronounce their name. The [National Institute of Korean Language’s romanization guide](https://www.korean.go.kr/front_eng/roman/roman_01.do) puts the family name first and permits a hyphen between given-name syllables. For a booking, invitation or badge, copy the individual’s requested spelling. For a form that asks for an official name, use the spelling on the relevant document.

| What you have | What you can check |
| --- | --- |
| Roman letters | Ask for the Korean spelling when you need to recognize or pronounce the name. The letters alone may leave more than one possibility. |
| Hangul | Confirm how to say it. If you want to know what the name means, ask whether the person uses Hanja or whether it has a meaning in Korean. |
| Exact Hanja | Look up the particular characters and ask how the person understands them. A dictionary explains characters. The personal story comes from the person. |
| A suggestion | Use it for a character or a language exercise. Check the characters independently if you need the meaning to be accurate. |

## Find out whether there are Hanja characters

[Article 44 of Korea’s family registration law](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1028580603) allows Hangul or commonly used Hanja for the name in a birth report and leaves the permitted Hanja range to Supreme Court rules. The [Supreme Court’s personal-name Hanja lookup](https://efamily.scourt.go.kr/cs/CsBltnWrtList.do?bltnbordId=0000010) lets you check a character and its permitted reading. It cannot tell you which characters a particular person uses. A Hangul-only name may have a meaning in Korean. If you cannot confirm a meaning, use the name as the person gives it.
