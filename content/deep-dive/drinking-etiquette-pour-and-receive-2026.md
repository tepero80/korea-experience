At a Korean meal, you may see people offer to pour for one another or use two hands when sharing a drink with an older person. These are gestures of courtesy. Everyone can decide what to drink. If you prefer not to have alcohol, you can say so and ask for another drink.

## Offering and receiving

In a more formal setting, use both hands to hold the bottle when offering a pour, or hold your glass with both hands when someone offers to pour for you. [Korea.net's guide to table manners](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Society/view?articleId=175173&pageIndex=1) describes these gestures in relation to older people. You can simply ask before pouring: “Would you like some?”

Practices vary between people and occasions. Watch your companions and follow their preferences rather than assuming that someone's age or position means they want a refill. There is no need to keep another person's glass full.

## If you do not want alcohol

You can decline without giving a medical or personal explanation. For example:

| Intent | Phrase |
| --- | --- |
| No, thank you. | **괜찮아요, 감사합니다.** (*Gwaenchanayo, gamsahamnida.*) |
| I don’t drink alcohol. | **저는 술 안 마셔요.** (*Jeoneun sul an masyeoyo.*) |
| Water, please. | **물 주세요.** (*Mul juseyo.*) |

You can join a toast with a non-alcoholic drink if you wish. If someone offers again, a calm repetition of your choice is enough. You do not need to accept a glass, taste it or have someone else drink it for you.

If the group pauses for a toast, [use a short toast that fits the occasion](/blog/korean-drinking-etiquette-pouring-and-toasting).

## Tradition and the present table

[Korea.net's account of traditional drinking manners](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=117340) describes receiving a drink from an elder with both hands and turning to the side. It also records historical expectations that a person taste an offered drink. Treat those as descriptions of traditions, not instructions you must follow today. A separate [historical account of a ceremonial gathering](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=117176) describes both offering and declining; it concerns a particular traditional ceremony, not a script for a modern dinner.

Whether you are with friends, hosts or colleagues, let your own preference guide what you accept. You can participate in the meal and conversation without drinking alcohol.
