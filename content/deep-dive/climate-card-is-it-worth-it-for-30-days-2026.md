## Which option applies to a long visit?

| Option | When to consider it |
| --- | --- |
| Stored value | Useful when you will walk, take days off, make regional side trips, or ride too little to justify a pass. Check each line and operator. |
| Short pass | Seoul’s [regular short-term Climate Card](https://english.seoul.go.kr/policy/transportation/climate-card/) lists 1-, 2-, 3-, 5- and 7-day periods. Use it for clustered days with many covered rides. Recharging starts the pass that day. |
| Resident pass | The [separate Climate Pass programme](https://english.seoul.go.kr/climate-pass-set-for-official-launch-on-september-1-with-on-site-events-offering-free-card-exchanges/) has residency and registration conditions. Review eligibility before budgeting based on it. |

## Build the month from your actual trips

1. Mark your arrival, departure, regional trips and days with little local transit. Keep those costs separate from your dense Seoul days.
2. Open the [fare comparison](/blog/seoul-transit-climate-card-vs-tmoney-2026) for the days you expect to ride close together. Count only covered rides and check your exact last stop. Allow for the physical card cost if you need to buy one.
3. Avoid loading a regular short-term pass a day early. Seoul lists the recharge date as the start. Use a separate [taxi or bike payment](/blog/t-money-for-taxis-and-bikes-2026) for those rides.

The older prepaid **30-day Climate Card recharge deadline was August 31, 2026**. Even if a cached article or old kiosk guide still shows a monthly fare, use the current city coverage and pass table for your dates.
