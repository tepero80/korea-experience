A 목욕탕 (mokyoktang) is a bathhouse; a 찜질방 (jjimjilbang) adds heated relaxation rooms. Facilities differ, so check what your ticket includes and whether the setting suits your comfort and access needs.

## Ask before entering

| Area | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Bath | Bathing areas are commonly separated by gender and used without swimwear; ask staff about the venue's rules. Wash thoroughly before entering a shared bath. |
| Sauna | Shared relaxation rooms commonly use issued clothing. Confirm whether rental is included. |
| Pool | A pool may have different swimwear and ticket rules. |
| Access | Ask about lifts, steps, slippery floors, grab rails, accessible toilets and assistance. |

The [tourism board's 2024 guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/sp/HallyuNew/contentsView.do?vcontsId=217505) describes separate bathing areas and a shared relaxation floor at Puksuwon Spaplex. It also describes keeping a locker key for extras. Ask about the venue's policy on assistance and gendered spaces before arriving; do not assume a private or accessible bath exists.

Keep phones and cameras out of changing and bathing areas, and follow posted instructions. Start with a cooler room and leave if uncomfortable. [CIMER's visitor guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=193081) illustrates how pool and sauna zones can use different tickets; verify current terms directly.
