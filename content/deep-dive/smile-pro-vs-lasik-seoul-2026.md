## Find out why the recommendations differ

If one Seoul clinic proposes SMILE Pro and another proposes LASIK, start by asking each doctor which findings in your exam led to that recommendation. The [U.S. FDA patient guide for LASIK](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/lasik/what-should-i-expect-during-and-after-surgery) describes a discussion of examination findings, risks and alternatives before a decision. Ask for that discussion about whichever procedure the clinician proposes.

## Copy the same details from each written plan

Use one copy of this table per clinic. If the quote gives a package name only, ask for a separate note from the doctor describing the plan.

| Compare | Write down for each clinic |
| --- | --- |
| Exam | Which exam or history finding led to this recommendation? Which findings still need clarification? |
| Options | What alternatives were discussed, including continuing with glasses or contacts? Why was this option proposed? |
| Charges | Does the quote include the examination, both eyes if proposed, planned follow-up and records? What would be charged separately? |
| Travel | What does the clinician expect to check in person? Who would see you if your plans change or a concern arises? |

## Ask a doctor to reconcile what matters

If one doctor raised a concern missing from the other note, show an eye doctor the two exam summaries and ask them to explain the difference. Keep a copy of the recommendations and give yourself time to ask follow-up questions. Before committing to flights or appointments, confirm who will provide follow-up after you leave Korea. The [UK government’s guidance on treatment abroad](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/in-hospital-abroad) is a planning aid for those arrangements.

Once you have a proposal you want to discuss further, [check the named device, consent form and eye records](/blog/smile-pro-eye-surgery-the-2026-tech-upgrade-from-lasik-2026) before committing.
