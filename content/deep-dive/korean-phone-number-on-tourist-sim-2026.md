Some Korean tourist SIMs provide a **010 number and incoming texts**, but a number alone does not guarantee that an app will accept it or that the line supports Korean mobile identity verification. Check the exact product and the exact service you need before paying.

## Check the four separate requirements

| What you need | What to ask before purchase |
| --- | --- |
| A Korean number | Does this particular SIM or eSIM provide a 010 number, and when can I see it? |
| An incoming code | Can this product receive Korean domestic SMS? Is an airport entry check or another activation step required first? |
| A reply or call | Can I send a domestic text or make a call? Is extra credit needed? |
| An identity check | Does the service require a carrier-based identity check, such as PASS, rather than a text code? Does the provider support that check for this exact line? |

Ask the app or service whether it accepts the number and method you plan to use. A text message arriving on your phone and a successful carrier identity check are different results.

## What two visitor products say

[KT's visitor eSIM page](https://m.roaming.kt.com/eng/esim) says its products provide 010 numbers. Its data eSIM lists incoming calls and texts after an entry check at an airport KT roaming center, but no outgoing calls or texts. Its separate voice option allows outgoing calls and texts at an additional charge after the check. KT also says its visitor eSIM does not support personal authentication. The entry check is therefore not proof that an app's identity check will work.

[LG U+'s visitor eSIM page](https://www.lguplus.com/korea-sim/eng/pc/product/esim?tab=data) says SMS verification is unavailable with its data-only service. It says its voice and SMS service can receive verification texts for restaurant and taxi reservation apps, while identity verification for banking or government services is not supported. The page describes its own products; it does not guarantee that every individual app will accept a particular number.

These are product examples, not rules for every SIM. In particular, do not assume that the words “data only” settle whether a number or incoming SMS is provided. Confirm the exact option with its seller.

## A practical check before you pay

1. **Open the service you intend to use.** Note whether it asks for a text code, a carrier identity check or both. Ask the service about accepted methods if the screen is unclear.
2. **Show the seller the requirement.** Ask whether the exact plan has a 010 number and receives domestic SMS; ask separately about outgoing texts, calls, costs and any activation or entry check. If a carrier identity check is required, ask specifically whether this line supports it.
3. **Check your phone.** Confirm that the device is unlocked and, for an eSIM, compatible with the provider's product. Ask when the line becomes usable and where help is available.
4. **Test the task while help is available.** Once activated, try receiving the intended service's code and, if needed, sending a text or placing a call. A successful test confirms that particular task at that time; it does not establish eligibility for other services.

Keep an existing working contact method available until the new line is working. Check your home carrier's roaming charges before using it abroad.

## If the number or verification does not work

Ask the venue or service whether it offers another booking or contact method, such as a website, email, phone call or in-person assistance. If the service specifically requires a carrier identity check, ask the service and a carrier which eligible line and documents are needed for your circumstances. Do not assume that adding voice credit changes the line's identity-verification capability.
