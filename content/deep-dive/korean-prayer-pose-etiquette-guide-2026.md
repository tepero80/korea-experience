## Pause at the entrance

Start with the signs at the temple you visit. The [Korea Tourism Organization’s Templestay etiquette guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=219&vcontsId=227777) describes using side doors to a main prayer hall where access is permitted and avoiding crossing in front of worshippers. Check which door is in public use before entering. Follow the local instructions if they differ.

| Check | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Hall | Check the posted entrance or ask staff. Follow signs about footwear and stay out if the space is reserved for a ceremony. |
| Bow | Keep a respectful distance and follow the host’s lead. [Bongeunsa’s program guidance](https://www.templestay.com/en/MI000000000000000062/reserve/view.do?areaCd=&areaSelect=&pageIndex=1&searchCnt=&searchEndDate=&searchKeyword=bongeunsa&searchStaDate=&templeBookMarkId=Bongeunsa&templeId=&templeIdTmp=&templePrgType=&templestaySeq=27567) is one example where palms together with a small bow is appropriate. |
| Photo | Ask about photography, especially indoors or while people are worshipping. You can continue through public outdoor areas if photos inside are restricted. |
| Visit | Use the [Beomeosa listing](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?vcontsId=111223) as an example of a separate venue to plan in Busan. Check the public route and entrance signs on the day. |

## Ask one short question if you are unsure

“여기 들어가도 되나요?” asks whether you may enter. “사진 찍어도 되나요?” asks whether you may take photos. If nobody can confirm, wait outside the hall. A quiet walk through open public areas is still a visit.
