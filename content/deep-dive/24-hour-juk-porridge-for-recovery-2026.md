Juk (죽) is Korean porridge. Start the search around the place where you will actually eat it, such as your hotel or the station where you arrive. A restaurant listing, a delivery estimate and a convenience store’s opening hours answer different questions.

## Use this order for tonight

| Option | Check before you leave |
| --- | --- |
| Shop | Ask whether this branch still accepts orders and has the dish you want. |
| Delivery | Check that checkout works and your lodging accepts delivery at that hour. |
| Pack | Check the store is open, then read the ingredients and preparation directions. |

Use the [Bonjuk branch finder](https://www.bonif.co.kr/brand/store?brdCd=BF101) to identify a shop and the [Bonjuk menu](https://www.bonif.co.kr/brand/menu?brdCd=BF101) to recognize dish names. Call before making a detour near closing time. Ask **아직 주문할 수 있나요?** (Can I still order?) and **포장 되나요?** (Can I get it to go?). Opening hours and last orders can differ.

## Know what to look for at the store

Look for **죽** on a cup, tray or pouch. **전복죽** is abalone porridge and **호박죽** means pumpkin porridge. A prominent vegetable name does not tell you what is in the broth. Read the full ingredient and allergen list or choose a product you can confirm. Check the specific store’s hours before leaving. The [Korea Tourism Organization’s N Seoul Tower GS25 listing](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/rgnContentsView.do?vcontsId=1573631) is an example of a convenience store with limited opening hours.

Check whether the package is ready to eat or needs heating, and whether it belongs on a shelf or in a refrigerator. Confirm where you can heat it and follow that specific package’s directions. If arriving after the local shops close, compare another meal in the [late-night Seoul dining guide](/blog/late-night-dining-24-hour-restaurants-in-seoul) or buy ahead only if you can follow the storage instructions.
