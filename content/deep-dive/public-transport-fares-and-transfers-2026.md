## Tap at each leg of the trip

| Where you are | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Boarding a city bus | Tap your card at the reader beside the entrance. Check that it accepted the tap. |
| Getting off a city bus | Tap again at the onboard reader by the exit, before you step onto the street. |
| Entering or leaving the subway | Tap at the gate with the same card. For a rail interchange, follow the line signs and use any gate the interchange requires. |
| Starting the next eligible leg | Tap the same card again. Keep one card per traveler when your route includes the subway. |

Use the [city’s illustrated bus guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/movement/public-transportation/) to see how boarding and exiting work. Take the transit card out of a wallet with several contactless cards so you know which one the reader received. If a tap fails, check the display and ask the driver or station staff before continuing. If you need a moment to cross the road or find a platform, put safety ahead of the transfer timer.

## Know when a transfer fare applies

Seoul’s [integrated fare rules](https://english.seoul.go.kr/policy/transportation/modes-of-transport/) generally link eligible legs when the next ride starts within **30 minutes** of tapping out of the previous leg. The city publishes a **60-minute window from 9 pm to 7 am** and allows up to **four transfers** in an eligible chain. Reboarding the same bus route does not receive the normal transfer treatment. A missed exit tap can prevent the next leg from linking. Check the full route with your map app, especially if a segment leaves Seoul or uses a separately priced service.

For example, on a subway-to-bus trip, tap in at the subway, tap out at your station, then use the same card to board and exit the eligible bus. A linked trip can still cost more than a base fare because the system considers distance and the higher applicable base fare. Keep enough balance for another boarding in case the legs do not link.

## Compare separate journeys with a pass

The [city’s subway fare table](https://english.seoul.go.kr/policy/transportation/modes-of-transport/subway/) lists **₩1,550** as the basic adult card fare, with distance charges where applicable. The [short-term Climate Card page](https://english.seoul.go.kr/policy/transportation/climate-card/) lists **₩5,000 for one day** and **₩10,000 for three days**, before any separate cost to buy a physical card.

Four independent subway journeys charged at the base fare would cost **₩6,200**. Four boardings in a linked trip might cost less. Before buying, mark your actual start and end stations, which legs link, and which are covered by the pass. The [transit pass calculator](/tools/transit-pass) lets you compare those assumptions. Use [the city’s current coverage list](https://english.seoul.go.kr/policy/transportation/climate-card/) for airport rail, outer Seoul stations and operator exceptions.

## If something goes wrong at a reader

- **No confirmation at the bus entrance:** check the balance or ask the driver. Use your own card for each traveler when combining buses and trains.
- **Forgot to tap out:** budget for your next ride as a separate journey. Ask staff if a gate or reader displays an error.
- **Route leaves the pass area:** carry a charged pay-as-you-go card and check how that particular station or operator handles the fare.
