Withdrawing cash with a foreign card in South Korea requires careful navigation of machine types, fee structures, and interface prompts. Many standard Korean ATMs are domestic-only and will reject foreign-issued cards immediately. To ensure a smooth transaction, you need to know which terminals accept international cards, how to manage the associated costs, and what fallback options exist if a machine fails.

## ATM Labels & Machine Compatibility

The most common hurdle when attempting to withdraw cash is running into a machine that only supports domestic networks (such as KFTC or BC Card). If you insert a foreign card into a domestic-only terminal, it will typically display an error message or immediately eject the card.

To use a foreign card, look for terminals explicitly labeled:
* **"Global ATM"** or **"Global Service"**
* Terminals bearing international network logos such as **Visa, Mastercard, Plus, Cirrus, UnionPay, or JCB**

Keep in mind that specific machine availability and software interfaces can vary unpredictably, even within the same bank brand or convenience store chain.

## Locations & Operating Hours

Finding a compatible machine depends largely on where you look:
* **Bank Branches:** Major commercial banks (including Woori, KB Kookmin, Shinhan, and Hana) feature dedicated 365-day automated corners that house Global ATMs. However, standalone bank kiosks outside of these corners may close late in the evening or follow standard branch operating hours.
* **Convenience Stores:** Stores such as CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, and emart24 operate 24 hours (subject to brief overnight system maintenance) and frequently include Global ATMs. Note that individual machines within certain convenience store locations may still reject specific foreign cards.

## Understanding the Three-Layer Fee Structure

ATM cash withdrawals in Korea can incur charges across three distinct layers:
1. **Local Korean Operator Fee:** Levied by the local bank or operator, this fee typically ranges from KRW 3,500 to KRW 5,000 per transaction (for instance, Woori generally charges around KRW 3,500, while Hana ranges from KRW 4,200 to KRW 5,000).
2. **International Network Fee:** Cross-border processing fees charged by networks like Visa or Mastercard, which are usually bundled directly into your exchange rate.
3. **Home Bank Fees:** Foreign transaction fees (frequently 1% to 3%) and flat out-of-network or international ATM fees ($3 to $5 USD) charged by your home institution, unless you use a fee-reimbursement account (such as Charles Schwab) or a specialized travel card (such as Wise or YouTrip).

## Avoiding the Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) Trap

When navigating the ATM interface, you may be prompted to choose the currency for your transaction. 

* **The Trap:** Selecting your home country’s currency triggers Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC), which imposes steep markup rates that often add an extra 5% to 10% to your total cost.
* **The Solution:** Always explicitly **decline DCC** or choose to be billed in **Korean Won (KRW)** to let your home bank handle the conversion at a standard rate.

## Limits, PINs, and Transaction Nuances

* **Withdrawal Limits:** Per-transaction limits generally cap between KRW 300,000 and KRW 1,000,000. These caps depend heavily on the ATM operator and any limits set by your home bank.
* **PIN Entry:** Korean ATMs primarily prompt for a **4-digit PIN**. If your home PIN is longer, travelers report mixed workarounds—such as entering the first 4 digits or appending "00"—though verifying a 4-digit standard with your home bank before travel is strongly advised.
* **Account Type Selection:** When prompted to select an account type, using a debit card typically requires choosing **"Savings"** or **"Checking."** If you use a credit card, the machine will process the transaction as a cash advance, which immediately incurs high interest rates and fees.

## Fallback Options & Alternatives

Because foreign cards can occasionally face rejections or network disruptions at local ATMs, it is wise to maintain alternative payment methods:
* **Digital Travel Wallets:** Prepaid, reloadable cards (such as WOWPASS) and mobile-supported transit options help minimize reliance on physical cash.
* **Card Acceptance:** Korea is broadly cashless, with international credit cards accepted nearly everywhere. Cash is generally only required for traditional street vendors, small market stalls, and regional transport card top-ups.
* **Physical Currency Exchange:** Licensed physical currency exchange booths—such as those located in Myeongdong—provide a reliable fallback if you need to exchange physical foreign banknotes directly using your passport.
