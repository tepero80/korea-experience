A pharmacy listing can help you find a nearby option, but it cannot guarantee that a particular branch is open now, has the medicine you need, or can assist in English. Check the listing for your date and area, then call before you travel.

## Find an open pharmacy

Start with the Korean Pharmaceutical Association’s [Pharm114 pharmacy directory](https://www.pharm114.or.kr/). Search by pharmacy name, street, or neighborhood, or use its map and area search. The directory is in Korean; browser translation can help with navigation, but confirm important details with the pharmacy directly.

Seoul also publishes a [public late-night pharmacy map](https://map.seoul.go.kr/smgis2/short/6PAOC) and a [current list of designated public late-night pharmacies](https://news.seoul.go.kr/welfare/archives/567003?listPage=1). These services show pharmacies assigned late-night schedules. A listed schedule does not mean the pharmacy is open 24 hours, and schedules can vary by day. Call the selected branch to confirm today’s hours, address/entrance, and whether it is open before setting out.

You can ask: **지금 영업하나요? 오늘 운영시간이 어떻게 되나요?** (“Are you open now? What are your hours today?”) If you need language assistance, ask whether the pharmacy can communicate in your language; English service is not guaranteed by a directory listing.

## Choose the right contact

- **Ambulance, fire response, or urgent medical help:** Call [119](tel:119) and give your current location. Do not delay an emergency call to search for a pharmacy.
- **Police:** Call [112](tel:112).
- **Routine travel questions or tourist interpretation:** Contact the Korea Tourism Organization’s [1330 call and chat service](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=140632). Check current channel and language availability. It is not emergency dispatch or medical consultation.
- **Infectious-disease information:** Contact the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency’s [1339 call center](https://www.kdca.go.kr/eng/4276/subview.do). It is not a pharmacy directory.
- **Non-urgent medicine questions:** Speak with a pharmacist at an open pharmacy. For a concern requiring diagnosis or treatment, contact a licensed healthcare provider.

For an urgent medical emergency, call **119**. See the [official emergency-number list](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/infoHtmlView.do?menuSn=376&vcontsId=140042) for other services.

## Before you go

- Check the date, district, listed hours, and address in the directory or map.
- Call the pharmacy to confirm it is open now and can provide the service you need. Ask about language help directly.
- Have the medicine name and active ingredient, packaging or a clear photo, and relevant prescription or medical documents available to show the pharmacist. Do not choose or substitute a medicine based only on its package appearance or an online article.
- Tell the pharmacist about relevant allergies and medicines you already take, if needed for the conversation. Ask the pharmacist or clinician to explain instructions you do not understand.

This guide helps you locate professional assistance. It does not diagnose symptoms or recommend a medicine, dosage, supplement, or treatment. For a broader visitor checklist, see [Pharmacies & Medical Help in Korea](/blog/korean-pharmacies-otc-meds-tourists-2026).
