When speaking to someone you do not know, start with a polite greeting and a request rather than trying to guess their age or status. The [National Institute of Korean Language’s phrase sheet](https://www.korean.go.kr/attachFile/edu/forein03.pdf) gives these useful starting points.

If you hear both 우리 and 저희 at an introduction, [use the context to tell which group the speaker means](/blog/the-importance-of-uri-we-understanding-korean-collectivism-2026).

## Five phrases to keep handy

| Phrase | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| **안녕하세요?** (annyeonghaseyo) | Hello, as a polite greeting. |
| **감사합니다.** (gamsahamnida) | Thank you. |
| **실례합니다.** (sillyehamnida) | Excuse me, to get someone’s attention. |
| **안녕히 계세요.** (annyeonghi gyeseyo) | Goodbye to someone who is staying when you leave. |
| **안녕히 가세요.** (annyeonghi gaseyo) | Goodbye to someone who is leaving when you stay. |

For a request, [the national dictionary](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=75798) gives 여기 물수건 좀 가져다 주세요: “Please bring a wet towel here.” The word 좀 can soften a request. Pointing to the phrase on your phone is fine if pronunciation is difficult.

Korean has several speech levels. The [language institute](https://korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=216&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=309614) explains that the polite -요 form and the more formal -습니다 form differ in formality. Context and relationship matter; these phrases are a practical start, not a complete lesson. Continue with [Korean for beginners](/blog/korean-language-learning-guide-for-beginners).
