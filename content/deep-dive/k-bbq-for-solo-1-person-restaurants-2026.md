A barbecue restaurant may seat one guest but still require an opening order of more than one portion for the grill. Ask both questions before you wait in line or reserve a time. Check the total for the first order you must place.

## Read the minimum as well as the price

| Where you see it | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| **1인** or **1인 세트** | One person or a set for one. Ask whether that set is served at the branch and time you chose. |
| **2인분부터** | The item starts at two portions. Confirm whether you can mix the cuts and whether this applies to the entire order. |
| **100 g** | A price per quantity. Ask how many grams you must order and which sides cost extra. |

For one published example, [Woohwa Hongdae’s FAQ](https://www.hongdaekoreanbbq.com/en/faq) describes a set for one and different conditions for individual cuts. Verify your actual menu and do not assume other shops match its conditions. The [KTO overview of Korean meals](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=229195) provides background on grilled food and sides.

## Ask a short question at the door

Say **한 명인데 식사할 수 있나요?** (Can I eat here alone?) and **몇 인분부터 주문할 수 있어요?** (What is the minimum number of portions?). If you are unsure how the grill works, ask whether staff cook the meat or you do it at the table.

If the minimum or the waiting time does not work for you, try a different shop or order an individual grilled dish at a restaurant without tabletop cooking.
