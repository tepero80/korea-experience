## Walk the market with a meal plan

[Gwangjang Market’s official listing](https://english.visitseoul.net/dongdaemunarea/Gwangjang-Market/ENP000286) locates the food alley on Changgyeonggung-ro. [Namdaemun’s listing](https://english.visitseoul.net/attractions/Namdaemun-Market/ENP000085) names Hoehyeon Station as a nearby access point. Use these listings for the visit and make a separate decision about eating. The [NIDDK celiac disease guide](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/celiac-disease/eating-diet-nutrition) explains how gluten can be introduced during preparation or serving.

| Check | What to ask before paying |
| --- | --- |
| Mix | Does the batter contain wheat flour? Can you show the mix or ingredient label? |
| Sauce | Can you check the seasoning, soy sauce and toppings? |
| Surface | Does this pan or griddle also cook foods that contain wheat? Are utensils shared? |
| Oil | Is the same oil used for wheat-battered food? |
| Reply | If nobody can confirm the ingredients and preparation you need, use your planned meal. |

## Save a question and a meal you can verify

Show **밀가루나 밀 성분이 들어가나요? 밀가루 음식을 만드는 조리도구나 기름을 같이 쓰나요?** (“Does this contain wheat flour or wheat ingredients? Are the cooking tools or oil shared with foods made with wheat flour?”). For celiac disease, explain that you need to avoid gluten and check what you need beyond wheat as well. The conversation helps you decide whether the stall can answer your questions.

Carry a food you already know suits you, or check a separate restaurant’s ingredients and kitchen procedures in advance. For labels, additional Korean phrases and the wider plan, use the [gluten-free dining guide](/blog/gluten-free-dining-in-korea-celiac-safe-options).
