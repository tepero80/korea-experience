## Respond to the behavior you can see

If an invitation, survey or conversation starts to feel like high-pressure recruitment, decide what to do based on how the person responds to your boundaries. An invitation by itself tells you little. Watch for pressure to decide immediately, pay, share identification, or go somewhere you did not agree to.

| If you… | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| Decline | Say “No, thank you” once if you want, end the conversation and continue with your plans. |
| Feel pushed | If the person keeps insisting, move toward a staffed public place where you can ask for help. You can call someone you trust and tell them your location. |
| Face a threat | Call **112** if someone threatens you, prevents you from leaving or follows you and you feel unsafe. Call **119** for urgent ambulance, fire or rescue help. |

## Ask for help and say where you are

The [Korea Tourism Organization’s safety advice](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=217&vcontsId=248145) lists 112 and 119 and explains how to request language help. After connecting, ask for an interpreter if needed. Give the nearest shop, station exit or address. Say what is happening, whether you can leave safely and whether anyone is hurt. Our [Korea emergency numbers guide](/blog/medical-emergencies-1330-vs-119-2026) helps you choose the right service.

## Once you are somewhere safe

If you already exchanged messages or paid money, keep the records you have and note where and when the encounter happened. If payment details may have been exposed, contact your card issuer using a number from the card or its official app. For non-urgent visitor information and language assistance, use the [1330 Korea Travel Helpline](https://contest.visitkorea.or.kr/eng/overview/facilities/infocenter.kto).
