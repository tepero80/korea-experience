# Photography for travel guides

Read this check before choosing or replacing a guide photograph, a destination cover, or the homepage feature. The approved composition and visual palette are in [Travel editorial design](../intent/TRAVEL_EDITORIAL_DESIGN.md).

## Choose a photograph that answers part of the traveler’s question

- Start with the real setting. Use a recognizable street, landscape, building, entrance, dish, boarding point or route detail for the right story. A transfer guide can benefit from a readable station sign. An outdoor itinerary can start with a wide view that shows where the visitor will go.
- For a destination hub, lead with a real scene that helps the traveler choose the place, such as a market, museum, landscape or neighborhood. Put platform signs and station interiors in the arrival guides where they help with wayfinding. Check the hub, directory tile and social preview together, and choose a different original from an existing article hero where possible.
- Prioritize a clear subject, natural color, useful visual detail and an uncrowded composition. Let individual places, seasons and times of day retain their own character. Capture dates describe what the photo shows and need no stylistic reinterpretation. Use warm page surfaces around images and keep edits to faithful crops, resizing and compression.
- Preview the actual mobile crop at 320 and 390px and the desktop crop at 768 and 1440px. Keep route-critical signs, crossings, landforms and entrances in frame. Preserve useful portrait originals in a frame that shows them clearly. Inspect the cropped sharing card separately.
- If the narrow share-card crop loses an off-center subject, set `cardImageCrop` to `east` or `west` in the guide's editorial record. Use `contain` when the full frame must remain visible for wayfinding or to preserve an original credit mark. Regenerate the card and inspect it at both full and thumbnail size.
- Each article hero has its own original. See the all-guide media guard for path, source and hash reuse. A contextual body photograph should add a different detail or viewpoint. Archive footage, stock scenes and illustrations need honest labels. Use only authenticated place photographs for visual directions and other real-world evidence.
- Record the original work, author, source, license and commercial reuse rights before publication. Record edits and any share-alike obligations alongside the reuse link. Alt text describes the visible scene. Captions identify the place and date when known, explain its relationship to the route, and distinguish historical scenes from present-day access, staff or schedules.
- Use `sourceLabel` on the photo credit when the photographer requires the exact title, filename or source name to appear. The article credit and the share card display that wording alongside the author and license.
- When protected architecture, artwork or a performance is the main subject, check the place or work owner's published reuse terms as well as the photographer's license. A nearby identifiable street, gate or station entrance can give useful orientation when its own reuse rights are documented. Describe the actual scene and how it relates to the route.
- When a replacement improves usefulness, update the article, media references, credit, alt text and captions together. Regenerate each affected share card with `npm.cmd run generate:cards -- --slug=<guide-slug>` and inspect the title, credit and crop. Keep responsive copies where they improve delivery and keep original downloads out of the public export. If a candidate’s rights or subject remain uncertain, use a verified existing photograph and record the reason.

## Image-led destination plans

- Score every proposed lead photograph for how much the scene answers the visitor’s question (0–3), how clearly people can identify its subject (0–2), and whether the focal area survives the card crop (0–2). Require a total of at least 5 and a subject score of at least 2. Verify the actual scene, license and author separately from the score.
- Record eligible card photos in `data/destination-guide-photos.json` after reviewing the source, card and article. Bind the approval to the current hero media ID. Keep illustrations and unverified images in their appropriate article context. A guide with a pending photograph is available from the hub’s guide list and can still be the right arrival link.
- Each “What comes next?” card shows the linked guide’s registered lead photograph with its source, author and license. Archived guides may use a registered photo identified by its lead image path. The build checks every card for an eligible photo. Keep useful links to guides awaiting photography in the article body. Confirm the responsive crop, credit and destination link. The destination plan score applies to featured hub cards.
- Review onward cards with the destination cover and verify that the travel reason is specific. Give a new hub a distinct lead photograph, at least three verified guides and at least three photograph-led plans, with one plausible onward decision. Add one regional link back from the guide and check the directory, sitemap and related cards after publication.
- Run `npm.cmd exec -- tsx scripts/audit-guide-heroes.ts` for the full archive inventory, visual contact sheets and `similarity-candidates.json`. Inspect the listed pairs side by side to distinguish actual reuse from different photos or illustrations with similar composition. Exact source and hash matches are checked during the build. Record visual judgments separately and reconsider any approval when the front image changes.
- Keep a dated review of the full guide inventory and the pages where each replacement was checked. Record whether a cover’s subject is confirmed, its reuse terms are documented, and it is suitable for a hub card. Seeing a thumbnail can establish what needs review. Source records and the actual original support the rights decision.
- For older batch covers, compare the current asset’s Git blob with the original output in Git history. The February 2026 generation batch in `ef54385` recorded successful guide slugs in `scripts/cover-progress-posts.json` and saved covers under `public/images/posts/`. A successful record for the same slug and an identical blob establish a generated archive cover. Record the source commit, slug and blob in the private audit. Check unmatched assets individually and prioritize generated scenes that travelers could mistake for real boarding, entry or wayfinding instructions.
- List confirmed legacy generated covers in `data/generated-archive-covers.json` with the matching Git blob ID. Use `recoveredCovers` for covers traced to a paired original in Git or local recovery. Key each entry by its exact public image path because the older filename can differ from the current guide slug. Record the source revision, raw file hash and how the pair was matched in the private audit. The article and guide cards identify these as AI-generated illustrations, and their share links use the site card while a suitable original is sourced. If a photo replaces an illustration, use a new registered image path so the original classification still describes the original bytes.
- Triage archive covers that could send visitors to the wrong building, platform, terminal or site before composition changes. If the underlying route or opening information is also in doubt, verify or revise that information before featuring the guide. A dated image of a former facility can appear as context with a caption about its date and a current alternative in the guide.
- If a misleading archive cover has no verified replacement yet, publish the corrected visitor guidance with a typographic share card. Record the specific `heroOmissionReason` in the guide's editorial record and keep the guide available in the full guide list while a suitable original is sourced. New guides require a distinct licensed photograph. Photo-led destination cards still require the checked asset and score above.
- Separately check the narrow share crop and mobile plan card for a useful landmark. For an arrival guide, keep the station or terminal sign readable where the photo is used for identification. If a destination has two photos of the same landmark, compare the actual source files and compositions before using one for the hub and the other for its guide.
- When a context photo shows a different exit or approach, identify both the photographed exit and the route travelers should use in the caption. A dish or historic object can explain the subject in an article. It cannot verify a shop's menu, hours, merchandise or the appearance of a guest room.

## Verify the map from the traveler’s starting point

- Offer Google Maps alongside the local map links for registered places and add a place-specific Google Maps search beside any standalone local map link used for directions. Check the Korean name and road address with the operator or public authority. Give separate links to similarly named stations, separate trailheads and terminals that share a subway stop. Keep the Korean address visible when the destination could be confused.
- In the rendered guide, open the labeled link and verify the exact destination passed to the map. For ticketed travel, tell readers to match the location and departure area on their current ticket. Verify schedules and accessible approaches with the operator.
- Use the search terms that return the intended place on each map. A full Korean address can help on Google Maps but send Kakao Map to a different business at the same address. Keep the verified address visible and compare the returned place, especially at markets, stations and multi-entrance sites.
- Prefer a verified place link when a search returns the wrong branch or facility. When a provider only lists the wider area, name that area on the link and give the official plan or signed entrance beside it.
- After a static build, run `npm.cmd exec -- tsx scripts/audit-article-map-links.ts` to find individual paragraphs, list items or table rows that still have a local destination link without a nearby Google option. Review flagged place-name, coordinate and short-link differences with the route’s official source. A link elsewhere in the article cannot verify the destination in a flagged block.

## Initial editorial review

The 60-guide November 23 expansion was the initial review cohort for the design rollout. Tongin Market’s prepared-food counter and Andong’s wide twilight view of Wolyeonggyo replace the former hero images on their respective guides. Their original images remain as relevant contextual figures with accurate captions. The Daejeon market portrait keeps its original sign and street context in the article frame. INSPIRE’s Airport Cargo Terminal Station image remains a labelled transport landmark.
